lesson: "How to Study Scripture"
pointNumber: 1
statement: >-
  Many believers put off reading the Bible for themselves, afraid they'll get
  it wrong, or unsure how to tell a faithful reading from someone else's
  mistaken one. Others read it as a self-help text, mining it for advice while
  disconnected from prayer and obedience. This lesson exists because Scripture
  itself tells us what kind of book it is, how it is meant to be prayed over,
  read, and obeyed — and that removes the guesswork, if we're willing to
  receive it on its own terms.
point: >-
  Scripture is God's own inspired Word, and rightly reading it follows a
  structure of its own: begin by asking the Spirit for understanding in
  prayer, since understanding is His to give; read the passage accurately
  before interpreting it; then ask what it reveals about God, about
  ourselves, and about the gospel of Jesus Christ, since Scripture has
  already shown itself to do exactly this; and finish by asking what must be
  done in response, since hearing without doing is self-deception. Reading it
  rightly is not complete until it is obeyed.

          These things I have spoken to you while I am still with you. But
          the Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name,
          he will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that
          I have said to you.
      - ref: "John 16:13-14"
        text: >-
          When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the
          truth, for he will not speak on his own authority, but whatever he
          hears he will speak, and he will declare to you the things that are
          to come. He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and
          declare it to you.
    commentary: >-
      Jesus promises His disciples that the Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the
      Father will send in His name, will teach them all things and bring to
      their remembrance all that He has said. In John 16 He adds that when
      the Spirit of truth comes He will guide believers into all truth,
      speaking only what He hears from the Father and glorifying Jesus by
      declaring what is His. This is why Bible reading must begin with
      prayer, before a word of the passage is read: understanding Scripture
      is not primarily a feat of intellect or scholarship, but a work the
      Spirit does in and for those who ask Him. That asking has real content
      — thanksgiving, particularly for the Lord Jesus's life, death, and
      resurrection, for the Spirit Himself, and for Scripture; confession of
      sin and a request for power against temptation; intercession for the
      church and for others facing hard situations; and a specific request
      for understanding of the passage at hand and for power to live it out.

  - id: B
    heading: "Reading well starts with knowing what the passage actually says"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Timothy 2:15"
        text: >-
          Do your best to present yourself to God as one approved, a worker
          who has no need to be ashamed, rightly handling the word of truth.
      - ref: "Nehemiah 8:8"
        text: >-
          They read from the book, from the Law of God, clearly, and they
          gave the sense, so that the people understood the reading.
    commentary: >-
      Once the Spirit's help has been sought, the next step follows on its
      own logical terms, before any interpretation begins: we cannot rightly
      ask what a passage means until we first know accurately what it says.
      Paul tells Timothy that rightly handling the word of truth is the mark
      of a worker approved by God, not something to be assumed or rushed.
      Nehemiah shows what this looked like in practice: the Levites read the
      Law clearly and gave the sense of it, so that the people understood the
      reading, before any application was drawn from it. Practically, this
      means retelling a passage plainly in its own words and context —
      checking unfamiliar terms, historical background, or the flow of the
      surrounding text against a resource like a study Bible or commentary —
      without yet asking what it means or requires.

    heading: "What a passage shows about God: Scripture is His own Word revealing Himself"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Timothy 3:16-17"
        text: >-
          All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching,
          for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness,
          that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work.
      - ref: "2 Peter 1:20-21"
        text: >-
          knowing this first of all, that no prophecy of Scripture comes from
          someone's own interpretation. For no prophecy was ever produced by
          the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along
          by the Holy Spirit.
    commentary: >-
      Once a passage has been read accurately, the first question to ask of
      its meaning is what it shows about God — and this lesson can ask that
      question of any passage because Scripture has already shown itself to
      do exactly this. Scripture does not merely contain God's Word or become
      His Word under certain conditions — Paul says all of it is "breathed
      out by God," which means its very origin is God Himself, making it
      inherently perfect, authoritative, and useful for teaching, correction,
      and training in righteousness. Peter grounds the same truth from the
      other direction: no prophecy of Scripture arose from a human
      interpreter's private opinion, because its human authors were not the
      ultimate source — they spoke from God as they were carried along by the
      Holy Spirit. Because Scripture is God's own Word, whatever it tells us
      about God is God revealing Himself to us, and asking this question of
      any passage is simply taking that claim seriously.

      - ref: "Hebrews 4:12"
        text: >-
          For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any
          two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of
          joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of
          the heart.
    commentary: >-
      The second question of meaning to ask of any passage is what it shows
      about us — and Scripture itself justifies asking this, since it claims
      to be exactly the kind of word that can search us. Jeremiah warns that
      the human heart is deceitful above all things and sick beyond our own
      capacity to diagnose — we cannot trust our own instincts or feelings as
      a reliable guide to truth, since only the Lord, who searches the heart,
      can render a true verdict on it. Hebrews takes this further: God's Word
      itself is living and active, able to pierce all the way to the
      division of soul and spirit and to discern the heart's true thoughts
      and intentions. Because we deceive ourselves and cannot fully know our
      own hearts, we need something outside ourselves — God's Word — to
      search, expose, and correct us as we read, rather than approaching
      Scripture only to confirm what we already believe.

          And he said to them, "O foolish ones, and slow of heart to believe

      - ref: "John 5:46-47"
        text: >-
          For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of
          me. But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my
          words?
    commentary: >-
      The third question of meaning to ask of any passage is what it shows
      about the gospel of Jesus Christ — His suffering, His glory, and the
      redemption He accomplished — and Scripture justifies asking this of
      every passage, not only the obviously messianic ones. On the Emmaus
      road, Jesus rebukes two disciples for being slow to believe "all that
      the prophets have spoken," then, beginning with Moses and all the
      Prophets, interprets to them "in all the Scriptures the things
      concerning himself" — His suffering and His entrance into glory. He
      makes the same point to unbelieving religious leaders in John's Gospel:
      Moses wrote of Him, and to reject Moses' writings is, in effect, to
      reject His own words. Because all of Scripture concerns Christ and His
      gospel, missing Him in a passage is missing that passage's fullest
      meaning.

    heading: "What must we do: Scripture is not rightly read until it is obeyed"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "James 1:22-24"
        text: >-
          But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving
          yourselves. For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer,
          he is like a man who looks intently at his natural face in a
          mirror. For he looks at himself and goes away and at once forgets
          what he was like.
    commentary: >-
      Having asked what a passage says and what it means, one question
      remains, justified on its own terms and asked last: what must we do?
      James warns believers not merely to hear the Word but to be doers of
      it, warning that anyone who hears without doing deceives himself — like
      a man who glances in a mirror, walks away, and immediately forgets what
      he looked like. Insight gained from Scripture that never reaches
      obedience is not truly gained at all; the whole purpose of praying,
      reading accurately, and asking what a passage shows about God,
      ourselves, and the gospel is to arrive at concrete, Spirit-empowered
      obedience. Practically, this means naming something specific to start
      doing, something to stop doing, and someone to tell.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Understanding Scripture is the Spirit's to give, so reading rightly
    begins by asking for it in prayer — giving thanks, confessing sin,
    interceding for others, and specifically asking for understanding and
    power to obey (John 14:25-26; John 16:13-14).
  - >-
    Before asking what a passage means, we must know accurately what it
    says — rightly handling the word of truth takes the same care Nehemiah's
    readers gave, reading clearly and giving the sense before any
    application is drawn (2 Timothy 2:15; Nehemiah 8:8).
  - >-
    Because Scripture is God's own breathed-out Word, every passage can be
    asked what it shows about God, and the answer is God revealing Himself,
    not merely a human record about Him (2 Timothy 3:16-17; 2 Peter 1:20-21).
  - >-
    Because our own hearts are deceitful and cannot fully know themselves,
    every passage can be asked what it shows about us — Scripture, sharper
    than any two-edged sword, examines and corrects us rather than merely
    confirming what we already believe (Jeremiah 17:9-10; Hebrews 4:12).
  - >-
    Because all of Scripture, from Moses and the Prophets onward, ultimately
    concerns Christ, every passage can be asked what it shows about the
    gospel of Jesus and His work of redemption (Luke 24:25-27; John 5:46-47).
  - >-
    Hearing Scripture without doing what it says is self-deception, so the
    process ends by naming something to start doing, stop doing, or tell
    someone about — the aim is Spirit-empowered obedience, not information
    alone (James 1:22-24).
