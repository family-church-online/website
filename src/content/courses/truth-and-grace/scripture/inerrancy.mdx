point: >-
  That because of the divine origin and inspiration of its words, the
  Scriptures are true in all that they affirm and are trustworthy and without
  error.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Holy Scriptures consist of the 66 books of the Old
  and New Testaments in their original writing; (2) that the Spirit of God so
  influenced the authors that these writings are the Word of God; (3) that
  because of the divine origin and inspiration of its words, the Scriptures
  are true in all that they affirm and are trustworthy and without error; and
  (4) that these Scriptures are the complete and only written revelation of
  God to people; (5) that every translation that is faithful to the originals
  and responsibly interpreted clearly reveals those truths necessary for our
  salvation, godly living, and maturity in Christ; and (6) that Scripture
  alone is our sufficient and final authority in all matters of belief and
  practice.
sections:
  - id: A
    heading: "Scripture cannot be broken: Christ's own verdict on inerrancy"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "John 10:35"
        text: >-
          If he called them gods to whom the word of God came — and Scripture
          cannot be broken.
      - ref: "John 17:17"
        text: >-
          Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.
    commentary: >-
      In these two texts the Lord Jesus Himself bears direct and personal
      witness to the inerrancy of Scripture — and His testimony takes two
      distinct forms. In John 10:35, the claim is incidental and all the more
      revealing for it: in the middle of a dispute, Jesus appeals to a single
      word from Psalm 82 and treats its accuracy as unassailable common
      ground, stating plainly that "Scripture cannot be broken." The phrase
      carries the force of absolute indestructibility — the written Word
      cannot be invalidated, overturned, or shown to be in error. In John
      17:17, the setting shifts to Christ's high-priestly prayer, where He
      makes an absolute identification: the Father's Word is not merely
      truthful but is truth itself. Because sanctification — the whole work of
      transformation into holiness — is accomplished through this Word, its
      truthfulness is not merely a theological point but a personal and saving
      reality for every believer. Together these two texts establish inerrancy
      as the conviction of Christ Himself: incidentally stated as common
      ground, and solemnly declared in prayer.

  - id: B
    heading: "Divine origin guarantees trustworthiness: theopneustos and the Spirit's guarantee"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Timothy 3:16–17"
        text: >-
          All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching,
          for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that
          the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work.
      - ref: "John 14:26"
        text: >-
          But the Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my
          name, he will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all
          that I have said to you.
    commentary: >-
      These two texts ground the trustworthiness of Scripture in the character
      and work of God Himself. In 2 Timothy 3:16–17, Paul's declaration that
      all Scripture is "God-breathed" (theopneustos) connects the reliability
      of every part of the Bible directly to its divine origin: because
      Scripture is exhaled from God, and God cannot lie, the text shares in
      His perfect truthfulness. The comprehensiveness of "all Scripture" rules
      out any portion that might fall below this standard — inerrancy is not
      selective but total. In John 14:26, Christ promises that the Holy Spirit
      will teach the disciples all things and bring to their remembrance
      everything He has said, providing the specific divine guarantee behind
      the accuracy of the apostolic witness recorded in the New Testament.
      Taken together, these texts show that inerrancy is not a claim made
      about human authors but a consequence of divine authorship: the God who
      breathed out the Scriptures and superintended their composition through
      the Spirit cannot be the source of error.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Scripture cannot be broken — Jesus Himself treated the written Word as
    indestructible and incapable of error, making inerrancy the conviction of
    Christ, not merely the invention of later theologians.
  - >-
    The truthfulness of Scripture is grounded not in human reliability but in
    the character of God Himself: because God cannot lie and His Word is
    breathed out by Him, the Scriptures share in His perfect truthfulness.
  - >-
    All of Scripture — not merely selected portions — is God-breathed and
    therefore entirely trustworthy; inerrancy applies comprehensively across
    every book, chapter, and verse of the canon.
  - >-
    The sanctifying and equipping work of Scripture depends directly on its
    inerrancy: a Word that contained error could not reliably transform lives
    or adequately prepare believers for every good work.
  - >-
    The Holy Spirit's promise to guide the apostles into "all truth" provides
    the divine guarantee behind the New Testament's accuracy, linking the
    doctrine of inerrancy to the ongoing ministry of the Spirit.
