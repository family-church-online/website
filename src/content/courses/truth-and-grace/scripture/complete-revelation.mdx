point: >-
  We believe that these Scriptures are the complete and only written
  revelation of God to people.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Holy Scriptures consist of the 66 books of the Old
  and New Testaments in their original writing; (2) that the Spirit of God so
  influenced the authors that these writings are the Word of God; (3) that
  because of the divine origin and inspiration of its words, the Scriptures
  are true in all that they affirm and are trustworthy and without error; (4)
  that these Scriptures are the complete and only written revelation of God to
  people; (5) that every translation that is faithful to the originals and
  responsibly interpreted clearly reveals those truths necessary for our
  salvation, godly living, and maturity in Christ; and (6) that Scripture
  alone is our sufficient and final authority in all matters of belief and
  practice.

    scriptures:
      - ref: "Matthew 24:35"

      - ref: "Romans 3:2"

      - ref: "Acts 7:38"
        text: >-

    commentary: >-
      These three texts together establish Scripture as the uniquely
      authoritative written communication of God — permanent, covenantally
      entrusted, and designed to be sufficient for every generation. In
      Matthew 24:35, Christ declares that His words will outlast creation
      itself: their permanence signals finality, not provisionality. No
      written word of God awaits supplementation; what He has spoken in
      Scripture is the enduring and definitive revelation. In Romans 3:2, Paul
      uses the term "oracles of God" (logia) to describe the Hebrew Scriptures
      — a designation reserved exclusively for authoritative divine
      utterances, marking the written Word as categorically distinct from all
      other human or religious writing. In Acts 7:38, Stephen describes the
      words given through Moses as "living oracles" — dynamically active
      divine speech given not merely for one moment in history but to be
      passed down as God's sufficient word for His people in every age.
      Together these texts establish that there is one uniquely authoritative
      written revelation from God — not one among many, but the singular
      deposit of divine speech entrusted to His people.

      - ref: "1 Corinthians 4:6"
        text: >-

          benefit, brothers, that you may learn by us not to go beyond what is
          written, that none of you may be puffed up in favor of one against

    commentary: >-
      These two Pauline texts together reveal how the apostle himself
      understood the nature and scope of Scripture as God's complete written
      word. In 1 Corinthians 9:8–10, Paul draws a principle of astonishing
      reach from a single verse in the Law of Moses: what was written then was
      written for us — not merely for its original recipients, but for every
      subsequent generation of God's people. This demonstrates that Scripture
      was never a provisional communication awaiting replacement; it was
      purposefully given as God's enduring word to all who are His. In 1
      Corinthians 4:6, Paul states the apostolic principle plainly: "do not go
      beyond what is written." This instruction is only coherent if "what is
      written" — the Scriptures — constitutes a defined and complete boundary.
      To venture beyond it is not spiritual advance but presumption. Taken
      together, these texts show that Paul regarded the written Word as both
      the sufficient norm for all generations and the authoritative limit that
      disciplines all teaching and practice.

    heading: "The canon is inviolable: divine warnings against addition or subtraction"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Deuteronomy 4:1–2"
        text: >-
          "And now, O Israel, listen to the statutes and the rules that I am

          giving you. You shall not add to the word that I command you, nor

      - ref: "Proverbs 30:5–6"
        text: >-

          refuge in him. Do not add to his words, lest he rebuke you and you
          be found a liar.
      - ref: "Revelation 22:18–19"
        text: >-
          I warn everyone who hears the words of the prophecy of this book: if
          anyone adds to them, God will add to him the plagues described in
          this book, and if anyone takes away from the words of the book of
          this prophecy, God will take away his share in the tree of life and
          in the holy city, which are described in this book.
    commentary: >-
      From the opening of God's written covenant at Sinai to the closing
      warning of the entire New Testament canon, Scripture frames itself as a
      revelation that is complete and therefore inviolable. In Deuteronomy
      4:1–2, Moses frames the prohibition on addition or subtraction within
      the context of life and blessing: to receive God's Word as given is to
      flourish; to tamper with it is to stray from the path of life. In
      Proverbs 30:5–6, the wisdom writer grounds this prohibition in God's own
      character — because every word of God proves true, to add to His words
      is not a theological refinement but a moral offence, making oneself a
      liar before the God who cannot lie. In Revelation 22:18–19, the most
      solemn warning in the entire canon is placed at its close, attaching the
      gravest consequences to addition or subtraction. The fact that the same
      protective principle appears at the beginning, middle, and end of the
      canon — across law, wisdom, and prophecy — demonstrates that God has
      always understood His written revelation as a complete and guarded
      deposit, not a provisional beginning awaiting expansion.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Scripture is closed and complete. God has given humanity a definitive
    written revelation in the 66 books of the Bible; no further written word
    from God is to be expected or accepted alongside it.
  - >-
    The Bible's own testimony demands its sufficiency. Warnings against adding
    to God's word — repeated from Deuteronomy through Revelation — make no
    sense unless Scripture is understood as complete and whole.
  - >-
    "What is written" is the authoritative boundary. Paul's apostolic
    principle (do not go beyond what is written) calls believers to find in
    Scripture everything needed for faith, doctrine, and godly living.
  - >-
    God's written word was given for every generation. Because Scripture is
    the complete written revelation of God, it speaks with living relevance to
    all people in all ages — not merely to its original recipients.
  - >-
    Adding to Scripture is a serious offence before God. The solemn warnings
    of both Moses and the apostle John make clear that the completeness of
    Scripture is to be guarded with reverence, and that any claim to
    supplement it stands under divine rebuke.
