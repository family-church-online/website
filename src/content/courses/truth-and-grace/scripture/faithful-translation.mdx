point: >-
  We believe that every translation that is faithful to the originals and
  responsibly interpreted clearly reveals those truths necessary for our
  salvation, godly living, and maturity in Christ.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Holy Scriptures consist of the 66 books of the Old
  and New Testaments in their original writing; (2) that the Spirit of God so
  influenced the authors that these writings are the Word of God; (3) that
  because of the divine origin and inspiration of its words, the Scriptures
  are true in all that they affirm and are trustworthy and without error; (4)
  that these Scriptures are the complete and only written revelation of God to
  people; (5) that every translation that is faithful to the originals and
  responsibly interpreted clearly reveals those truths necessary for our
  salvation, godly living, and maturity in Christ; and (6) that Scripture
  alone is our sufficient and final authority in all matters of belief and
  practice.

    heading: "Scripture is accessible: faithful translation and responsible interpretation yield genuine truth"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Timothy 2:15"
        text: >-
          Do your best to present yourself to God as one approved, a worker
          who has no need to be ashamed, rightly handling the word of truth.
      - ref: "2 Peter 3:16"
        text: >-
          …as he does in all his letters when he speaks in them of these
          matters. There are some things in them that are hard to understand,
          which the ignorant and unstable twist to their own destruction, as
          they do the other Scriptures.
      - ref: "Acts 17:11"
        text: >-
          Now these Jews were more noble than those in Thessalonica; they
          received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily
          to see if these things were so.
    commentary: >-
      These three texts together establish that Scripture, when handled with
      diligence and honesty, is genuinely accessible — yielding the truth God
      intends to those who engage with it responsibly. In 2 Timothy 2:15,
      Paul's exhortation to "rightly handle the word of truth" presupposes two
      equally important things: that Scripture can be handled rightly, and
      that it can be handled wrongly. The invitation to diligent, careful
      engagement assumes that such engagement will produce genuine and
      trustworthy results — faithful translation and responsible
      interpretation is precisely this kind of right handling. In 2 Peter
      3:16, Peter acknowledges that some passages are difficult, but locates
      the danger not in the text itself but in those who wilfully twist it.
      The text is not the problem; distortion is. This actually confirms the
      accessibility of Scripture to the honest and careful reader. In Acts
      17:11, the Bereans are commended for combining eager reception with
      daily, disciplined examination of the Scriptures — and their practice is
      held up as a model precisely because it produces trustworthy
      conclusions. Taken together, these texts affirm that a faithful
      translation, responsibly interpreted, is exactly the kind of handling
      Scripture rewards: truth genuinely accessible to those who seek it with
      diligence and integrity.

    heading: "Scripture is sufficient: everything needed for salvation, godliness, and maturity is already given"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Peter 1:3–4"
        text: >-
          His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life
          and godliness, through the knowledge of him who called us to his own
          glory and excellence, by which he has granted to us his precious and
          very great promises, so that through them you may become partakers
          of the divine nature, having escaped from the corruption that is in
          the world because of sinful desire.
      - ref: "Psalm 19:7–14"
        text: >-
          The law of the Lord is perfect, reviving the soul; the testimony of
          the Lord is sure, making wise the simple; the precepts of the Lord
          are right, rejoicing the heart; the commandment of the Lord is pure,
          enlightening the eyes; the fear of the Lord is clean, enduring
          forever; the rules of the Lord are true, and righteous altogether.
          More to be desired are they than gold, even much fine gold; sweeter
          also than honey and drippings of the honeycomb. Moreover, by them is
          your servant warned; in keeping them there is great reward. Who can
          discern his errors? Declare me innocent from hidden faults. Keep
          back your servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have
          dominion over me! Then I shall be blameless, and innocent of great
          transgression. Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my
          heart be acceptable in your sight, O Lord, my rock and my redeemer.
    commentary: >-
      These two texts together establish that Scripture is not merely a useful
      resource but a fully sufficient provision — containing everything needed
      for salvation, holy living, and growth into maturity in Christ. In 2
      Peter 1:3–4, Peter makes a sweeping and comprehensive claim: God's
      divine power has granted "all things that pertain to life and godliness"
      through the knowledge of Him mediated in His word and promises. Nothing
      necessary for the whole Christian life lies beyond what God has already
      given. The promises of Scripture are the very means by which believers
      are transformed and drawn into participation in the divine nature — a
      work of such depth and scope that no supplement could be needed. In
      Psalm 19:7–14, the psalmist catalogues the active effects of God's word
      on the whole person with specificity and delight: it revives the soul,
      imparts wisdom to the simple, brings joy to the heart, enlightens the
      eyes, warns against sin, and brings great reward. These are precisely
      the effects required for salvation, godly living, and maturity — and the
      psalmist's own response of longing and prayerful transformation is
      itself a model of what faithful engagement produces. Together these
      texts affirm that a translation faithful to these promises and this word
      carries within it the full power to accomplish all that God intends for
      His people.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Faithful translation preserves the power of the original. A translation
    that honestly renders what God gave in the original languages carries
    within it everything needed for salvation, sanctification, and growth in
    Christ.
  - >-
    Responsible interpretation is both possible and required. The Bible calls
    its readers to diligent, careful, and humble handling of the text —
    assuming that such handling yields genuine and trustworthy truth.
  - >-
    Difficulty in Scripture is not the same as inaccessibility. While some
    passages are complex, the problem Peter identifies is wilful distortion,
    not inherent obscurity; the truths necessary for life in Christ are
    genuinely accessible.
  - >-
    God's word is sufficient for the whole Christian life. The Scriptures
    contain everything that pertains to life and godliness — no supplementary
    teaching or tradition is needed for salvation, holy living, or maturity in
    Christ.
  - >-
    Scripture transforms those who receive it rightly. The Psalms and the
    apostles alike bear witness that God's word, faithfully engaged, produces
    real and profound change — reviving souls, imparting wisdom, and
    conforming believers to the character of Christ.
