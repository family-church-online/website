point: >-
  That the Holy Scriptures consist of the 66 books of the Old and New
  Testaments in their original writing.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Holy Scriptures consist of the 66 books of the Old
  and New Testaments in their original writing; (2) that the Spirit of God so
  influenced the authors that these writings are the Word of God; (3) that
  because of the divine origin and inspiration of its words, the Scriptures
  are true in all that they affirm and are trustworthy and without error; (4)
  that these Scriptures are the complete and only written revelation of God to
  people; (5) that every translation that is faithful to the originals and
  responsibly interpreted clearly reveals those truths necessary for our
  salvation, godly living, and maturity in Christ; and (6) that Scripture
  alone is our sufficient and final authority in all matters of belief and
  practice.

    heading: "The canon is a divinely appointed, defined, and authoritative collection"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Romans 3:2"
        text: >-
          Much in every way. To begin with, the Jews were entrusted with the
          oracles of God.
      - ref: "Matthew 5:18"
        text: >-
          For truly, I say to you, until heaven and earth pass away, not an
          iota, not a dot, will pass from the Law until all is accomplished.
      - ref: "Luke 24:27"
        text: >-
          And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to
          them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself.
    commentary: >-
      These three texts together establish the canon as something far more
      than a product of human religious history — it is a divinely appointed,
      precisely defined, and coherent body of writing. In Romans 3:2, Paul
      describes the Hebrew Scriptures using the weighty term "oracles of God"
      (logia), elevating them to the level of authoritative divine utterance
      and grounding the canon's origin not in human consensus but in God's own
      initiative and providential entrustment. In Matthew 5:18, the Lord Jesus
      upholds even the smallest details of the written Law — its very letters
      and markings — as possessing eternal authority, implying that the
      Scriptures exist as a fixed and bounded text whose precise form matters
      to God Himself. In Luke 24:27, the risen Christ moves through the
      breadth of the Old Testament canon — from Moses through all the Prophets
      — treating "all the Scriptures" as a recognised, coherent whole that
      bears unified witness to Himself. Together these texts establish that
      the canon is not arbitrary or accidental: it is divinely given,
      precisely bounded, and sufficient to bear the full weight of God's
      redemptive revelation.

  - id: B
    heading: "The canon is closed and complete: neither to be added to nor subtracted from"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Revelation 22:18–19"
        text: >-
          I warn everyone who hears the words of the prophecy of this book: if
          anyone adds to them, God will add to him the plagues described in
          this book, and if anyone takes away from the words of the book of
          this prophecy, God will take away his share in the tree of life and
          in the holy city, which are described in this book.
      - ref: "Deuteronomy 4:1–2"
        text: >-
          "And now, O Israel, listen to the statutes and the rules that I am
          teaching you, and do them, that you may live, and go in and take
          possession of the land that the Lord, the God of your fathers, is
          giving you. You shall not add to the word that I command you, nor
          take from it, that you may keep the commandments of the Lord your
          God that I command you."
      - ref: "Proverbs 30:5–6"
        text: >-
          Every word of God proves true; he is a shield to those who take
          refuge in him. Do not add to his words, lest he rebuke you and you
          be found a liar.
    commentary: >-
      From the giving of the Law at Sinai through the close of the New
      Testament canon, Scripture itself consistently legislates against human
      addition or subtraction from God's Word. In Deuteronomy 4:1–2, Moses
      frames the prohibition on alteration within the context of life and
      blessing: to receive God's Word as given is to flourish; to tamper with
      it is to stray from the path of life. In Revelation 22:18–19, the most
      solemn warning in all of Scripture is attached to exactly the same
      principle at the close of the New Testament, showing that the canon's
      closure carries divine sanction from first to last. Proverbs 30:5–6
      grounds the canon's sufficiency most deeply: because every word of God
      proves true, to add to His words is not a theological refinement but a
      moral offence — it is to make oneself a liar before the God who cannot
      lie. Taken together, these three texts establish that the 66-book canon
      is the complete and inviolable act of God's written self-disclosure,
      sufficient for all His purposes and not subject to human revision or
      expansion.

takeaways:
  - >-
    The Holy Scriptures are a specific, defined collection — 66 books, neither
    more nor less — entrusted by God to His people and recognised through
    history as His authoritative written Word.
  - >-
    Jesus Himself affirmed the precise authority of the written Scriptures,
    validating even their smallest textual details and reading the whole canon
    as a unified witness to Himself.
  - >-
    God has consistently warned against adding to or subtracting from His
    Word, which confirms that the canon is complete and closed — a finished
    act of divine self-disclosure.
  - >-
    The boundaries of Scripture are not a human invention but reflect God's
    own design to give His people a sufficient and trustworthy written
    revelation.
  - >-
    Our confidence in the 66-book canon rests ultimately on the character of
    God Himself: because every word of God proves true, we can receive the
    Scriptures with full trust and without anxiety.
