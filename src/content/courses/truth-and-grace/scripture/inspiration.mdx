statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Holy Scriptures consist of the 66 books of the Old
  and New Testaments in their original writing; (2) that the Spirit of God so
  influenced the authors that these writings are the Word of God; (3) that
  because of the divine origin and inspiration of its words, the Scriptures
  are true in all that they affirm and are trustworthy and without error; (4)
  that these Scriptures are the complete and only written revelation of God to
  people; (5) that every translation that is faithful to the originals and
  responsibly interpreted clearly reveals those truths necessary for our
  salvation, godly living, and maturity in Christ; and (6) that Scripture
  alone is our sufficient and final authority in all matters of belief and
  practice.

    heading: "Inspiration is divine origination, not human will"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Peter 1:19-21"
        text: >-
          And we have the prophetic word more fully confirmed, to which you
          will do well to pay attention as to a lamp shining in a dark place,
          until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts,
          knowing this first of all, that no prophecy of Scripture comes from
          someone's own interpretation. For no prophecy was ever produced by
          the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along
          by the Holy Spirit.
    commentary: >-
      The mechanism of biblical inspiration is not human initiative or
      interpretation but divine origination. Peter insists that the prophetic
      writings did not arise from the wills or minds of their authors: rather,
      men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit. The
      Greek word behind "carried along" (pheromenoi) pictures a vessel driven
      by the wind — the human authors were genuinely active, yet the sovereign
      directing power came entirely from God. Inspiration, on this account, is
      not a matter of God assisting gifted writers; it is a matter of God
      speaking through them so completely that what they wrote constitutes His
      own Word. This is the foundational principle from which every other
      claim about Scripture — its authority, its truthfulness, its sufficiency
      — ultimately derives.

  - id: B
    heading: "God speaks through human instruments: the consistent pattern of dual authorship"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Luke 1:70"
        text: >-
          As he spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets from of old.
      - ref: "Acts 1:16"
        text: >-
          "Brothers, the Scripture had to be fulfilled, which the Holy Spirit
          spoke beforehand by the mouth of David concerning Judas, who became
          a guide to those who arrested Jesus."
      - ref: "Acts 3:18"
        text: >-
          But what God foretold by the mouth of all the prophets, that his
          Christ would suffer, he thus fulfilled.
    commentary: >-
      Across Luke's Gospel and the early chapters of Acts, a single
      theological formula appears with striking consistency: God spoke *by the
      mouth of* His prophets. In Luke 1:70, Zechariah identifies God as the
      speaker and the prophets as the mouths. In Acts 1:16, Peter attributes
      the Psalms not ultimately to David but to the Holy Spirit who spoke "by
      the mouth of David." In Acts 3:18, Peter again frames the prophetic
      Scriptures as what "God foretold by the mouth of all the prophets." The
      pattern is unambiguous: the human author is real and acknowledged, but
      the divine author is ultimate and primary. This dual-authorship model —
      fully human, fully divine — is not a theological refinement of later
      centuries; it is the apostolic church's settled, working assumption
      about the Scriptures they proclaimed and applied. The unity implied in
      "all the prophets" also rules out a piecemeal view of inspiration; the
      Spirit's authorship is consistent across the entire prophetic deposit.

  - id: C
    heading: "Scripture is the Spirit's living and present Word, not merely a past deposit"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Ephesians 6:17"
        text: >-
          And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which
          is the word of God.
      - ref: "Hebrews 3:7"
        text: >-
          Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says, "Today, if you hear his
          voice..."
    commentary: >-
      These two texts together reveal that the Spirit's relationship to
      Scripture is not merely historical — it is present and active. In
      Ephesians 6:17, Paul calls the word of God "the sword of the Spirit":
      the sword belongs to the Spirit, it is His weapon, and He wields it. In
      Hebrews 3:7, the author introduces a quotation from Psalm 95 with the
      present-tense verb "says" — not "said." The Holy Spirit does not merely
      have spoken through these words; He speaks through them now, to every
      generation that reads or hears them. Taken together, these texts
      establish that inspiration is not a completed past event that produced
      an inert text. The Spirit who breathed out the Scriptures remains
      vitally connected to them, continuing to speak and to work through them.
      This is why the Bible retains full authority, power, and freshness
      across all ages — it is the Spirit's own living instrument.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Scripture is the product of divine origination through human
    instrumentality — the Holy Spirit carried the authors along, so that what
    they wrote was genuinely God's own Word, not merely human reflection on
    divine things.
  - >-
    The Bible consistently presents a dual-authorship model: the human authors
    wrote with their own voices and circumstances, yet the Holy Spirit was the
    sovereign source and guide of all they recorded.
  - >-
    The Spirit's connection to Scripture is not merely historical — He
    continues to speak through the written Word in the present, making it a
    living and active instrument in every age.
  - >-
    The early church, including the apostles Peter and Paul, understood and
    taught verbal inspiration as a settled conviction, not an academic theory
    — it was the foundation on which they proclaimed and applied Scripture.
  - >-
    Because the Holy Spirit is the author of Scripture, the Word of God
    carries divine authority that no human writing can claim — to receive it
    with faith is to receive the voice of God Himself.
