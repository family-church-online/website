  That He created humankind male and female as His image bearers, endowing

statement: >-
  We believe (1) that in the beginning God sovereignly created all things
  visible and invisible and took delight in it all; (2) that He created
  humankind male and female as His image bearers, endowing each with a dignity
  of equal worth and calling to govern and care for His creation; (3) that
  human life is sacred to God and begins at conception; (4) that God created
  marriage to be an exclusive and lifelong union between a natural man and a
  natural woman, giving the man the responsibility of self-sacrificial and
  loving leadership and the woman the responsibility of compatible support;
  and (5) that the first man, Adam, sinned and thereby incurred the penalty of
  death, physical and spiritual, for all people, and as a consequence of his
  sin, all humans inherit a sinful nature, which results in actual
  transgression and personal guilt.

    heading: "Humanity is created in God's image, male and female, and commissioned to steward creation"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Genesis 1:26-31"
        text: >-
          Then God said, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness.
          And let them have dominion over the fish of the sea and over the
          birds of the heavens and over the livestock and over all the earth
          and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth." So God
          created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him;
          male and female he created them. And God blessed them. And God said
          to them, "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it,
          and have dominion over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the
          heavens and over every living thing that moves on the earth." And
          God said, "Behold, I have given you every plant yielding seed that
          is on the face of all the earth, and every tree with seed in its
          fruit. You shall have them for food. And to every beast of the earth
          and to every bird of the heavens and to everything that creeps on
          the earth, everything that has the breath of life, I have given
          every green plant for food." And it was so. And God saw everything
          that he had made, and behold, it was very good. And there was
          evening and there was morning, the sixth day.
    commentary: >-
      This passage is the primary scriptural foundation for understanding
      humanity's unique place within creation. The deliberate divine counsel —
      "Let us make man in our image" — sets humanity apart from everything
      else God had spoken into existence, indicating that the creation of
      human beings was a special and intentional act within the Godhead. Both
      male and female are equally made in God's image, each bearing His
      likeness fully and without qualification, which establishes the equal
      dignity and worth of every human person regardless of sex. The dominion
      mandate that follows is not a licence for exploitation but a royal
      calling — human beings are appointed as God's representatives, entrusted
      with the stewardship and care of everything He has made. That God
      surveys this completed work and declares it "very good" confirms that
      the creation of gendered, image-bearing humanity was precisely what He
      intended and in which He delighted.

    heading: "The male-female distinction is a deliberate and permanent feature of God's creational design"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Mark 10:6"
        text: >-
          But from the beginning of creation, 'God made them male and female.'
    commentary: >-
      In this brief but weighty statement, Jesus Himself reaches back to
      Genesis to affirm that the male-female distinction is not a cultural
      development or biological accident, but a deliberate creative act of God
      established "from the beginning of creation." By grounding His teaching
      in the Creator's original design, Jesus signals that the binary sexual
      differentiation of humanity carries permanent theological significance.
      The fact that Jesus quotes Genesis 1 as authoritative and definitive
      reinforces that the image-bearing identity of humankind is inseparable
      from the way God made us — as male and female together. This verse
      confirms that both sexes, in their distinction and complementarity,
      reflect something true about God and His purposes for humanity.

  - id: C
    heading: "The image of God in humanity remains morally active and demands how we treat one another"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "James 3:9"
        text: >-
          With it we bless our Lord and Father, and with it we curse people
          who are made in the likeness of God.
    commentary: >-
      James invokes the image of God not as an abstract theological concept
      but as a present, practical reality that must govern how we treat one
      another. His argument is striking: to curse a fellow human being is to
      direct contempt at one who bears God's own likeness, making such speech
      an indirect dishonour of God Himself. This verse demonstrates that the
      imago Dei — the image of God in humanity — was not lost at the fall to
      the point where it no longer carries moral weight; every person we
      encounter still bears God's likeness and is therefore owed genuine
      dignity and respect. For the believer, this truth transforms ordinary
      human interaction into a matter of profound theological consequence. The
      equal worth of every person affirmed in Point 2 is not merely a social
      value but a reality rooted in the act of creation itself.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Every human being is made in God's image. The imago Dei is not reserved
    for a particular class, sex, or people group — God created both male and
    female as His image bearers, establishing the equal dignity and worth of
    all people from the very beginning.
  - >-
    Sexual differentiation is God's deliberate creative act. Jesus confirms in
    Mark 10:6 that humanity as male and female is not incidental but
    foundational — part of God's original and purposeful design "from the
    beginning of creation."
  - >-
    Image-bearing confers a royal stewardship. To bear God's image is to be
    entrusted with the care and governance of His creation. Dominion is a
    calling rooted in responsibility, not a licence for exploitation —
    humanity governs as a servant of the Creator, not as an independent owner.
  - >-
    The image of God has ongoing moral significance. James 3:9 shows that the
    imago Dei remains active after the fall, continuing to ground the dignity
    of every person we encounter and shaping the ethical demands of how we
    speak and act toward one another.
  - >-
    Humanity's creation was the crown of God's creative work. The specific
    divine deliberation before creating humanity, and the final verdict of
    "very good" after, together signal that human beings hold a unique and
    honoured place within all that God has made — loved, blessed, and
    purposefully designed to reflect His glory.
