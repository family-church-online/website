point: >-
  That human life is sacred to God and begins at conception.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that in the beginning God sovereignly created all things
  visible and invisible and took delight in it all; (2) that He created
  humankind male and female as His image bearers, endowing each with a dignity
  of equal worth and calling to govern and care for His creation; (3) that
  human life is sacred to God and begins at conception; (4) that God created
  marriage to be an exclusive and lifelong union between a natural man and a
  natural woman, giving the man the responsibility of self-sacrificial and
  loving leadership and the woman the responsibility of compatible support;
  and (5) that the first man, Adam, sinned and thereby incurred the penalty of
  death, physical and spiritual, for all people, and as a consequence of his
  sin, all humans inherit a sinful nature, which results in actual
  transgression and personal guilt.

    heading: "The sanctity of human life is rooted in God's image and His personal authorship of every person"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Genesis 9:6"
        text: >-
          "Whoever sheds the blood of man, by man shall his blood be shed, for
          God made man in his own image."
      - ref: "Psalms 119:73"
        text: >-
          Your hands have made and fashioned me; give me understanding that I
          may learn your commandments.
    commentary: >-
      Together, these two verses establish that the sanctity of human life
      rests not on social consensus or legal protection but on the direct
      creative act of God. Genesis 9:6 grounds the prohibition against taking
      human life explicitly in the imago Dei — to destroy a human being is to
      assault the living image of the Creator. The severity of the prescribed
      penalty reflects the gravity of that offence. Psalm 119:73 deepens this
      by affirming that every individual life is not the product of impersonal
      biological process but of God's own hands — He made and fashioned this
      particular person. The psalmist's response to this recognition is not
      pride but worship and submission: God as personal Maker shapes the whole
      posture of the creature before Him. Together, these texts show that the
      sacredness of human life is a theological reality flowing from God's
      image and God's authorship, not a value that human authority bestows or
      removes.

    heading: "God's intimate knowledge and active formation of every person begins in the womb"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Psalms 139:13-16"
        text: >-
          For you formed my inward parts; you knitted me together in my
          mother's womb. I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully
          made. Wonderful are your works; my soul knows it very well. My frame
          was not hidden from you, when I was being made in secret,
          intricately woven in the depths of the earth. Your eyes saw my
          unformed substance; in your book were written every one of them, the
          days that were formed for me, when as yet there was none of them.
      - ref: "Jeremiah 1:5"
        text: >-
          "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were
          born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations."
    commentary: >-
      These two passages together establish that God's knowledge of and
      purposeful engagement with a human person does not begin at birth, or
      even at the moment of physical conception, but precedes both. Psalm 139
      portrays God as the intimate craftsman knitting and weaving each person
      together in the womb, with eyes fixed even on "unformed substance" — the
      earliest, pre-formed stage of human existence. The days of this
      individual's life were already written in God's book before any of them
      had come to be, affirming that personal identity and divine purpose are
      realities from the very origin of life. Jeremiah 1:5 reaches even
      further back: God's knowledge of and consecration of Jeremiah preceded
      His formation of Jeremiah's body in the womb, locating human personhood
      firmly in the prior knowledge and intentional purpose of God rather than
      in any developmental milestone. Together these texts provide the
      clearest biblical grounding for the conviction that life, as known and
      purposed by God, begins at conception.

      the grandest acts of cosmic creation, showing that each individual human
      life is a purposeful divine act of the same order as stretching out the
      heavens and founding the earth. The human spirit is not a by-product of
      biological processes but something God personally forms within each
      person. Luke 1:41 brings this into the New Testament with striking
      concreteness: John the Baptist, still in the womb, leaps in response to
      the presence of the incarnate Christ — a Spirit-prompted act of
      recognition that affirms the genuine personhood and awareness of the
      unborn child. Together these texts make clear that life in the womb is
      not a pre-personal biological entity awaiting personhood, but already a
      person held in the direct creative and sustaining hands of God.

takeaways:
  - >-
    The sanctity of human life is rooted in God's image. Genesis 9:6 makes
    clear that the prohibition against taking human life rests on the imago
    Dei — every human being bears God's likeness, making each life sacred by
    virtue of its Creator, not by social agreement or legal status.
  - >-
    God is personally involved in the formation of every human life. Psalms
    119:73, 139:13-16, and Isaiah 42:5 together portray God not as a distant
    first cause but as the intimate, hands-on Maker of every individual person
    — knitting, forming, and breathing life into each one.
  - >-
    Human life and personal identity begin before birth. Psalm 139 speaks of
    God's knowledge of "unformed substance," and Jeremiah 1:5 places God's
    knowledge and consecration of a person before their formation in the womb
    — leaving no biblical basis for locating the beginning of meaningful human
    life at any point after conception.
  - >-
    The unborn child is a genuine human person. Luke 1:41 presents John the
    Baptist in the womb as a person capable of Spirit-prompted response to the
    presence of Christ, affirming that personhood is not contingent on birth
    but is present from the earliest stage of human life.
  - >-
    Every human life exists as a direct gift from God. Because God is the One
    Who gives breath, forms the spirit, and knits each person together, human
    life at every stage carries a dignity and sacredness that belongs to it by
    virtue of its divine origin — and that no circumstance or human decision
    can legitimately remove.
