point: >-
  That God created marriage to be an exclusive and lifelong union between a
  natural man and a natural woman, giving the man the responsibility of
  self-sacrificial and loving leadership and the woman the responsibility of
  compatible support.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that in the beginning God sovereignly created all things
  visible and invisible and took delight in it all; (2) that He created
  humankind male and female as His image bearers, endowing each with a dignity
  of equal worth and calling to govern and care for His creation; (3) that
  human life is sacred to God and begins at conception; (4) that God created
  marriage to be an exclusive and lifelong union between a natural man and a
  natural woman, giving the man the responsibility of self-sacrificial and
  loving leadership and the woman the responsibility of compatible support;
  and (5) that the first man, Adam, sinned and thereby incurred the penalty of
  death, physical and spiritual, for all people, and as a consequence of his
  sin, all humans inherit a sinful nature, which results in actual
  transgression and personal guilt.

    scriptures:
      - ref: "Genesis 2:24"
        text: >-
          Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast
          to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.
      - ref: "Malachi 2:15"
        text: >-
          Did he not make them one, with a portion of the Spirit in their
          union? And what was the one God seeking? Godly offspring. So guard
          yourselves in your spirit, and let none of you be faithless to the
          wife of your youth.
      - ref: "Matthew 19:5-6"
        text: >-
          and said, 'Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and
          hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh'? So they
          are no longer two but one flesh. What therefore God has joined
          together, let not man separate.
    commentary: >-
      These three passages together establish the foundation, character, and
      authority of marriage as God has designed it. Genesis 2:24 provides the
      creational definition: the three movements of leaving, holding fast, and
      becoming one flesh portray marriage as a covenant of exclusive
      commitment and profound personal unity between one man and one woman.
      Malachi 2:15 deepens this by revealing that God Himself is present and
      active in the union — the Spirit is a portion of the marriage — and that
      the covenant's purpose includes the raising of godly offspring,
      confirming that marriage is not a merely personal arrangement but one
      with generational and redemptive dimensions. Matthew 19 records Jesus
      citing Genesis 2:24 as the authoritative, unalterable standard, and His
      declaration — "what therefore God has joined together, let not man
      separate" — identifies God as the active agent in every true marriage.
      Marriage is therefore not a human institution that society may define or
      redefine, but a divine institution whose terms were set at creation and
      remain binding.

    commentary: >-
      Together these passages establish the distinct callings of husband and
      wife within marriage, and the high honour in which God holds the
      institution. Paul grounds both the wife's willing support and the
      husband's loving leadership not in cultural convention but in the
      relationship of Christ and His Church — the most exalted analogy
      Scripture offers. The wife's submission is not a mark of lesser worth
      but of ordered love within covenant, modelled on the Church's glad
      submission to Christ. The husband's headship is not a licence for
      domination but a call to self-giving love modelled on Christ's sacrifice
      — a love that gave everything for the sanctification and flourishing of
      the one He leads. Hebrews 13:4 frames the whole with a declaration of
      marriage's sacred honour: it is to be held in esteem by all, and its
      sexual exclusivity is a moral absolute with eternal consequences. These
      texts together show that the roles within marriage and the honour of the
      institution are inseparable from the gospel itself.

    commentary: >-
      Across both Testaments, Scripture maintains a consistent and unambiguous
      position: sexual practice is rightly expressed only within the covenant
      marriage of a man and a woman, and homosexual practice is a departure
      from God's creational design. Leviticus 18 and 20 prohibit male
      homosexual practice with the strongest available term — "abomination" —
      in the context of laws distinguishing Israel's sexual ethics from
      surrounding nations, signalling that this is not culturally relative but
      rooted in the created order. Paul in Romans 1 presents same-sex sexual
      practice as a paradigmatic consequence of the suppression of the
      knowledge of God, describing it as "contrary to nature" — contrary to
      the complementary design of male and female established in creation.
      First Corinthians 6 includes those who practice homosexuality among
      those who will not inherit the kingdom of God, but closes with the
      gospel's transforming word: "such were some of you." The same Lord who
      defines the standard of holiness is the One who washes, sanctifies, and
      justifies all who come to Him in repentance and faith. Scripture holds
      together moral clarity and redemptive hope without collapsing either.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Marriage is a divine institution, not a human one. Jesus' declaration that
    "God has joined together" what marriage unites establishes that the terms
    of marriage belong to the Creator, not to culture or legislation. Its
    definition as the union of one man and one woman is therefore not subject
    to redefinition by human authority.
  - >-
    The roles of husband and wife are grounded in a profound theological
    analogy. Ephesians 5 locates husbandly leadership and wifely support
    within the relationship of Christ and His Church — elevating both callings
    far above mere social convention and giving them a beauty and dignity
    rooted in the gospel itself.
  - >-
    Sacrificial love, not domination, defines biblical headship. The husband's
    leadership is measured by Christ's self-giving love for the Church — a
    love that gave everything for her sanctification and flourishing. Any
    exercise of headship that falls short of this standard has departed from
    Scripture's vision.
  - >-
    God's sexual ethic is rooted in creational design. The Old and New
    Testaments together present the exclusive sexual union of husband and wife
    as the boundary God has established for sexual expression. Sexual practice
    contrary to this design — including homosexual practice — is consistently
    presented in Scripture as a departure from God's order.
  - >-
    The gospel offers both a standard and a Saviour. 1 Corinthians 6:9-11
    holds together moral clarity and redemptive hope: the same Lord Who
    defines the standard of sexual holiness is the One Who washes, sanctifies,
    and justifies all who come to Him in repentance and faith.
