  Triumphing over sin, death, and all the forces of evil, and purchasing for Himself a people out of every tribe, language, and nation.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Lord Jesus Christ lived a sinless life, was perfectly obedient to His Father in every way, and ushered in the promised Kingdom of God in all His words and deeds; (2) that, for the joy set before Him, He offered up Himself as the perfect substitutionary and atoning sacrifice for sinners, (3) triumphing over sin, death, and all the forces of evil, and purchasing for Himself a people out of every tribe, language, and nation; (4) that on the cross the Son of God received in His person the due penalty of God’s wrath against our sin, and (5) that His shed blood is the sole basis for our salvation and the greatest revelation of the glory of the grace of our God.
sections:
  - id: "A"
    heading: "Christ triumphed over sin, putting it away once for all and cancelling the record of debt against us."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Hebrews 9:26"
        text: "for then he would have had to suffer repeatedly since the foundation of the world. But as it is, he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself."
      - ref: "Colossians 2:14"
        text: "by canceling the record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands. This he set aside, nailing it to the cross."
    commentary: >-
      Christ has appeared once for all to put away sin, and the finality of His work is the mark of His triumph. Where the old sacrifices had to be repeated, His single offering accomplishes what they could only foreshadow. Sin is not merely covered but put away. Paul describes our sin as a record of debt with legal demands that stood against us, and Christ cancelled it by nailing it to the cross, so that the charge against us is gone. The accuser has nothing left to bring, for the debt has been fully paid. This is the ground on which the powers of evil are disarmed.

  - id: "B"
    heading: "Christ triumphed over death, destroying the devil's power and abolishing death itself."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "1 Corinthians 15:54-57"
        text: "When the perishable puts on the imperishable, and the mortal puts on immortality, then shall come to pass the saying that is written: “Death is swallowed up in victory.” “O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting?” The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law. But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."
      - ref: "Hebrews 2:14"
        text: "Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil."
      - ref: "2 Timothy 1:10"
        text: "and which now has been manifested through the appearing of our Savior Christ Jesus, who abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel."
    commentary: >-
      Paul looks ahead to the day when death itself is swallowed up in victory, and he taunts it as a defeated enemy. He identifies sin as the sting of death, which is why Christ's dealing with sin on the cross disarms death at its root. The victory is entirely God's gift, given "through our Lord Jesus Christ." The Son took on flesh and blood so that He could die, and through His death overthrow the one who held the power of death. Christ does not conquer by avoiding death but by passing through it, and in this way those who lived in fear of death are set free. Paul also declares that Christ has abolished death, rendering it powerless over those who belong to Him, and has brought life and immortality to light through the gospel. Death is not merely postponed but its authority is broken.

  - id: "C"
    heading: "Christ triumphed over the devil and the forces of evil, destroying their works."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "John 12:31"
        text: "Now is the judgment of this world; now will the ruler of this world be cast out."
      - ref: "1 John 3:8"
        text: "Whoever makes a practice of sinning is of the devil, for the devil has been sinning from the beginning. The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil."
    commentary: >-
      Jesus speaks these words as He anticipates the cross, showing that He understands His death as a decisive victory. The "ruler of this world" is the devil, whose hold over humanity is broken by what Christ is about to accomplish. The hour of apparent defeat is in fact the hour of judgment on the powers of darkness. John states the purpose of the Incarnation in terms of victory over the devil's works: the Son of God came to destroy the whole structure of sin and evil that the enemy has built. This gives believers confidence that the powers arrayed against them are already defeated. It also calls them to live as those who belong to the conquering Son and not to the one who has been overthrown.

  - id: "D"
    heading: "Christ purchased for Himself a people from every tribe, language, people, and nation."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Revelation 5:9"
        text: "And they sang a new song, saying, “Worthy are you to take the scroll and to open its seals, for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation.”"
      - ref: "Revelation 7:9"
        text: "After this I looked, and behold, a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, clothed in white robes, with palm branches in their hands."
      - ref: "Ephesians 1:11-14"
        text: "In him we have obtained an inheritance, having been predestined according to the purpose of him who works all things according to the counsel of his will, so that we who were the first to hope in Christ might be to the praise of his glory. In him you also, when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and believed in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it, to the praise of his glory."
    commentary: >-
      In the throne room of heaven the Lamb who was slain is declared worthy because of His triumph. His blood has ransomed people for God, which is the language of purchase and redemption, and its scope reaches across every tribe, language, people, and nation. John then sees the fruit of Christ's work as a multitude too great to count, standing before the throne and the Lamb, clothed in white and bearing palm branches, the signs of victory. Paul shows that the triumph of Christ results in a people who belong to God and share an inheritance in Him: both Jewish and Gentile believers hear the gospel, believe, and are sealed with the Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee that what Christ has won will be fully possessed. All of it is according to the counsel of God's will and to the praise of His glory.

takeaways:

  - "Christ destroyed the devil's power by passing through death, and He abolished death itself, bringing life and immortality to light."
