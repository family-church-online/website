  We believe (1) that the Lord Jesus Christ lived a sinless life, was perfectly obedient to His Father in every way, and ushered in the promised Kingdom of God in all His words and deeds; (2) that, for the joy set before Him, He offered up Himself as the perfect substitutionary and atoning sacrifice for sinners, (3) triumphing over sin, death, and all the forces of evil, and purchasing for Himself a people out of every tribe, language, and nation; (4) that on the cross the Son of God received in His person the due penalty of God’s wrath against our sin, and (5) that His shed blood is the sole basis for our salvation and the greatest revelation of the glory of the grace of our God.
sections:
  - id: "A"
    heading: "Christ's shed blood is the sole basis for our salvation: our redemption, forgiveness, and peace with God."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Colossians 1:14"
        text: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins."
      - ref: "Colossians 1:20"
        text: "and through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, making peace by the blood of his cross."
      - ref: "Hebrews 9:22"
        text: "Indeed, under the law almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins."
    commentary: >-
      This brief statement says that redemption and forgiveness are found "in whom," that is, in Christ alone. No other person or work is offered as a source of release from the bondage and guilt of sin. Redemption speaks of a price paid, and the context makes clear that the price was His blood. All who are in Him possess this forgiveness as a present and certain gift. Peace between God and His creation is made "by the blood of his cross," and by no other means. The reconciliation reaches across heaven and earth, showing the vast reach of what Christ's death accomplished. The enmity caused by sin is removed, and what was estranged is brought back to God. The blood of the cross is therefore the sole ground of every peace that God grants. The writer of Hebrews states the principle that forgiveness requires the shedding of blood, which is why no other basis for salvation will suffice. Since forgiveness cannot come without blood, and since the blood of Christ is the only blood that truly cleanses, His shed blood is the sole foundation of our salvation. This verse closes every other door and opens the one that God has provided.

  - id: "B"
    heading: "Christ's shed blood is the greatest revelation of the glory of God's grace."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Romans 8:32"
        text: "He who did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us all, how will he not also with him graciously give us all things?"
      - ref: "Ephesians 1:5-7"
        text: "he predestined us for adoption to himself as sons through Jesus Christ, according to the purpose of his will, to the praise of his glorious grace, with which he has blessed us in the Beloved. In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace."
      - ref: "Revelation 5:9"
        text: "And they sang a new song, saying, “Worthy are you to take the scroll and to open its seals, for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation.”"
    commentary: >-
      Paul points to the cross as the supreme proof of God's generosity: the Father did not spare His own Son but gave Him up for us all. If God has given the greatest gift, there is no good thing He will withhold from His people. Our salvation therefore rests wholly on what the Father and the Son have done, and not on anything we bring. The cross reveals a grace that is costly to God and free to us. Paul also places redemption through the blood of Christ at the center of God's eternal purpose to adopt sinners as His sons. The whole plan is directed "to the praise of his glorious grace," so that the glory of God's grace is the goal of salvation itself. Forgiveness of trespasses comes "through his blood," which identifies the cross as the sole means of that forgiveness. It flows from the riches of God's grace and not from any merit in us. In heaven the redeemed ascribe their salvation entirely to the Lamb, who was slain and who ransomed them by His blood. The new song celebrates Christ's worthiness, and none of the glory is given to the ransomed themselves. The cross is here the theme of eternal worship, which shows that it is the greatest revelation of God's grace. All creation will forever praise the Lamb for what His blood has accomplished.
