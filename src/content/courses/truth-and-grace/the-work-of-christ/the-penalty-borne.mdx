point: >-
  That on the cross the Son of God received in His person the due penalty of God’s wrath against our sin.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Lord Jesus Christ lived a sinless life, was perfectly obedient to His Father in every way, and ushered in the promised Kingdom of God in all His words and deeds; (2) that, for the joy set before Him, He offered up Himself as the perfect substitutionary and atoning sacrifice for sinners, (3) triumphing over sin, death, and all the forces of evil, and purchasing for Himself a people out of every tribe, language, and nation; (4) that on the cross the Son of God received in His person the due penalty of God’s wrath against our sin, and (5) that His shed blood is the sole basis for our salvation and the greatest revelation of the glory of the grace of our God.
sections:
  - id: "A"
    heading: "The Son personally bore the penalty for sin, as foretold by Isaiah and endured on the cross."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Isaiah 53:5-6"
        text: "But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned—every one—to his own way; and the Lord has laid on him the iniquity of us all."
      - ref: "Matthew 27:45-46"
        text: "Now from the sixth hour there was darkness over all the land until the ninth hour. And about the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, saying, “Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?” that is, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?”"
    commentary: >-
      Isaiah describes the Servant as pierced and crushed for transgressions that were not His own. The chastisement that should have fallen on us fell on Him, and the result is our peace with God. The prophet makes plain that this was the Lord's own doing, for the Lord laid on Him the iniquity of us all. The penalty for sin was thus borne personally by the Servant rather than being set aside. The darkness that covers the land and the cry of Jesus mark the awful moment in which He bears the judgment of God. His words from Psalm 22 express the real experience of abandonment as He stands in the place of sinners. The Son who had known unbroken fellowship with the Father now endures the separation that sin deserves. This is a holy mystery before which believers bow in reverence and gratitude.

  - id: "B"
    heading: "The Son was made sin and became a curse, receiving in our place what sin deserves."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Corinthians 5:21"
        text: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God."
      - ref: "Galatians 3:13"
        text: "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us—for it is written, “Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree.”"
    commentary: >-
      Paul teaches that God made the sinless Christ to be sin for us, so that the guilt of sinners was reckoned to Him. Because God's wrath is the righteous response to sin, taking sin upon Himself meant that Christ received what sin deserves. The exchange is entirely for our sake, resulting in the gift of God's righteousness to those in Him. The law pronounces a curse on all who fail to keep it, and Christ redeemed us by taking that curse upon Himself. Paul uses the language of the tree to show that Jesus bore the very judgment that Scripture pronounces on the guilty. He did this "for us," meaning in our place and on our behalf. Because the curse has been borne, those who trust Him are no longer under it. This shows the justice and the mercy of God meeting at the cross.

  - id: "C"
    heading: "Christ offered Himself to God, paying the penalty of death that sin demands."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Hebrews 9:14"
        text: "how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God, purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God."
      - ref: "Hebrews 9:22"
        text: "Indeed, under the law almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins."
    commentary: >-
      Christ offered Himself to God, which shows that the sacrifice was directed Godward to satisfy divine justice. His blood purifies the conscience as nothing in the old order could, because it deals with the real guilt of sin before God. The offering was made through the eternal Spirit and without blemish, so it was entirely acceptable. A cleansed conscience is the fruit of the penalty having been fully paid. The whole sacrificial system of the old covenant taught that sin carries the penalty of death and that forgiveness comes only through a life given up. The shedding of blood shows that sin must be answered for and cannot simply be ignored. These sacrifices all pointed forward to Christ, who gave His life in full. At the cross the penalty was paid in His person, and forgiveness became possible.

  - id: "D"
    heading: "The Son bore God's wrath, and in doing so revealed God's love."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Romans 5:9"
        text: "Since, therefore, we have now been justified by his blood, much more shall we be saved by him from the wrath of God."
      - ref: "1 John 4:10"
        text: "In this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins."
    commentary: >-
      Paul links justification directly to the blood of Christ and draws from it the certainty of future deliverance from God's wrath. If the blood of Christ has already secured our justification, the wrath of God cannot fall on those who are in Him. This implies that the wrath was real and that it has been dealt with at the cross. Believers can therefore stand with confidence, saved by Him from the judgment they deserved. John shows that the propitiation is itself the supreme expression of God's love and not a rival to it. God did not wait for us to love Him, but sent His own Son to turn aside His wrath against our sin. The Father who is just is also the Father who gives, and the Son who bears the penalty is the gift of the Father's love. At the cross, wrath and love are not opposed but are revealed together.
