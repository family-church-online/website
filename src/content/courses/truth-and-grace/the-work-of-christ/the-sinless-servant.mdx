  We believe (1) that the Lord Jesus Christ lived a sinless life, was perfectly obedient to His Father in every way, and ushered in the promised Kingdom of God in all His words and deeds; (2) that, for the joy set before Him, He offered up Himself as the perfect substitutionary and atoning sacrifice for sinners, (3) triumphing over sin, death, and all the forces of evil, and purchasing for Himself a people out of every tribe, language, and nation; (4) that on the cross the Son of God received in His person the due penalty of God’s wrath against our sin, and (5) that His shed blood is the sole basis for our salvation and the greatest revelation of the glory of the grace of our God.

    scriptures:
      - ref: "Isaiah 53:7-9"
        text: "He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth; like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent, so he opened not his mouth. By oppression and judgment he was taken away; and as for his generation, who considered that he was cut off out of the land of the living, stricken for the transgression of my people? And they made his grave with the wicked and with a rich man in his death, although he had done no violence, and there was no deceit in his mouth."
    commentary: >-
      Centuries before the Incarnation, Isaiah portrayed the Servant of the Lord as One who suffered without protest and without guilt. His silence before His oppressors reveals a perfect submission to the Father's will, even when that will led to affliction and death. The prophet's testimony that He had done no violence and that no deceit was found in His mouth declares His complete innocence in both deed and word. In the life of Jesus, this portrait finds its fulfilment, for His obedience was never reluctant but willing, steady, and without blemish.

  - id: "B"
    heading: "Christ lived a sinless life in word and deed, tempted in every respect yet without sin."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "1 Peter 2:22"
        text: "He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth."
      - ref: "Hebrews 4:15"
        text: "For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin."
    commentary: >-
      Peter, who walked closely with Jesus, bears eyewitness testimony to the purity of His life, echoing the words of Isaiah's prophecy. The statement covers both action and speech, showing that Christ's holiness was complete in every part of His conduct, and that even under unjust accusation and suffering He did not retaliate or speak falsely. The writer of Hebrews adds that this sinlessness was not the result of being shielded from temptation, for He was tempted in every respect as we are. His obedience was tested and proven, and at every point He remained faithful to the Father. Because His victory over temptation was real, His sympathy with our weaknesses is also real, and we draw near to a High Priest who both understands our struggle and is perfectly holy.

  - id: "C"
    heading: "Christ's sinlessness is the necessary foundation of His saving work."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Corinthians 5:21"
        text: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God."
      - ref: "1 John 3:5"
        text: "You know that he appeared to take away sins, and in him there is no sin."
    commentary: >-
      Paul states with great economy that Christ "knew no sin," affirming that His sinlessness was not merely outward conduct but the very character of His life. This truth is essential to the gospel, for only a sinless substitute could bear the sins of others without needing atonement for His own. John joins the purpose of Christ's coming with the purity of His person: He appeared to take away sins, and in Him there is no sin. His perfect obedience therefore stands as the foundation upon which the exchange of grace rests, our sin laid upon Him and His righteousness given to us. The sinless life of Jesus is not an isolated virtue but the necessary qualification for His saving work, and the pledge that His work will not fail.
