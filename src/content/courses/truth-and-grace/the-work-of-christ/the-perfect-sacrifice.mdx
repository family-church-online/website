point: >-
  That, for the joy set before Him, He offered up Himself as the perfect substitutionary and atoning sacrifice for sinners.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) that the Lord Jesus Christ lived a sinless life, was perfectly obedient to His Father in every way, and ushered in the promised Kingdom of God in all His words and deeds; (2) that, for the joy set before Him, He offered up Himself as the perfect substitutionary and atoning sacrifice for sinners, (3) triumphing over sin, death, and all the forces of evil, and purchasing for Himself a people out of every tribe, language, and nation; (4) that on the cross the Son of God received in His person the due penalty of God’s wrath against our sin, and (5) that His shed blood is the sole basis for our salvation and the greatest revelation of the glory of the grace of our God.

His present seat at God's right hand is the Father's confirmation that the offering was accepted.

    heading: "Christ's sacrifice was substitutionary: the righteous for the unrighteous."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Isaiah 53:5-12"
        text: "But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned—every one—to his own way; and the Lord has laid on him the iniquity of us all. He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth; like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent, so he opened not his mouth. By oppression and judgment he was taken away; and as for his generation, who considered that he was cut off out of the land of the living, stricken for the transgression of my people? And they made his grave with the wicked and with a rich man in his death, although he had done no violence, and there was no deceit in his mouth. Yet it was the will of the Lord to crush him; he has put him to grief; when his soul makes an offering for guilt, he shall see his offspring; he shall prolong his days; the will of the Lord shall prosper in his hand. Out of the anguish of his soul he shall see and be satisfied; by his knowledge shall the righteous one, my servant, make many to be accounted righteous, and he shall bear their iniquities. Therefore I will divide him a portion with the many, and he shall divide the spoil with the strong, because he poured out his soul to death and was numbered with the transgressors; yet he bore the sin of many, and makes intercession for the transgressors."
      - ref: "1 Peter 3:18"
        text: "For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit."
    commentary: >-
      Isaiah's prophecy portrays the Servant as one who is pierced and crushed in the place of others, bearing the iniquity that belongs to the many. The language of substitution is unmistakable: He is wounded for our transgressions so that we might be healed, and the Lord lays on Him the sin of us all. His soul is made "an offering for guilt," showing that His death is a sacrifice offered to God and not merely the tragedy of an innocent man. Yet the passage also looks beyond the suffering to satisfaction, for the Servant sees the fruit of His anguish and makes many to be accounted righteous. Peter summarizes the substitutionary pattern in the simple phrase "the righteous for the unrighteous." Christ suffered once and for all, so His sacrifice is complete and needs no repetition. The purpose of His suffering is relational: that He might bring us to God. His being made alive in the Spirit points to the vindication that follows His death.

  - id: "C"
    heading: "Christ's sacrifice was perfect because He was without sin and without blemish."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "2 Corinthians 5:21"
        text: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God."
      - ref: "Hebrews 9:14"
        text: "how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God, purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God."
    commentary: >-
      Here the great exchange at the heart of substitution is stated with striking brevity. The sinless Christ is treated as sinful on our behalf, bearing the guilt that was ours. In return, all who are in Him receive the very righteousness of God. The writer of Hebrews contrasts the repeated sacrifices of the old covenant with the once-for-all offering of Christ. He "offered himself," so that His sacrifice was willing and personal, and He did so "without blemish," fulfilling the requirement of a perfect offering. This sacrifice does what animal blood never could, cleansing the conscience itself and not merely the outward person. The result is freedom to leave behind dead works and to serve the living God, and the sacrifice was perfect precisely because the One who offered it had no sin of His own.

  - id: "D"
    heading: "Christ's sacrifice was atoning: the propitiation for our sins."
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Romans 3:23-25"
        text: "for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith. This was to show God’s righteousness, because in his divine forbearance he had passed over former sins."
      - ref: "1 John 2:2"
        text: "He is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only but also for the sins of the whole world."
    commentary: >-
      Paul establishes the universal need of humanity before declaring the remedy that God Himself has provided. Christ is "put forward as a propitiation by his blood," which means His sacrifice satisfies the righteous anger of God against sin. This atonement is received as a gift of grace through faith, and not earned by any human effort. In the cross God also demonstrates that He is just, for sin is not overlooked but fully dealt with in the person of His Son. John affirms again that Christ Himself is the propitiation, the sacrifice that turns aside God's wrath against sin. The sufficiency of this atoning work is set out in the widest terms, reaching beyond the first readers to people of every nation, and this gives believers confidence that nothing further is needed to deal with their sin.

takeaways:
  - >-
    Christ offered Himself willingly as a sacrifice, taking the place of sinners rather than merely suffering alongside them.
  - >-
    His death is both substitutionary, bearing our sin in our place, and atoning, satisfying God's justice through propitiation.
  - >-
    The sacrifice was perfect because the One who offered it was without blemish and without sin.
  - >-
    The cross was endured "for the joy set before Him," revealing a Saviour whose sacrifice flowed from purpose and love rather than reluctance.
  - >-
    This once-for-all offering cleanses the conscience and brings sinners to God, received by grace through faith.
