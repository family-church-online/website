statement: >-
  We believe (1) that when the time was right, the eternal Son of God took on
  human nature, and was made like us in every way, yet without sin; (2) that,
  after being conceived by the Holy Spirit, He was born of the virgin Mary,
  was named Jesus (meaning 'God saves'), and from conception was and always
  will be fully God and fully man in one person; and (3) that He is,
  therefore, the perfect and only mediator between God and people.

    heading: "The eternal Son entered human history at God's appointed time"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "John 1:14"
        text: >-
          And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his
          glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and
          truth.
      - ref: "Galatians 4:4"
        text: >-
          But when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son, born
          of woman, born under the law.
    commentary: >-
      John announces the miracle of the Incarnation in the most direct terms:
      the eternal Word, who was with God and was God from the beginning,
      entered human existence by taking on flesh. "Dwelt among us" carries the
      sense of God pitching His tent in our midst, echoing the tabernacle of
      the Old Testament, and those who witnessed Him beheld His glory —
      confirming that in becoming man, He did not diminish His divine nature.
      Paul's words in Galatians reinforce that this was no accident, but the
      culmination of God's sovereign plan unfolding at precisely the right
      moment in history. The Son was "sent forth" by the Father, affirming
      both His pre-existence and the intentional, Trinitarian nature of the
      redemptive mission, and by being "born of woman, born under the law," He
      fully embraced human birth and placed Himself under the very obligations
      humanity had failed to keep.

    heading: "The Son's taking on human nature was an act of voluntary self-humbling"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Philippians 2:5-8"
        text: >-
          Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus,
          who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with
          God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form
          of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in
          human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of
          death, even death on a cross.
    commentary: >-
      This magnificent passage traces the humility of Christ from the glory of
      heaven to the shame of the cross. Though eternally existing in the form
      of God, He did not cling to His divine privileges but willingly emptied
      Himself, taking on the form of a servant. His becoming "like us in every
      way" is here shown to be an act of voluntary self-humbling, not a
      diminishing of who He is, but an addition of a genuinely human nature
      and experience — a mindset held up as the pattern for all believers to
      follow.

    commentary: >-
      The writer of Hebrews explains the purpose behind the Son's taking on
      flesh and blood: so that He might enter fully into the human condition,

      became flesh and blood, not as a mere appearance but as a genuine
      partaking of our nature — purposeful and redemptive, aimed at breaking
      the power of the one who held humanity in the fear of death.

    heading: "Christ was made like us in every way, tempted as we are, yet entirely without sin"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Hebrews 4:15"
        text: >-
          For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with
          our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we
          are, yet without sin.
    commentary: >-
      This verse holds together the twin truths of Christ's full humanity and
      His complete sinlessness. Because He was made like us in every respect,
      He is able to sympathise with our weaknesses, having genuinely faced
      temptation as we do. Yet, unlike us, He never yielded to sin, remaining
      perfectly holy throughout His earthly life. This qualifies Him uniquely
      to serve as our compassionate and perfect High Priest, one who
      understands our struggles from the inside while never having been
      compromised by them.
