statement: >-
  We believe (1) that when the time was right, the eternal Son of God took on
  human nature, and was made like us in every way, yet without sin; (2) that,
  after being conceived by the Holy Spirit, He was born of the virgin Mary,
  was named Jesus (meaning 'God saves'), and from conception was and always
  will be fully God and fully man in one person; and (3) that He is,
  therefore, the perfect and only mediator between God and people.

          to be born will be called holy—the Son of God."
      - ref: "Matthew 1:20-21"
        text: >-
          But as he considered these things, behold, an angel of the Lord
          appeared to him in a dream, saying, "Joseph, son of David, do not
          fear to take Mary as your wife, for that which is conceived in her
          is from the Holy Spirit. She will bear a son, and you shall call his
          name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins."
    commentary: >-
      These three annunciation texts together establish the miraculous origin
      and identity of the child to be born. Gabriel's word to Mary sets the
      naming of the child as Jesus, meaning "God saves," in motion — no human
      choice, but a divine appointment declaring from the outset the purpose
      for which He came. The mechanism of the conception is then revealed: the
      Holy Spirit's overshadowing power, not human agency, ensuring the child
      would be holy and rightly called the Son of God from His very beginning.
      Matthew's account confirms the same miraculous conception from Joseph's
      perspective and ties the naming directly to the child's mission, showing
      that from the earliest announcement of His coming, Christ's identity and
      His saving mission were inseparable.

    heading: "Christ's two natures held together: human descent and divine sonship"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Romans 1:3-4"
        text: >-
          concerning his Son, who was descended from David according to the
          flesh and was declared to be the Son of God in power according to
          the Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead, Jesus
          Christ our Lord.
    commentary: >-
      Paul holds together the two natures of Christ in a single sentence:
      descended from David according to the flesh, yet declared to be the Son
      of God in power. His human lineage roots Him firmly in Israel's history
      and the promises made to David, while His divine sonship is powerfully
      vindicated by His resurrection. This passage teaches that Christ's
      humanity and deity were never in competition but were always held
      together in the one person of the Lord Jesus.

    commentary: >-
      Paul contrasts Adam, the first man formed from the dust of the earth,
      with Christ, the second man who is from heaven. This heavenly origin

      took on a genuinely human nature in the Incarnation. As those united to
      Adam bear his earthly nature, so those united to Christ will bear the
      likeness of the heavenly man, a truth made possible only because the Son
      of Heaven truly became man.

  - id: D
    heading: "The fullness of deity dwelling in Christ bodily"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Colossians 1:19"
        text: >-
          For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell.
      - ref: "Colossians 2:9"
        text: >-
          For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily.
    commentary: >-
      These two brief but weighty statements affirm that the entirety of God's
      nature was pleased to take up residence in the person of Christ — not a
      partial or diminished deity indwelling a human body, but the complete
      fullness of God dwelling within Him, genuinely and bodily. Paul
      reinforces this truth with sharper precision in Colossians 2:9: Jesus
      Christ is fully God and fully man united in one person, not two persons
      or a blend of natures, but one Lord in whom deity and true humanity are
      perfectly and permanently joined. The Father's good pleasure in this
      arrangement reveals that the Incarnation was the deliberate and joyful
      outworking of the Godhead's eternal purpose, not a reluctant concession.
