point: >-
  That this triune God is perfect and infinite in His being and attributes —
  an all-holy, all-loving, and all-wise Sovereign.
statement: >-
  We believe (1) in one God who eternally exists in three co-equal persons -
  the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; (2) that though each divine person
  is fully and equally God, They differ in relationship towards each other and
  Their work in the world; (3) that this triune God is perfect and infinite in
  His being and attributes - an all-holy, all-loving, and all-wise Sovereign;
  (4) that from before creation, God determined to save sinners through the
  sacrifice of His Son, to govern all history according to His sovereign plan
  and to finally bring all things under one head, even Christ; and (5) that
  all things exist from Him, through Him, and for Him, and are created to
  display the glory of His wonderful Name.

    scriptures:
      - ref: "1 Timothy 6:15–16"
        text: >-
          Which he will display at the proper time — he who is the blessed and
          only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords, who alone has
          immortality, who dwells in unapproachable light, whom no one has
          ever seen or can see. To him be honour and eternal dominion. Amen.
    commentary: >-
      Paul's doxology ascends to the very summit of what can be said about
      God. He is not one ruler among many but the sole, unchallenged Sovereign
      over every king and every lord — His authority is absolute and
      unrivalled. His immortality belongs to Him entirely, not derived or
      shared with any creature, and the unapproachable light in which He
      dwells speaks of a holiness and majesty that no created being can bear
      to behold in its fullness. Together these qualities establish that God's
      perfection is not a theological abstraction but a blazing, living
      reality — He is infinite in His being in a way that places Him in a
      wholly different category from everything He has made. The doxological
      conclusion — "to him be honour and eternal dominion" — is the only
      fitting response to so great and perfect a God.

    scriptures:
      - ref: "Isaiah 6:3"
        text: >-
          And one called to another and said: "Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of
          hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory!"
      - ref: "1 Peter 1:15"
        text: >-
          But as he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your
          conduct.
    commentary: >-
      The seraphim's threefold repetition of "holy" is without parallel in
      Scripture, signalling that holiness is not one attribute among others
      but the defining atmosphere of God's very being — He is utterly set
      apart, transcendent, and wholly unlike anything in creation. Yet this
      same God of unapproachable holiness fills the whole earth with His
      glory, meaning His perfection presses into every corner of the created
      order, not confined to a distant realm. Peter draws out the immediate
      implication: because the God who calls His people is holy, the holiness
      of God is not a quality to be admired at arm's length but the very
      standard that summons His people toward transformation. The holy God who
      inhabits heavenly thrones is the same God who reaches down in grace to
      call sinners into relationship with Himself — and that call carries with
      it the imperative to reflect His character in all of life.

    commentary: >-
      Together these passages establish that God's love is not a sentiment He
      occasionally adopts but the very definition of His eternal being —
      John's declaration that "God is love" means love is not a role He plays
      but the expression of who He is. This infinite love is bound up with His
      unfailing faithfulness: Moses declares that God keeps covenant and
      steadfast love across a thousand generations, the psalmist describes His
      love as surpassing all national and temporal boundaries, and Paul
      exhausts every conceivable category of threat only to conclude that
      nothing in all creation can sever God's love from His people. The throne
      from which God rules rests on righteousness and justice, yet steadfast
      love and faithfulness attend His every movement toward His creation —
      His love is not in tension with His justice but flows from the same
      perfect character. The all-loving God is not defined by sentiment but by
      sovereign, covenant-keeping devotion that nothing can defeat or
      diminish.

      - ref: "1 Timothy 1:17"
        text: >-

          honour and glory forever and ever. Amen.
      - ref: "Isaiah 55:9"
        text: >-
          "For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher
          than your ways and my thoughts than your thoughts."
      - ref: "Romans 16:27"
        text: >-
          To the only wise God be glory forevermore through Jesus Christ!
          Amen.
    commentary: >-
      These passages together establish that God's wisdom is not merely vast
      but infinite — operating from an entirely different order of being than
      any creaturely mind can reach. Paul erupts in doxology after eleven
      chapters of theological argument, declaring God's judgements
      unsearchable and His ways inscrutable: no one has ever been His
      counsellor or placed Him in their debt, because He is the beginning,
      middle, and end of all things — from Him, through Him, and to Him. God
      uses the incomprehensible distance between heaven and earth to picture
      the gap between His thoughts and human thoughts, not to frustrate but to
      invite trust: the God whose wisdom exceeds all understanding is the same
      God who has spoken and revealed His purposes. Paul's closing doxology
      names Him explicitly as "the only wise God," wisdom belonging to Him
      essentially and exclusively, disclosed most fully through Jesus Christ —
      the One in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.
      The all-wise Sovereign governs history not from an impersonal
      omniscience but from a wisdom that is simultaneously infinite in its
      reach and gracious in its self-disclosure.

takeaways:
  - >-
    God's holiness is the foundation of all His other attributes. His
    perfection is not a balance of competing qualities but a unified, flawless
    whole — every attribute shines with the same infinite purity.
  - >-
    God's love is not a sentiment but a sovereign, eternal reality. Because
    God is love, His love for His people is as infinite and indestructible as
    His own being — no power in the universe can sever it.
  - >-
    God's faithfulness is the anchor of the believer's hope. The
    covenant-keeping God who has been faithful across a thousand generations
    is the same God whose promises sustain His people today. His character
    does not change.
  - >-
    God's wisdom is incomprehensibly vast yet graciously revealed. His ways
    are higher than ours by an infinite degree, yet He has chosen to make
    Himself known through His Word and supremely through His Son, inviting
    trust where full understanding is impossible.
  - >-
    Right knowledge of God's attributes leads to worship. Scripture does not
    present God's infinite perfections as merely academic truths; again and
    again, their proper conclusion is doxology — praise, awe, and the glad
    surrender of the whole self to so great a God.
