statement: >-
  We believe (1) in one God who eternally exists in three co-equal persons —
  the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; (2) that though each divine person
  is fully and equally God, they differ in relationship towards each other and
  their work in the world; (3) that this triune God is perfect and infinite in
  His being and attributes — an all-holy, all-loving, and all-wise Sovereign;
  (4) that from before creation, God determined to save sinners through the
  sacrifice of His Son, to govern all history according to His sovereign plan
  and to finally bring all things under one head, even Christ; and (5) that
  all things exist from Him, through Him, and for Him, and are created to
  display the glory of His wonderful Name.

    scriptures:
      - ref: "John 1:1–3"
        text: >-
          "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the
          Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made
          through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made."
      - ref: "John 3:16"
        text: >-
          "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever
          believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."
      - ref: "1 Corinthians 8:6"
        text: >-
          "yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things
          and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are
          all things and through whom we exist."
    commentary: >-
      Taken together, these three passages establish both the full equality of
      the divine persons and the real distinctions between Them. John 1
      declares the Son to be simultaneously with God — indicating distinct
      personhood in eternal communion with the Father — and God Himself,
      indicating full and equal deity. This is not a contradiction but the
      very heart of Trinitarian truth: the Son shares the divine nature
      completely, yet He is not the Father. John 3:16 reveals the shape of
      Their relationship in the work of redemption: the Father loves and
      gives, the Son is given and willingly comes — the Father sends, the Son
      is sent. Paul's precise language in 1 Corinthians 8:6 anchors this same
      truth at the level of creation and existence: all things are from the
      Father as their ultimate source, and all things are through the Son as
      the agent of their coming into being. Three passages, three angles — and
      one consistent testimony that the persons of the Godhead are co-equal in
      essence while genuinely distinct in relationship and role.

  - id: B
    heading: "The Spirit, the Son, and the Father: distinct roles in the life of the church"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "1 Corinthians 12:4–5"
        text: >-
          "Now there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there
          are varieties of service, but the same Lord."
    commentary: >-
      Paul draws a direct line between the diversity of gifts and ministries
      in the body of Christ and the distinct yet united work of the three
      divine persons. The Spirit distributes gifts; the Son — the Lord —
      appoints spheres of service; and the Father, as Paul makes explicit in
      the following verse, is the One who works all of it in everyone. Far
      from confusion, this reveals ordered cooperation: each divine person
      operates in the sphere that is particularly His, while remaining
      inseparable from the others in the one divine will and purpose. The
      church's rich variety of gifts and callings is therefore not accidental
      but a direct reflection of the Trinitarian God who gives, assigns, and
      works. The diversity within the body mirrors the distinctions within the
      Godhead, and the unity of the body reflects the unity of the one God.

          sanctification of the Spirit, for obedience to Jesus Christ and for
          sprinkling with his blood: May grace and peace be multiplied to
          you."
    commentary: >-
      These three passages provide the fullest panoramic view in Scripture of
      the Trinity at work in saving sinners. In Galatians 4, Paul traces the
      sequence with precision: the Father sends the Son into the world to
      accomplish redemption, then the Father sends the Spirit of the Son into
      the hearts of the redeemed, enabling them to cry out to God as Father.
      The whole arc of salvation — from the incarnation to the believer's most
      intimate prayer — is a Trinitarian movement. Ephesians 1 deepens this
      panorama by showing that the three persons each contribute a distinct
      and essential act: the Father chooses and predestines before the
      foundation of the world; the Son redeems through His blood and unites
      all things in Himself; the Spirit seals the believer as the guarantee of
      the inheritance yet to come. And Paul frames every stage with the same
      refrain — "to the praise of His glory" — showing that the distinct works
      of Father, Son, and Spirit are not separate plans but one unified and
      glorious purpose. Peter adds a brief but crystalline summary in his
      opening greeting: the Father foreknows and elects, the Spirit
      sanctifies, the Son receives the believer's obedience and cleanses by
      His blood. To know the triune God is to know Him in His works — each
      person carrying out His particular role in perfect communion with the
      others.

takeaways:
  - >-
    The one true God exists eternally as three co-equal, co-eternal persons —
    Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — each fully and equally God, yet distinct in
    personhood and relationship.
  - >-
    The differences between the persons of the Godhead are not differences of
    essence, worth, or glory, but of role, relationship, and mission within
    the eternal life and saving work of God.
  - >-
    Salvation itself is a Trinitarian work: the Father chooses and sends, the
    Son redeems and reconciles, and the Spirit applies and seals — and all
    three act in perfect harmony for the praise of God's glory.
  - >-
    Recognising the distinct roles of the divine persons enriches our worship
    and prayer, helping us to relate rightly to the Father through the Son and
    by the power of the Spirit.
  - >-
    The unity and diversity within the Godhead is the pattern for the church's
    life together, where varieties of gifts and service flourish under one
    Lord, one Spirit, and one God and Father of all.
