statement: >-
  We believe (1) in one God who eternally exists in three co-equal persons —
  the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; (2) that though each divine person
  is fully and equally God, They differ in relationship towards each other and
  Their work in the world; (3) that this triune God is perfect and infinite in
  His being and attributes — an all-holy, all-loving, and all-wise Sovereign;
  (4) that from before creation, God determined to save sinners through the
  sacrifice of His Son, to govern all history according to His sovereign plan
  and to finally bring all things under one head, even Christ; and (5) that
  all things exist from Him, through Him, and for Him, and are created to
  display the glory of His wonderful Name.
sections:

    heading: "God is the sole origin, sustainer, and goal of all things; His glory He will not give to another"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Isaiah 48:11"
        text: >-
          "For my own sake, for my own sake, I do it, for how should my name
          be profaned? My glory I will not give to another."
      - ref: "Romans 11:36"
        text: >-
          For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be
          glory forever. Amen.
    commentary: >-
      Together these two texts establish that the entire created order exists
      within a relationship of total dependence on and orientation toward God.
      Isaiah's emphatic declaration — "for my own sake, for my own sake" — is
      not a statement of divine selfishness but the declaration of the One who
      is the highest good in all existence: it would be wrong for God to act
      for the sake of anything less than Himself, because nothing else is
      worthy of that position. His glory He will not transfer or share with
      another, because there is no other being of comparable worth. Paul
      distils this into the most compressed theological formula in Scripture:
      the three prepositions — from, through, and to — encompass the entire
      relation of everything that exists to its Creator. All things find their
      origin in God (from), their sustenance and governance through God
      (through), and their ultimate purpose in God (to). Nothing exists
      independently of Him, nothing is maintained apart from Him, and nothing
      reaches its true end except in returning glory to Him. The doxology that
      closes Romans 11:36 is not a pious addition but the only logical
      conclusion to what has just been stated.

    heading: "The salvation of sinners is itself a theatre of God's glory, displayed across the ages"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Ephesians 1:5–6, 14"
        text: >-
          He predestined us for adoption to himself as sons through Jesus
          Christ, according to the purpose of his will, to the praise of his
          glorious grace, with which he has blessed us in the Beloved… who is
          the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it,
          to the praise of his glory.
      - ref: "Ephesians 2:7"
        text: >-
          So that in the coming ages he might show the immeasurable riches of
          his grace in kindness toward us in Christ Jesus.
    commentary: >-
      Paul's great opening doxology in Ephesians is structured around a
      recurring refrain that reveals the ultimate purpose of every saving act:
      it is directed "to the praise of his glorious grace" and "to the praise
      of his glory." Election, adoption, redemption, and the sealing of the
      Spirit are not ends in themselves but movements within the larger drama
      of God's self-display. This means that the salvation of sinners is not
      merely a humanitarian rescue operation but a theatre in which the
      magnificence of God's grace is put on display for the wonder of the
      redeemed and the watching universe. Ephesians 2:7 extends this horizon
      into eternity: the redeemed community will serve "in the coming ages" as
      a permanent, living demonstration of the immeasurable riches of God's
      grace. The word "immeasurable" signals that no accounting, no matter how
      extended, will ever exhaust the wonder of what God has done in Christ.
      The Church is, in this sense, God's masterwork of self-revelation —
      created to display who He is.

    heading: "The end of all things is universal, unending worship: creation fulfils its purpose in doxology"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Revelation 5:13"
        text: >-
          And I heard every creature in heaven and on earth and under the
          earth and in the sea, and all that is in them, saying, "To him who
          sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing and honour and glory
          and might forever and ever!"
    commentary: >-
      John's vision of the consummation of all things is a vision of
      universal, unending doxology. Every creature — in every realm of
      existence, in every corner of creation — joins in the ascription of
      blessing, honour, glory, and might to the Father and the Son. This is
      not a coerced performance but the fulfilment of the very purpose for
      which all things were made: to glorify their Creator and Redeemer. The
      breadth of the chorus — heaven, earth, under the earth, and sea —
      reflects the scope of Romans 11:36: all things are from Him, through
      Him, and to Him, and here in Revelation 5 that "to Him" finds its
      perfect and everlasting expression. The whole creation, redeemed and
      renewed, becomes the amphitheatre of God's glory. This final vision is
      not an addition to what creation was always about — it is creation
      arriving at its appointed destination.

takeaways:
  - >-
    God is the origin, sustainer, and goal of all things. The three
    prepositions of Romans 11:36 — from, through, and to — establish that
    nothing exists independently of God and that nothing reaches its true end
    apart from returning glory to Him.
  - >-
    The glory of God is the ultimate purpose of creation. God did not create
    the world for any purpose outside of Himself; He created it to display the
    wonders of His name. This is not divine self-centredness but the proper
    ordering of all things around the one Being who is infinitely worthy.
  - >-
    Salvation itself is a display of God's glory. The redemption of sinners is
    not merely a rescue mission; it is the means by which the immeasurable
    riches of God's grace are put on permanent, universal display — in this
    age and in all the ages to come.
  - >-
    The believer's existence is inherently doxological. Because all things
    exist for God, the redeemed person's life finds its deepest meaning and
    truest fulfilment in the praise and glorification of the One from whom all
    things come.
  - >-
    The end of all things is universal worship. Revelation 5 shows us where
    history is heading: every creature in every realm joining in the endless
    ascription of glory to God and the Lamb. Creation's story begins and ends
    with the glory of its Maker.
