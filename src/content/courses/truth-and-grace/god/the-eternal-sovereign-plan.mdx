point: >-
  That from before creation, God determined to save sinners through the

statement: >-
  We believe (1) in one God who eternally exists in three co-equal persons —
  the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; (2) that though each divine person
  is fully and equally God, They differ in relationship towards each other and
  Their work in the world; (3) that this triune God is perfect and infinite in
  His being and attributes — an all-holy, all-loving, and all-wise Sovereign;
  (4) that from before creation, God determined to save sinners through the
  sacrifice of His Son, to govern all history according to His sovereign plan
  and to finally bring all things under one head, even Christ; and (5) that
  all things exist from Him, through Him, and for Him, and are created to
  display the glory of His wonderful Name.
sections:

    heading: "God's saving purpose is eternal: chosen in Christ before the foundation of the world"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "John 3:16"
        text: >-
          "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever
          believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."
      - ref: "Ephesians 1:4–5, 11, 13"
        text: >-
          Even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that
          we should be holy and blameless before him. In love he predestined
          us for adoption to himself as sons through Jesus Christ, according
          to the purpose of his will… In him we have obtained an inheritance,
          having been predestined according to the purpose of him who works
          all things according to the counsel of his will… In him you also,
          when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and
          believed in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit.
    commentary: >-
      Together these passages establish that the salvation of sinners did not
      arise as a divine response to human crisis but flows from God's eternal
      character and eternal counsel. The "giving" of the Son in John 3:16 is
      not an improvised act of rescue but the costly expression of a love as
      old as eternity itself — God parting with the most precious thing in all
      existence. Paul draws back the curtain on what this eternal love
      purposed: before the foundation of the world, God chose His people in
      Christ, predestined them for adoption, and ordered the entire sweep of
      redemptive history — election, inheritance, and the sealing of the
      Spirit — according to "the purpose of him who works all things according
      to the counsel of his will." The phrase "before the foundation of the
      world" places these saving purposes outside of time altogether, in the
      eternal counsel of the Trinity. This is not a cold decree but an act of
      love: it is "in love" that the Father predestined His children for
      adoption through the Son. To believe is to step into a plan that was
      already in place before the first moment of creation.

    scriptures:
      - ref: "1 Timothy 1:15"
        text: >-
          The saying is trustworthy and deserving of full acceptance, that
          Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the
          foremost.
      - ref: "2 Timothy 1:9"
        text: >-
          Who saved us and called us to a holy calling, not because of our
          works but because of his own purpose and grace, which he gave us in
          Christ Jesus before the ages began.
      - ref: "Revelation 13:8"
        text: >-
          And all who dwell on earth will worship it, everyone whose name has
          not been written before the foundation of the world in the book of
          life of the Lamb who was slain.
    commentary: >-
      These three texts together establish that the mission of Christ as
      Saviour of sinners and the grace extended to the redeemed are both
      realities that were fixed in God's eternal purpose before the ages
      began. First Timothy 1:15 declares Christ's coming to be purposeful and
      defined — He did not arrive and discover a role; He came specifically to
      save sinners, including those as deeply fallen as Paul considered
      himself. Second Timothy 1:9 removes all ambiguity about the basis of
      that calling: it is entirely from God's side, rooted in "his own purpose
      and grace" given "before the ages began" — prior to any human work or
      merit. Revelation 13:8 seals the point with a striking image: the names
      of the redeemed are written in the Lamb's book of life "before the
      foundation of the world," and the Lamb Himself is described as having
      been slain from that same eternal standpoint. The sacrifice of the Son
      was not a contingency plan activated by the fall but the very heart of
      what God purposed from eternity.

          inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay his hand or say to him,

        text: >-
          And he made from one man every nation of mankind to live on all the

    commentary: >-
      Together these passages make the case that God's governance of history
      is not passive observation or contingent response but active, purposeful
      direction of all events — including human evil, political powers,
      individual suffering, and the rise and fall of nations — toward His
      predetermined end. Joseph's word to his brothers is the paradigmatic
      statement: human intent and divine intent are real and distinct, but
      God's intent governs and redeems even the worst of what humans purpose.
      Isaiah 46 presses this to its logical extreme: God's unique ability to
      declare the end from the beginning is offered as the very proof of His
      deity — His counsel shall stand, and His purpose shall be accomplished,
      not eventually or tentatively, but with certainty. Nebuchadnezzar's
      confession in Daniel 4 adds the widest possible scope: every inhabitant
      of earth, and every member of the heavenly host, is subject to the will
      of the One who does as He pleases, and none can restrain Him. Paul
      frames all of this within God's redemptive intention: even the ordering
      of nations and their epochs serves the larger purpose of making God
      findable to those who seek Him.

    heading: "All of history is moving toward its appointed goal: all things united under the headship of Christ"
    scriptures:
      - ref: "Ephesians 1:10"
        text: >-
          As a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in him,
          things in heaven and things on earth.
    commentary: >-
      This single verse names the ultimate goal toward which all of God's
      sovereign governance of history is moving: the uniting of all things —
      in heaven and on earth — under the headship of Christ. The Greek word
      translated "unite" or "sum up" carries the sense of bringing everything
      to its proper head, as one gathers scattered items and places them under
      a single point of order. The "fullness of time" indicates that this
      cosmic consummation will come at the moment God has appointed — not
      before, not after. Every thread of history, every act of providence,
      every step of redemption is being woven together toward this one
      magnificent end. The sovereign plan of God is not merely about saving
      individuals but about the restoration and re-heading of the entire
      created order in the Lord Jesus Christ.

takeaways:
  - >-
    God's plan of salvation is eternal, not reactive. The decision to save
    sinners through the sacrifice of the Son was made before the foundation of
    the world, in the free and sovereign love of God — not in response to
    human need or human merit.
  - >-
    Sovereign governance covers all of history. From the rise and fall of
    nations to the suffering of an individual like Joseph, God works all
    things according to the counsel of His will. Nothing falls outside the
    scope of His purposeful governance.
  - >-
    The cross was central to God's eternal plan. Christ is "the Lamb slain
    before the foundation of the world" — the sacrifice of the Son was not a
    contingency plan but the very heart of what God purposed from eternity.
  - >-
    God's purposes cannot be thwarted. What He has spoken, He will bring to
    pass; what He has purposed, He will do. The believer's confidence rests
    not on human faithfulness but on the unshakeable resolve of the sovereign
    God.
  - >-
    All of history is moving toward Christ. The grand goal of God's sovereign
    plan is the uniting of all things in heaven and on earth under the
    headship of Jesus Christ — a cosmic consummation toward which every moment
    of history is being purposefully directed.
