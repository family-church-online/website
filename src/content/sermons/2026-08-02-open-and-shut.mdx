# Open and shut

### Philadelphia's Exceptional Letter

Revelation 3:7 introduces the letter to the church in Philadelphia. This is an exceptional letter because the people in Philadelphia didn't have anything that Jesus confronted. No correction, no rebuke. Just encouragement—Jesus saying well done and keep going. It's the only letter like that, and it's exciting for many different reasons.

I'm going to go through this letter verse by verse, explain the important features, and then tell you the story of a man whose name we don't know, but he's an amazing man from John chapter 9. There's such attitude in this guy, such character, such faithfulness, and it couldn't have been easy for him.

What happened to him is an example of what the people at the church in Philadelphia experienced. As you walk through his experience, you can connect with what they went through. Then when you hear what Jesus is saying to the church in Philadelphia, you get a deeper appreciation for how to apply it to your own life. We understand how they experienced it, then we understand how Jesus speaks to them, and then we can translate that to how Jesus is speaking to us.

This letter was written to Philadelphia two thousand years ago, but it's also written for us. Every single one of the seven letters ends with the same instruction: "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches." In other words, the Holy Spirit is speaking to us as a church today.

### Jesus Introduces Himself

Verse 7: "To the angel of the church in Philadelphia write, the words of the Holy One, the True One, who has the key of David, who opens and no one will shut, and who shuts and no one opens."

The phrase "it's an open and shut case" gives this sermon its title today: Open and Shut. It's straightforward. It's either open or it's closed. There's no halfway door, and you're going to see how Jesus uses this to draw us to the most critical part of the message.

Jesus introduces himself with images and words and descriptions from the vision in Revelation 1. If you want to catch up, you can go back and listen to those sermons. The very first one told us about the vision that John had of Jesus, and there were elements of that vision which Jesus now raises up here. He's the Holy One. He's the True One. He has the key of David, and when he opens it stays open, and when he shuts no one can open it.

It's critical for us to gain understanding of who is talking to this church. Jesus uniquely is the Holy One. Holy means one of a kind, set apart. God alone is holy. We're told that we should be holy because God is holy, but the kind of holiness that human beings are called to is something we call imputed holiness. It means a holiness that's counted to us even though we don't have it. Because we believed in Jesus, he has forgiven us and he considers us holy. But he is the Holy One. That's a claim to being divine, a claim to being God. The first thing Jesus says is, I'm totally unique, I'm totally set apart. I am the Holy One of God.

### The One True Way

Then he says, I am the one true one. It's an amazing thing to claim that you are true. I can't claim that. Can any of us claim that we're one hundred percent true? None of us can. Jesus can.

He says in John 14, Thomas asks him, "Lord, we don't know where you're going. How can we know the way?" Jesus had just told them, "You know the way to where I'm going." Thomas had to say, "I don't get it." I always wanted Thomas in a congregation because he would say the things I'm too embarrassed to say. He would just blurt out, "I don't understand this." I love having you here, every Thomas in this room. Please don't ever stop doing what you do.

Jesus said to him, "I am the way and the truth and the life, and no one comes to the Father except through me. If you had known me, you would have known my Father also. From now on, you do know him and have seen him."

Together with that introduction, we understand that this greeting of Jesus is: I am God Almighty, the one true God. We struggle to understand the nature of God. He's one God and he's three persons—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Every analogy that tries to help us understand that basically falls short. The beauty of knowing that God is one God in three persons is something that we've got to accept, not comprehend. He's the creator, we are created. We're not going to understand everything about him.

What's important in this message is that you understand it's critical to accept Jesus for who he is. If you say, "Jesus is just a prophet," are you accepting him? No, you're not accepting him for who he says he is, because he's not just a prophet. If I say he's just a good man that taught some wonderful things, am I accepting Jesus? No, I'm not. If I don't believe in him for who he actually is, do I believe in Jesus? It's critical for what happens.

Jesus makes this claim: "I am the truth. I am the way—the way. How many ways? The way. And I am the life." Pair that with this introduction of the one who is holy, the one who is true, and the one who has the key of David, who opens and shuts. What he's saying is: I am the one way to God. I'll open the door to God for you to come in by faith in me, or I'll close the door to God because you have rejected me and not believed in me for who I am. It's one way.

### The Pressure in Philadelphia

This is something that the people in the church in Philadelphia would have really battled with. Let me tell you what kind of pressure they were under.

So verse 8: "I know your works. Behold, I've set before you an open door which no one can shut. I know that you have but little power, and yet have kept my word and have not denied my name."

He says to this faithful church, you've kept my word, you've not denied my name, and so I'll keep the door open for you, and no one will shut it. Why is that important to tell this place? There's a little bit of support that comes in Revelation 4:1. John says, "After this I looked and behold a door standing open in heaven, and the first voice which I had heard speaking to me like a trumpet said, 'Come up here and I will show you what must soon take place.'" That's the door that Jesus can open. There are interpretations of this open door—opening opportunities for ministry and all sorts of things. What I'm going to focus on today is this welcome, this opening, this saying, come to me, come to the Father by faith in me.

The next thing in verse 8 is he says, I know you don't have a lot of power, but that's actually good. It's actually to your benefit. Jesus tells us in 2 Corinthians that his power is made perfect in our weakness. We get to rely on him more when we don't have our own strength.

He also tells us in John 14:23, "If anybody loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him." This relationship stems from faith in Jesus, and the ability to keep his word by holding onto that faith—even in our own weakness, he gives us the strength to do that. Our trust in him is admitting our own weakness so that we can appreciate and hold onto his strength.

So you see where we've got to here? He is saying, well done. I've opened the door for you, and no one will shut it, because you've not denied my name, you've kept my word.

### The Synagogue and Persecution

Verse 9 tells us: "Behold, I'll make those of the synagogue of Satan who say they are Jews and are not, but lie. Behold, I will make them come and bow down before your feet, and they will learn that I have loved you."

There were synagogues—at least a synagogue in Philadelphia. There was a Jewish community, a Jewish faith community. Probably most of the Christians in Philadelphia were Jewish Christians. They had accepted that Jesus is the Messiah without rejecting their other teaching, without abandoning everything else. But that caused a problem for them.

This is where the story of an anonymous man from John 9 becomes crucial. When you can see what happens to this man in John 9, you can see that this was the reality for the Christians—particularly the Jewish Christians—in Philadelphia. You put the two together and they illuminate each other.

### The Blind Man's Healing

There's this guy who sits begging because he's blind. One day Jesus comes past and heals him and restores his sight. He doesn't restore his sight by coincidence. He gives him sight. Just stop a little bit and think about that. Think about how profound that is.

Think about what you go through as a human being. If you have never ever seen anything in your life before, and you hear this voice, it tells you to do something really strange, but you do it. When you do it and when you're obedient, you see for the first time in your life. What do you even do with visual information if you've never got it before? What's that conversation going to be like? How do you even have a conversation with them without them spending the entire conversation just looking at everything, appreciating color, looking at the faces they've grown to love but never seen? Just imagine the space that this man is in.

### The Religious Authorities' Response

Of course, there are enemies who have decided that Jesus is not the Messiah, and they decide to persecute him. They decide to persecute this man because they don't believe that Jesus is the Messiah. They're angry. They call this man in—all these top religious leaders. Head of the church, holy people. People with special titles and special dress, and people treat them specially because they're holy people. But they're not. People so proud of their Jewish heritage, so proud of their connection to Moses and the prophets. Proud Jews. But actually they're not, and we can see they're not because of this: they don't actually believe that Jesus has healed a man born blind.

So they call his parents and they grill his parents. The poor parents are terrified. They answer in John 9:20-21: "We know that this is our son and that he was born blind. But how he has come to see, we don't know. Nor do we know who opened his eyes. Ask him. He is of age. He'll speak for himself." His parents said these things because they feared the Jews. For the Jews had already agreed that if anyone should confess Jesus to be Christ, he was to be put out of the synagogue.

That didn't mean someone would walk up to you and say, "Please, you know, leave the church." It's not that simple. It's: Please leave the church, and you may never come back. You never can have anything to do with any of the members of this church. You are out. I have a Jewish friend that converted to become a Christian. His parents held a funeral for him because that son of theirs was now dead. This is what happened in the very earliest Jewish communities who had rejected Christ. Some accepted him, but some like these rejected Jesus to such a degree that they would effectively execute a person by making them not exist anymore. You couldn't be part of that community anymore.

But it's worse than that. In Roman times, you had to worship Caesar. You had to declare that Caesar was Lord and worship him, and you had to denounce Jesus, else you could be executed, you could be put in the Colosseum, you could be ripped apart by gladiators. If you were Jewish, you were given exemption from that because the Romans were so tired of the brutal way in which the Jews would rebel if you touched their faith that they stopped touching it. They said, if you're Jewish, you don't have to worship Caesar, but if you're not Jewish, you have to.

So you had this protection if you were considered Jewish. If you were thrown out of the synagogue, you lost that protection. It's quite reasonable to assume that the Romans would come and say, "Is this person Jewish?" They claim to be Jewish and are therefore exempt from all of this. Are they? It's quite reasonable to assume that the synagogue, or those in charge of the synagogue, could then say, "What is his name?" And then say, "That one we've thrown out. That one follows this other cult."

So these poor people were being persecuted by their own nation, plus by the Roman nation, and all they had to do to avoid that persecution is deny knowing Jesus. They could have said, "Well, Moses—which these guys reminded us when they said, 'You are his disciple, but we are disciples of Moses. We know that God has spoken to Moses, but as for this man, we don't know where he comes from.'"

They could have said, "Look, I believe in Jesus, but I just believe there's one way to God. Lots of people find different ways to God. I can still go to the synagogue. I can still say I don't follow Jesus as the Messiah. I can follow Moses's teachings because it doesn't really matter. I can still get to God because Jesus is not the only way to God." They would have had that escape clause, that excuse. As long as they could stay in the synagogue, they kept that protection. They could still trade, still be part of their family, still stay in their community. All they had to say is a little lie, and they could be forgiven for that because it doesn't really matter.

Can you see how that could have been a temptation?

On the other side of the temptation, they could have said, "Look, I have to say Caesar is Lord, so what? He's a Lord and a Saviour, that's fine. Because all religions basically lead to God anyway, why am I losing my life? Why am I wrecking everything for this name of Jesus?"

But we've already heard that Jesus said, "I am the truth, the way, and the life." If you reject Jesus in favor of any other messiah, you reject him completely, and he is the only way to the Father. And that's what these incredibly faithful people did. They would not give up.

### The Man's Bold Confession

Just like this man, after the authorities grilled him over and over again, after they grilled his parents over and over again, they bring him back in again. This is what he says to them. This is an amazing thing:

"You don't know where he comes from, and yet he opened my eyes. We know that God doesn't listen to sinners, but if anyone is a worshiper of God and does his will, God listens to him. Never since the world began has it even been heard that somebody opened the eyes of a man born blind. If this man were not from God, he could do nothing."

And they answered him, "You were born in utter sin, and you teach us." And they cast him out. They excommunicated him. They cut him off. No more community. No more family. No more protection. He now exists on his own for the rest of his life. First day being able to see. Instead of going to him and marveling and saying, "How did that happen? Who is this Jesus that gave you sight?"—Get out. We want nothing to do with you. You're dead to us. Cut off. Gone. That name of him now is a name associated with expulsion, rejection, and not belonging.

I love this. Don't you love the attitude in him though? He speaks up. He's had one interaction with Jesus, and he stands up for Jesus more than any of his twelve disciples did when Jesus was arrested. Even though it cost him everything.

### Jesus Finds and Welcomes Him

Verse 35 of John 9 is what I would consider the highlight of that passage: "Jesus heard that they had cast him out, and having found him, what does that tell you? Jesus searched him out and found him. Having found him, he said, 'Do you believe in the Son of Man?' He answered, 'Who is he, sir, that I may believe in him?' Jesus said to him, 'You have seen him, and it's he who's speaking to you.' He said, 'Lord, I believe.' And he worshiped him."

Something massive has just happened here. Jesus found him, introduced himself, the opener of doors to the blind man, and welcomed him through the door in heaven, which no one can ever shut. Just like that. One day with new eyes, and he gets thrown out by those who call themselves Jews and are not, and welcomed into the kingdom that will never ever end, and one from which no one can ever exclude him. One from which no one can ever cast him out.

### The Promise of Protection and Belonging

Let's go back to the letter to the Philadelphians. Verse 10: "Because you have kept my word about patient endurance, I will keep you from the hour of trial that is coming on the whole earth to try those who dwell on the earth."

This is a slight change in topic, but I believe it's important to stress this. This is one of the strongest verses that support not only the rapture of the church, but a rapture before the wrath of God gets poured out on the world during tribulation. It is a promise that says, if this church will hold on and keep my word and not deny my name and patiently endure, I will keep you from the trial that's coming on the whole earth.

Remember, at the end of this letter it says, "If you have an ear, listen to what the Spirit says to the churches." This is not specifically just for Philadelphians. This is for all of us who hold on to our confession of Jesus.

By saying "the hour," it limits it to an actual period of suffering. We find out in Daniel and later in Revelation that it's a seven-year period. He says, "I will keep you from that hour of trial." Some believe that means that during that tribulation the church will still be here, but God will supernaturally protect them. You're welcome to hold that. But the particular grammar used here is, I will keep you from it, not in it, not during it. So it's a very strong argument that when this great tribulation comes to try the whole earth, which we read about in Revelation, in Thessalonians, in Daniel, everywhere else—listen to what Daniel says in chapter 12: "At that time shall arise Michael the great prince who has charge of your people, being Israel, and there will be a time of great trouble, such as never has been since there was a nation till that time. But at that time your people shall be delivered, everyone whose name shall be found written in the book."

An amazing promise. You're written in that book, you belong to Jesus, you hold on to your faith, and you are kept from that terrible trial. That doesn't mean that we're not going to suffer. It doesn't mean that the church can say, "Well, that's great. Jesus is going to keep us from all suffering." No. Jesus said, "In this world you will have tribulation, but take heart. I've overcome this world." What we are guaranteed to never have to face is the wrath of God.

How do we know that we will never face the wrath of God? Romans 5:9: "Since therefore we've been justified by his blood, much more shall we be saved by him from the wrath of God." Suffering can happen to us. Death can happen to us. But God pouring out his wrath on us like is documented so clearly in the book of Revelation—no, that's not for Christians.

Might there be Christians in that time of tribulation who have not been saved yet, who have to go through it and are nevertheless saved? Revelation makes that clear. We hear one of the elders in Revelation 7:13: "One of the elders addressed me saying, 'Who are these clothed in white robes and from where have they come?' I said to him, 'Sir, you know,' and he said to me, 'These are the ones coming out of the great tribulation. They have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.'"

### Holding Fast to Your Crown

Verse 11: "I'm coming soon. Hold fast what you have, so that no one may seize your crown."

This is a difficult verse. Why? Because we've already been told that if you're a Christian, your name was written in the book of life from the beginning of the foundation of the world. God chose you. It's nothing you did. You didn't choose him. He chose you. We believe that you're saved by faith, by grace alone. Jesus said, "No one comes to me unless the Father first draws them." This raises this big debate: Can you lose your salvation if you really are chosen?

There are different ways of seeing and understanding this, but there are two things we can't get away from. One is what it says in 1 Corinthians 15: "I'd remind you brothers of the gospel I preached to you, which you received, in which you stand, and by which you're being saved, if you hold fast to the word I preached to you, unless you believed in vain."

And this very verse: "Hold fast to what you have, so that no one may seize your crown."

The way that we resolve this tension is with two things. Left to myself, I'm like the Apostle Peter. Jesus says to him, "Peter, I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail. When you have turned, strengthen your brothers." In other words, without Jesus intervening and holding Peter's faith together, he would have lost it. But because Jesus prayed—"Peter, I've prayed that your faith may not fail"—Jesus can say, not if you turn, but when you turn, strengthen your brothers.

It's in that beautiful interaction with Peter that we can hold these two truths. One is: Be terrified of your own frailty, weakness, and sin. Be terrified of the fact that you will abandon your faith given the slightest pressure because you're human. Hold on to that fear and guard against that. But as you do so, don't ever let go of the peace that comes from knowing that you've been chosen. That your faith is guarded by Jesus Christ, that he is praying for you and interceding for you, that your faith may not fail, and that he doesn't fail.

That keeps us in a beautiful tension of knowing it's not us. We can't be complacent. We can't rely on ourselves. But at the same time have this peace of knowing God's got me by the gospel, by faith in Jesus Christ. I'm going to hold on to that with everything I've got, and I'm going to be careful that I don't behave in a way that jeopardizes my faith. I'm not going to do things and be tempted by things that are going to put me in a space where my faith becomes endangered. I have to hold on to my faith. I have to hold on to Jesus. I have to guard it with everything I've got. But understand at the same time that it's him who holds me. It's him who called me. It's him who chose me. And he will never abandon me and never let me go.

Look at scriptures like: "I hold you in my hand and nobody can snatch you from my hand." Isn't that amazing? That's the promise he's giving to these people. He's saying hold on tight to your faith so that nobody can snatch it from you.

### A Pillar in the Temple

Verse 12: "To the one who conquers, I'll make him a pillar in the temple of my God. Never shall he go out of it. I'll write on him the name of my God, the name of the city of my God, the new Jerusalem, which comes down from my God out of heaven, and my own new name."

This is where everything ties together with the introduction and where we get to close this in this magnificent conclusion. I will make you a pillar in the temple of my God. You got thrown out of the synagogue? Well, come into the temple and I'll make you a pillar. A pillar cannot ever leave the temple. It's what makes the temple. Yes, you've been thrown out by people who say they're Jews and they're not, but you've been welcomed in by the Messiah, by the one who opens that no one can shut. And it says, you will never go out of it. Never.

What incredible comfort that must have given a guy like our friend, the blind man. To have Jesus come to him and say, "Do you believe in the Messiah?" He says, "Yes, Lord. If it's you, I believe, and I worship." And Jesus says, "Here, the door is open for you. No one can shut it. And once you're in, no one can trust you out. Not now, not ever. And in the meantime, as you try and work your way around a society that's rejected you and rejected your name, I'll be with you. I'll help you. I'll comfort you, and I'll keep your faith."

I will write on him the name of my God. The name of my God, that's Jesus's Father. The Jews were known, if we take Simon as an example, Simon Peter. He was known as Peter, son of Jonah. Simon Bar Jonah. Peter Bar Jonah. How you identified in society is whose son you were. If you look at the genealogies of who belongs in Israel and who belongs in the priestly line, you will see: the son of so and so, the son of so and so, the son of so and so. Your identity came from who your father was. And Jesus is saying, you lost your identity. You've been kicked out of your family. Well, you're in a new family because now the name of my Father is on you.

Very often, if you take Judas Iscariot, for example, his name quite likely means Judas of Kirioth, of the town where he comes from. You identified by the town you come from. Well, in the new way of things, when Jesus has opened the door for you, he will write on you the name of the city of his God, the new Jerusalem. You have the name of the Father on you. You have the name of the new city of Jerusalem, where you come from, where you belong. And if that's not enough, my own new name. The name that Jesus gives us. You lost all that stuff? You lost nothing. Welcome. This is who you are now. You're a son of the living God. You're a daughter of the living God. You belong in heaven, in the heavenly city of Jerusalem, the new Jerusalem, and the new creation.

### The New Jerusalem

Revelation 21:22 reveals the ultimate fulfillment: "I saw no temple in the city for its temple is the Lord God, the Almighty, and the Lamb. And the city has no need for sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb. By its light will the nations walk, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it, and its gates will never be shut by day, and there will be no night there. They will bring into it the glory and the honor of the nations, but nothing unclean will ever enter it, nor anyone who does what is detestable or false, but only those whose names are written in the Lamb's Book of Life."

How wonderful is that? Named, chosen, protected, welcomed in, kept in, kept protected eternally. I think that blind guy must have suffered terribly. I don't know how much longer he lived, but I can tell you one thing by the word of God that we see here: He is still a temple. He's still in the temple. He's still a pillar in the temple of his God. He still bears the name of his Father, of Jesus, and it'll never change.

### Application for Us Today

How does it apply to us here and now? I'm ashamed to say that for a lot of my Christian life, I would have professed Jesus, and I would have said that I believe Jesus saves me. But if you pushed me and said, "Well, what about this person who doesn't believe in Jesus? What about them?" I would have struggled to say they have no hope, because Jesus is the way, the truth, and the life.

The day I was able to make that declaration, the day I was able to confess that with peace, trusting that God will do what's right, God will give every person the right opportunity, God will be merciful and just to everybody—I have the peace of saying, "Without Jesus you have no eternal life."

I think some of you will struggle with that just like I did. And the challenge of today's scripture is you have to make a choice. The whole message to the people of Philadelphia is you cannot hold that and belong to me. You're going to be sold out or you're not at all. You can't be a little bit of Caesar, a little bit of Moses, a little bit of this, a little bit of that. You cannot be that. You have to be convicted that it's Jesus or it's not.

I pray for you today that you have that same journey that I've had—to be able to do that, to be able to confess that, to have peace with that and know it's good and it's right.

The next thing is that you have to hold on to who he is. It's no use saying he's a good man, a prophet, a teacher. He is the Son of God, and God is Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, one God. Without that confession, you don't have Jesus. He's the only one. He is who he says he is.

And finally, to hold on, we've got to say, I believe that what you teach is the truth. I'm going to struggle to obey it. We all are. But the moment I look at Jesus teaching and say I don't believe that, I don't agree with that—Jesus defines marriage, I define marriage a different way; Jesus defines eternal life differently, confession of sin differently—the moment I reject any of Jesus's teaching, am I really accepting him?

### Closing Prayer

When we come to the table today, we get to do something amazing. It's an incredible opportunity to make this so clear. If you're going to eat this bread, if you're going to drink this wine, you're saying three things:

Lord Jesus, I believe you are the way, the truth, and the life, the only way. That's why I'm coming to you and I'm not going to anybody else. I'm not calling on another savior. I'm not holding some hope for some other way to know God.

Second, I believe that Jesus Christ is God, that he died for me, and that he has forgiven me just because of my faith in him.

Third, you are right. What you teach is true, and I trust in you. Because if we hold on to that, he opens this door that nobody can shut, not ever. And that's very comforting for us. But it's also comforting to remember something else: Those that you've lost, those that have gone before you, who have held that confession—the door is open for them. They are there waiting. And no one can take them out of it.
