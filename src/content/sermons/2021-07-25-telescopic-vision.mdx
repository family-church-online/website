# ── PRESENTATION COPY ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
tagLine: "Faith strong enough to see what's coming when you can't yet see what's here."
shortDescription: "Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob died without seeing what God promised them — yet they never stopped believing. What does it take to live with that kind of confidence when the promises you're waiting for still feel impossibly far away? And are you living like a pilgrim passing through, or have you settled in a place that was never meant to be home?"
subtitle: The patriarchs all died in faith, not having received the promises, but seeing them and greeting them from afar — acknowledging they were strangers and exiles on the earth, seeking a homeland.
hook: "How do you keep believing in something you've been promised when you can't yet see it? Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob lived their entire lives with this tension — seeing the promise from afar, greeting it as certain, yet never holding it in their hands. What gave them that kind of telescopic vision, and how do you develop it when the wait stretches long and your current circumstances demand you settle in?"

# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "True faith is telescopic — it sees what's promised from afar, greets it as certain, and refuses to settle in places that were never meant to be home."
keyScriptureRef: "Hebrews 11:13-16 ESV"
keyScriptureText: These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar, and having acknowledged that they were strangers and exiles on the earth. For people who speak thus make it clear that they are seeking a homeland. If they had been thinking of that land from which they had gone out, they would have had opportunity to return. But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for he has prepared for them a city.

mainPoints:
  - title: "Faith sees what's promised before it arrives."
    body: Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob all died before Israel became a great nation, yet they greeted the promise as certain — living as though the future was already secured.
  - title: Faith requires living like a pilgrim.
    body: The patriarchs acknowledged they were strangers and exiles, refusing to adopt the practices and values of the land they were passing through, signaling they were headed somewhere better.
  - title: Faith moves forward, not backward.
    body: If they had looked back to where they came from, they could have returned — but instead they fixed their gaze on the city God was preparing for them.

keyIllustration: "The Israelites arriving at the Jordan River — flooding, thirty meters wide, three meters deep, with a powerful current. From afar they could see the Promised Land, but immediately in front of them was the river. God didn't stop it first; He told them to step into the water and then He would act. Only when the priests put their feet into the flooding river did the waters stop. It was their belief in what they could see from afar that gave them the courage to step into the danger immediately in front of them."

application:
  - "Examine where you've stopped sojourning and started settling — in a season, a comfort, a relationship, a mindset — and ask whether you've adopted the practices of a place you were only meant to pass through."
  - "When you're waiting for something God has promised, live as though it's already yours — not presumptively, but with the confidence that greets the future as certain even when it's still distant."
  - "If you focus on where you've been, you stand in danger of returning there; faith moves forward, fixing its gaze on the city God is preparing."

toRemember: God is not ashamed to be called the God of those who live like pilgrims — because He has prepared a city for them.
