### The Universal Longing for Peace

[00:00] Thank you so much, Kim and Bryn. How many of you in the last week woke up at some time in the night or the early hours of the morning feeling restless or troubled? A recent study by the University of Duke examined what adults desire most—a wish list, if you will. Peace was the top thing among sixty-seven percent of the people who took part in the survey. Not wealth, not power, peace.

[00:44] In a way, that is surprising, and in a way, it's completely understandable. It's surprising because peace is what everybody desires, but it's not what everybody seeks out. It's not the way that we live our lives. We're not searching for that peace, certainly not in the right ways. But it's also not surprising, because we are living in dark days.

[01:06] As we've been going through over the last few weeks, we are engaged in a spiritual warfare, and peace at this time is very hard to come by. For most of us, when we are engaged in these battles of spiritual warfare, we wish that we could get through the circumstances in the battle so that we can then experience peace. It's the natural way of humankind. But I wanted to share a story with you that sets the scene for this morning's scriptures.

### Nicholas Ridley's Peace Before Battle

[01:40] It's about a man by the name of Nicholas Ridley. He lived in the fifteen hundreds. He was one of the English reformists, and he was resolute in his belief in our Lord Jesus Christ. He stood firm. He wanted his will to be done on earth. And he lived a good life. He challenged many false doctrines.

[01:58] But his end came when, very much like John the Baptist in the Bible, he opposed the appointment of the monarch at that time, fiercely, because it was ungodly. And this battle went on for a while, and eventually the false monarch was appointed, and he was accused of treason and sentenced to death. But not just any death. In those days, it was being burnt at the stake. That was his sentence.

[02:26] And so in fifteen fifty-five, Nicholas Ridley, on the eve of his execution, he was awaiting his trial in prison. And you can imagine what he must have been going through knowing the battle he was going to have to face the next day. And in love, his brother came to him that night, and he said to him, Brother, I'm going to spend the night with you and help you in your angst and to be with you at this time.

[02:49] To which Nicholas responded, Please don't. I plan to sleep as peacefully as ever, Lord willing. He had faith in his Lord. That's where his peace came from, and he didn't mind what he had to go through. He had peace before the battle had even begun.

[03:17] And so today, when we're looking at the armor of God, we're going to be focusing on the sandals of peace that commonly become known as the shoes of peace, what Paul teaches us about the shoes of peace.

In the ESV, it says, Stand therefore, having fastened on the belt of truth, and having put on the breastplate of righteousness, and as shoes for your feet, having put on the readiness given by the gospel of peace.

### The Ephesian Spiritual Battlefield

[05:25] We've been working through this book of Ephesians for quite some time now, and I think this is a good time to take a look back and understand exactly what it was that these Ephesians were going through at the time that Paul wrote this letter. What was the spiritual warfare that they were facing that required them to have this armor of God?

There were so many things that were going on at that time that caused tremendous spiritual warfare. For starters, remember, they had the temple of Artemis in the center of Ephesus, in this thriving metropolis in its time. And people and travelers came from around the world to see this temple and to take part in the rituals of fertility and hunting. They used to have these annual festivals. They used to have these temple prostitutes, and at these festivals, they'd have these huge parties and these drunken orgies. That was the focal point of Ephesus at the time.

[05:58] Along with that, people were practicing all sorts of magic and sorcery. Remember, in Acts nineteen, we see that they burned books to the value of five thousand silver coins. These were books containing charms and spells and curses to manipulate the spiritual forces into getting what they wanted. Power, love, wealth, possessions, status.

[06:32] And on top of this, we have what's referred to as the mystery religions. Promises of immortality and enlightenment. There were the cults of at least Isis, Mithras, Sybil, and Dionysus at the time. These mystery religions included rituals that included self-harm, sexual orgies, and even, in some cases, human sacrifice.

[07:07] So the church of Ephesus was this melting pot of all these cultures. And whether they were practicing in any of these things or not, in some way or another, all the Ephesians were involved and caught up in this battle, either themselves personally through the things that they'd done, or through their families and their friends and their loved ones. And when you start meddling in these things, peace becomes a major problem.

[07:59] And so Paul, in this section of the letter today, he turns and he focuses on peace, and how we need to deal with peace while we're at war. And so the instrument of peace, the equipment of peace, in this case, has become known as the sandals of peace.

### The Sandals of Peace: Design and Purpose

[07:59] In those days, the sandals were quite an innovation. They came about, it's estimated, at the time of Alexander the Great. They were leather sandals that you'd fasten to your feet. The soles were really thick leather, and the straps would come quite high up to around about your knee. And at the bottom of these sandals were these studs. They were like spikes at the bottom of the shoes, at the bottom of these sandals.

[08:20] And the studs served two purposes. First of all, often when they went into battle, on the battlefield, the enemy would put spikes and shrapnel and all sorts of nasties for the enemy to stand on. We don't often think about our feet when we're in warfare, but if you stand on something and you disable your foot, you're out of action. So the studs were there to defend their feet, to protect their feet from whatever was on the battlefield. That was the first purpose.

[08:45] And the second purpose is these studs gave the sandals this tremendous grip. Remember, in those days, combat was hand to hand. They fought with shields and spears and swords and maces, so with the studs on their feet, they could stand firm, particularly in their defense when defending blows from the enemy.

[09:14] The other great innovation that came from these sandals at the time was the mobility. Despite the fact that they were so good for defense, they were light. It changed the art of warfare in that time. Armies could move, they could mobilize, they could change positions quickly because they weren't wearing these heavy boots, and they could traverse terrain that they didn't have to traverse before. So it gave them stability and mobility. Those are the two spiritual elements that is being alluded to in this scripture.

### Standing Fast in the Truth

When you read the works of many authors and commentary, commentators and Bible scholars, when they talk about donning the shoes of peace, they talk about being mobile, the mobility portion of the sandals. Now there is the mobility aspect, but a lot of teachers and commentators say that, and pastors indeed, they say that this mobility is so that we can go out and spread the gospel, the gospel of peace. Let it be known to the world. And indeed, that is a biblical principle. We must do that. We are commanded to do that.

[10:47] But in my studies, I've come to agree with the thinking of Martin Lloyd Jones. When you look at the scripture, all of the armor of God is used in defense. It's to defend ourselves from the attacks of the enemy. In this case, there's no time to be going out and spreading the gospel. That's an offensive. The armor of God is defensive.

[11:07] So, first and foremost, the sandals are talking about standing fast, standing firm. It's that stability that the studs of the shoes gave. The idea of the mobility, it comes from Isaiah fifty-two, verse seven. The mobility to go out and spread the gospel of peace comes from Isaiah fifty-two, verse seven. And it's repeated in Romans ten, verse fifteen.

[11:34] But in Isaiah fifty-two, verse seven, it says, How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of whom brings good news, who publishes peace, who brings good news of happiness, who publishes salvation, who says to Zion, Your God reigns. So that's where the idea of spreading the gospel and using the mobility to take out the gospel of peace comes from. But in this context, I believe it's all about defense, standing firm, and having a firm footing.

[12:21] We have a God who loves us immensely. Despite our sinful natures and our inclination to sin, he loves us and he wants to be with us. He wants to be in fellowship with us. He made a way back to us, back to him, by sending his Son to Earth to die for our sins, to make that bridge so that we can be right with him. That is a remarkable truth. That is something to stand fast in. We must stand fast in that truth.

[12:42] What do you believe? Where do you stand when it comes to the Bible? Do you believe that every word, every verse, every chapter, every book is inerrant, infallible, is the spoken word of God? In the world today, there are so many gray areas. You can be one thing today and another thing tomorrow. You can support one team today, and tomorrow support another one. And this kind of behavior in the world today is praised. It's respected.

[13:42] But it's not like this in the Bible. It's not like this when it comes to our faith and our Christian journey. We have to stand fast in what we believe. There's that old adage, If you don't stand for something, you will fall for anything.

### The World's Shifting Values

And we were having a chat, and she mentioned, in passing, her friends who she said were non-binary. [15:53] Sometimes, the old ways are easier. It's at those times we have to say no. We have to have that stability. We have to stand fast. No, we are with God now. That's what is meant by the stability.

### The Enemy's Constant Tactics

[16:13] The other spiritual aspect represented by the sandals is the mobility. One Peter five, verse eight reads, Be sober-minded. Be watchful. Your adversary, the devil, prowls around like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour. We read in the scripture, all over scripture, that Satan is cunning.

[16:43] In Genesis three, verse one, we read that the serpent was more crafty and cunning than any other creature created. In Revelation twelve, verse nine, Satan is called the great deceiver of the world. Satan is constantly changing his strategy and his tactics to get us involved in sin, to ensnare us, to trap us, to separate us from God. And he does not stop.

And still, later in the week, he'll masquerade himself in clothes of righteousness and peace. He's constantly changing tactics to ensnare us.

[17:46] We need to have the mobility, those light sandals, so that we can move and adapt to the attacks, so that we can parry, we can dodge, we can get ready for their counter-attack. That's the mobility that comes from the sandals. We need to be able to adjust on the fly. The sandals represent this mobility.

[18:14] Heavy boots, on the other hand, are a killer. On the battlefield, if you're wearing these heavy boots and your enemy are in these light shoes, you can't move to keep up with the enemy. They're going to encircle you. They're going to flank you. It's the same in our Christian faith.
