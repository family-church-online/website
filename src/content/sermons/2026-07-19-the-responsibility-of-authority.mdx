# The responsibility of authority

### The Vision of Jesus in Revelation

This is the revelation of Jesus Christ. It's showing us not only who Jesus is, but what he's done, and it starts with Jesus saying to John, who's on this desert island, that he's a prisoner there, suffering there, and the Lord gives him this amazing vision of Jesus revealing himself to John and says to John, Show what you see. Write what you see to all the churches, and he puts it in a very specific way. He says, Write what you have seen, what is, and what is to come, and we're traveling through Revelation on the framework that that provides us. There's three things we're looking at: what John saw, what Jesus says is, and what Jesus says is going to be happening.

The vision that he saw is what he writes about first. He writes about this vision of Jesus. Let me remind you a little bit about it from Revelation one. The hairs of his head were white, like white wool, like snow. His eyes were like a flame of fire. His feet, like burnished bronze, refined in a furnace, and his voice was like the roar of many waters. In his right hand he held seven stars. From his mouth came a sharp two-edged sword, and his face was like the sun shining in full strength. When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead, but he laid his right hand on me saying, Fear not. I am the first and the last. So Jesus says, Tell them that you saw me. It's important that whenever you hear whatever revelation's got, you have a mental image of the one who is giving you the revelation, of the one who's being revealed.

So powerful and awe-inspiring was that vision that John, the apostle John, he says he fell like one dead. Have you ever seen something or heard something that makes you fall like one dead? It's not a rhetorical question. I really want to know if anybody here has seen something so awesome that you actually cannot stand anymore and you collapse. Why doesn't that happen to us a whole bunch? Can you see that it's not to be taken lightly?

Imagine being in prayer. Revelation says that John was in the spirit. He was in prayer. He was in meditation. ### God Who Sees All

The key to understanding the vision is in verse twenty-three: All the churches will know that I am he who searches mind and heart and will give to each of you according to your works. The burning eyes are not like idols' eyes. Remember, part of the problem here with these churches was idolatry, because they lived in this culture where everybody around them, and probably them themselves, came from a history of worshiping idols, worshiping the Roman or Greek gods, and going to bow down before statues, and following all sorts of religious rituals that were designed to worship either these idols or even Caesar. That's what they were used to. Many of them were Jewish Christians that had understood the fulfillment of the law in Jesus. They'd heard the gospel, they'd understood it, and they came from this Jewish context of learning.

The prophets used this image of idols that were unable to see, even though they had eyes, unable to hear, even though they had ears, unable to move even though they had feet, because they were stone statues, or metal statues, or wooden statues. Very often in scripture, this contrast is drawn between people who can see, between an idol who cannot see and God who sees, between people who worship an idol and therefore can't see because they become like what they worship, and people who worship the living God who can see and therefore can see like he does because they're becoming what they worship.

When Jesus says, I have eyes that burn like fire, he's not only saying, I can see unlike the idols that you worship, but he's saying, I can see right through you. I can see right into the depths of your heart. I am he who searches mind and heart. Often, when we have ministry to the kids here at school, and we talk about the fact that God sees you every moment of the day, the first thing they do is get very uncomfortable, because I can't hide. God's going to see all the naughty things I do. I can't break something and hide it. I can't say something ugly and pretend I didn't, because God sees everything, and there becomes, with this knowledge, a fear, but there's a joy in that as well, if you understand who it is that sees everything that you do.

There's a deep joy in knowing that though Jesus sees right through us, into our hearts, into our thoughts, into our minds, he still loves us. That's incredible. You don't have to pretend, you don't have to put on a facade, you don't have to build a great big thing and say, Look, Lord, look how amazing I am, because he sees right through it anyway. When you're afraid, when you're angry, you can come to him and pray, and you can just open up. You can just say how it is, because he knows anyway. And so this is the introduction we get. This is the Jesus that speaks to us.

### The Authority to Judge

Not just the eyes that see right through us, but these feet of burnished bronze. The key here is in verse twenty-three where he says, Not only do I search the heart and mind, but I will give to each of you according to your works. You've got the full knowledge and understanding of the human condition of the church, but also these powerful feet which represent power and authority. The authority to give what you have earned. The authority to judge you.

This makes us uncomfortable when we remember that. This is not just Jesus who looks through you and still loves you, but if you do not accept, in faith, the grace he gives you, and repent and trust in him, also the judgment of this holy and mighty Son of God. Even John the Apostle that went fishing with him, and lay on his shoulder at the last supper, and knew him that well, and was that familiar with him, even that John who fell like somebody dead when he saw Jesus in his glory, that's the one who's going to judge us as well.

Now remember that the Christian judgment is not one of heaven and hell. Have you believed in Jesus? Do you trust in him as your savior? Do you believe he's the Son of God? That he died for you? That he was raised to life for you? That he took your punishment on his shoulders? Do you believe that? Then you will never be judged one way or the other, heaven and hell, because you have already gone through judgment, because you've already believed, because Jesus has already taken your punishment for you. You don't have to fear that judgment, as long as you hold onto that faith.

But Jesus makes it very clear that even us as believers will sit before him, and he will give to us what we have earned with our decisions, with our attitudes, with our actions, with our words. We will be held accountable to him, and he will give to each of us what we have earned. Not the wrath of God, because he's taken all of that. But some of us will be greatly rewarded, and others not so rewarded. By faith in Jesus Christ, you've got eternal life, but that doesn't mean that everybody has the same level of reward for eternity. It's made very clear, and this passage particularly, talking to the church, emphasizes that. It says, I will give to some of you this, I will give some of you that, everything as you have deserved it, because I see right through you. I know.

### Discernment Versus Judgment

Ephesus had this incredible fight against false prophets, but that fight is missing in Thyatira, and it's fascinating that Ephesus had a problem of their love going cold because of the false prophets they were actively opposing and confronting, whereas Thyatira wasn't opposing, wasn't confronting them, was able to keep that love, but they were missing the discernment. They were missing the judging that has to happen.

How many times have you heard that Christians are judgmental? Have you heard that? We can be. How many times have you heard it preached, don't judge? It is biblical, because Jesus says to us, Why do you judge one another? The way that you judge each other will be used to judge you. First, take the log out of your own eye, so that you can see the speck in your neighbor's eye. When you've taken the log out of your own eye, you'll see clearly, so you'll be able to take the speck out of your neighbor's eye.

Does that tell you not to discern, not to judge at all? I find it interesting that we forget that part. It's very easy for us to forget that part. Jesus says, first take the speck out of your own eye, then you'll see better and you'll be able to help your neighbor to get the log out, or the speck out of your neighbor's eye. It doesn't say, Don't help your neighbor to get stuff out of his eye. We fall for that as a church. In love, we don't confront one another and say, listen, you've got a splinter in your eye. Let me help you get it out. Or, we go the other way. We're like, my eyes are perfect. Your eyes, you all got stuff in your eyes. I'm going to fix all of you. Both are wrong. Jesus is going to guide us into the right way of doing this.
