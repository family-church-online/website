# ── PRESENTATION COPY ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
tagLine: "The cross saves you, but what's on the other side changes you completely."
shortDescription: "You've been saved—but have you grown? The law once protected you like a guardian protects a child, but mature faith requires stepping beyond rituals and works into a harder freedom: trusting Christ alone. If you feel stuck, stagnant, or struggling to believe something about God, that tension is the doorway to maturity."
subtitle: Salvation is the beginning, not the destination—maturity means leaving works behind and trusting the sufficiency of the cross.
hook: "Why do so many Christians feel stuck spiritually, cycling through rituals and trying harder instead of growing deeper? Paul shows the Galatians that living under law—even after salvation—keeps you immature, enslaved to systems that promise control but deliver burden. Maturity means facing what the cross truly demands: full surrender, uncomfortable truth, and dependence on grace alone."

# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: Spiritual maturity means transitioning from law-based childhood faith to grace-based adult faith—trusting Christ alone, not rituals, rules, or your own works.
keyScriptureRef: "Galatians 4:1-3 ESV"
keyScriptureText: I mean that the heir, as long as he is a child, is no different from a slave, though he is the owner of everything. But he is under guardians and managers until the date set by his father. In the same way we also, when we were children, were enslaved to the elementary principles of the world.

mainPoints:
  - title: The law is for children, not heirs.
    body: Just as wealthy Roman heirs lived under strict guardianship until they came of age, believers under law are spiritually immature—controlled by rituals and works that promise blessing but deliver bondage.
  - title: "What stops spiritual growth: the flesh and the cross."
    body: "Our pride prefers earning over receiving, and the cross forces us to confront uncomfortable truths—God's sovereignty, the reality of sin, the exclusivity of Christ—that our natural selves resist."
  - title: Maturity requires honest wrestling.
    body: "If something about God or His Word troubles you, don't bury it—pray about it, speak about it with other believers, and let God do the uncomfortable work of changing you."

keyIllustration: "An Australian priest of Muslim background received violent death threats for preaching Christ. When a sixteen-year-old assailant stabbed him fifteen times at the pulpit, the knife miraculously malfunctioned. The next day, from his hospital bed missing an eye, he released a voice message forgiving his attacker and calling him \"my son.\" Years earlier, he had been denied ordination and spent six years deepening his relationship with Christ—moving from law-focused ambition to grace-centered maturity. That season prepared him for this Christ-like response under fire."

application:
  - "Ask God to reveal what's holding you back from deeper faith—pride, fear, unconfessed sin, or theological discomfort—and pray for the courage to face it."
  - "Stop trying to earn God's favour through spiritual performance; rest in the sufficiency of the cross and let your obedience flow from love, not fear."
  - Join a small group or find a trusted believer to talk honestly about your struggles—spiritual growth happens in community, not isolation.

toRemember: "The cross saves you—but what lies on the other side of surrender is where you truly meet God and become who He's called you to be."
