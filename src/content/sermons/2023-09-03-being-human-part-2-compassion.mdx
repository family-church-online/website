# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: Jesus healed the sick and afflicted not to gain followers, but because their suffering—rooted in slavery to sin—caused Him physical, gut-level pain. He calls us to the same costly compassion, even toward our enemies.
keyScriptureRef: "Matthew 9:36 ESV"
keyScriptureText: When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd.

mainPoints:
  - title: Jesus healed to fulfill scripture and prove His identity to the disciples.
    body: His signs and wonders were necessary so the apostles would believe unequivocally and build His church after His death and resurrection.
  - title: Compassion is a physical ache that compels action.
    body: "The Greek word used nine times to describe Jesus' compassion refers to the intestines and womb—a deep pain in the lower abdomen. It always leads to action."
  - title: "The crowds' true affliction was spiritual slavery to sin."
    body: "Physical diseases were symptoms of a deeper problem: they had abandoned the true God, and their shepherds (religious leaders) were obsessed with status instead of leading them to truth."
  - title: We are called to have compassion for all, even enemies of God.
    body: "It's easy to feel compassion for those we love; it's supernatural to feel it for those who mock God or harm us. Yet Christ commands it: \"Love your neighbor as yourself.\""

keyIllustration: "A father watches his young daughter struggle in a school swimming gala after she's unexpectedly entered in a three-lap medley instead of her planned single lap. She can only swim one stroke well, and by the second lap she's exhausted, humiliated, and desperate. The father feels an intense ache in his gut—compassion—and wants to jump in the water or stop the race. Instead, he screams encouragement until she finishes. This gut-wrenching feeling is the same word used to describe Jesus' compassion for the crowds."

application:
  - "When you see people enslaved to sin—whether they're flaunting wealth in nightclubs or mocking God on social media—your natural response is anger. But Christ calls you to gut-level compassion that leads to action: prayer, service, and forgiveness."
  - "Biblical compassion isn't a feeling you wait to have. It's a command. Put on \"compassionate hearts\" (Colossians 3:12) the same way you put on clothes—deliberately, daily, as part of your character."
  - "Remember Christ's ultimate compassion: hanging on the cross, back shredded, He said, \"Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.\" That's the standard—and He gives us His Spirit to make it possible."

toRemember: We have a God who loves us so deeply that it physically hurts Him to watch us suffer—and He calls us to love others with that same costly, gut-wrenching compassion.
