# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "Work is not something to escape — it is a form of worship, and God calls you to take Jesus with you into the workplace, whether you're leading or following."
keyScriptureRef: "Ephesians 6:5-9 ESV"
keyScriptureText: Bondservants, obey your earthly masters with fear and trembling, with a sincere heart, as you would Christ, not by the way of eye-service, as people-pleasers, but as bondservants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart, rendering service with a good will, as to the Lord and not to man, knowing that whatever good anyone does, this he will receive back from the Lord, whether he is a bondservant or is free. Masters, do the same to them, and stop your threatening, knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven, and that there is no partiality with him.

mainPoints:
  - title: Work is hard because God cursed it — to bring you back to Him.
    body: "After the fall, God cursed the ground so that work would involve toil, sweat, and difficulty. This wasn't vindictive punishment — it was redemptive discipline, meant to remind us we need Him."
  - title: "Employees: obey, work conscientiously, and focus on purpose."
    body: "Obedience doesn't mean cowering or enabling abuse. It means working wholeheartedly, with integrity, even when no one's watching — because Jesus is."
  - title: "Employers: treat employees as equals and stop threatening."
    body: You hold a title, not superiority. God sees no partiality between you and those under your authority. Lead with fairness, consistency, and respect — and create space for mistakes, not fear.
  - title: Work for your heavenly reward, not your earthly paycheck.
    body: "If you're grinding for retirement or financial freedom, you'll endure endless suffering. Shift your perspective: you're not clocking in for a salary — you're working for a King."

keyIllustration: "A blind musician played piano daily in a WWI military hospital, never knowing if anyone listened or cared. He played faithfully, tenaciously, without applause or recognition — until the day the King visited and commended him. That's the posture of a Christian employee: working as unto the Lord, not for human approval."

application:
  - Picture Jesus leaving your house with you in the morning. Imagine Him sitting at your desk, in every meeting, watching how you respond to your boss or treat your team.
  - "Stop chasing financial freedom as your salvation. It's the one percent seduction — a lie that traps most of us in endless striving. Instead, ask: how can I glorify God in this job, today?"
  - "If you lead people, remember Philemon and Onesimus. Paul told a master to receive his runaway slave back not as property, but as a brother. That's the standard for Christian leadership."

toRemember: "You're not home yet — so stop living for the applause you get here, and start living for the reward waiting there."
