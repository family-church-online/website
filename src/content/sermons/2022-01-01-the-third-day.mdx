# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "God's consistent pattern throughout Scripture is to bring new life, deliverance, and breakthrough on the third day—not when your circumstances improve, but when you prepare in faith during the waiting."
keyScriptureRef: "1 Corinthians 15:3-5 ESV"
keyScriptureText: "For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that He was buried, that He was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures, and that He appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve."

mainPoints:
  - title: "The \"Third Day\" Pattern Runs Throughout Scripture"
    body: From creation (life appears after chaos on day three) to Abraham (Isaac spared on the third day) to Moses (God descends on Sinai on day three) to Hezekiah (healed on the third day) to Esther (the king welcomes her on day three) to Jonah (released from the fish after three days) to Christ (raised on the third day)—God consistently acts decisively after a period of waiting, testing, or darkness.
  - title: Right Belief Must Lead to Right Behavior
    body: "Knowing God's pattern isn't enough—you must act on it now, during the hardship. Consecrate yourself like Israel at Sinai. Trust God's word like Abraham. Fast and focus like Esther. Repent like Jonah. Don't wait for pain to end before you worship, pray, study Scripture, or serve. Moving in obedience now transforms the waiting from wasted time into preparation."
  - title: The Resurrection Guarantees Your Third Day
    body: "Christ's resurrection is the ultimate third day—the one all others pointed toward. If the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, He will give life to your mortal body. Your storms, your grief, your unanswered prayers are not evidence of God's absence—they are the first two days before He brings new life."

keyIllustration: "A preacher struggling to learn guitar discovered his fingers kept landing in the wrong sequence, creating frustrating delays and buzzing chords. For years he tried different approaches without success. Finally, he realized he needed to visualize the correct finger placement and retrain the muscle memory—not wait until playing felt natural. The breakthrough came when he stopped waiting for ease and started practicing the right way despite the discomfort. Similarly, believers must not wait for circumstances to improve before they obey, worship, and serve—they must retrain their spiritual \"muscle memory\" now, in the hardship, so that joy becomes their constant companion rather than a distant destination."

application:
  - "Identify where you're waiting for circumstances to change before you fully engage with God—then choose one discipline (prayer, Scripture, worship, service) and practice it daily regardless of how you feel."
  - "Memorize the seven key verses referenced in this sermon (Genesis 1:13, Proverbs 3:5-8, 1 Peter 2:9-11, Isaiah 26:3, Hebrews 12:10-11, Romans 8:11, 1 Peter 5:10-11) to anchor your hope in God's pattern of third-day deliverance."
  - "When storms hit—relational conflict, financial pressure, illness, loss—ask, \"What is God preparing in me through this?\" rather than \"Why is this happening to me?\""

toRemember: "You don't wait for the perfect world before you serve God—you serve Him now, in the darkness, so that when the third day comes, you're ready for the new life He brings."
