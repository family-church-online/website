mainPoints:
  - title: Even human covenants cannot be broken once ratified.
    body: "Paul appeals to what the Galatians already understand: a covenant, once sealed, cannot be added to or annulled — and God's covenant is far more binding than any human agreement."
  - title: "God's promise to Abraham pointed to one offspring: Christ."
    body: The Abrahamic covenant was never about many descendants earning their way to God, but about one descendant — Jesus — through whom all nations would be blessed by faith.
  - title: The Law did not replace the promise.
    body: "The Mosaic Law came centuries after Abraham and served to separate Israel from surrounding nations, but it was never the means of salvation — that was always by faith in God's promise."
  - title: The inheritance comes by promise, not performance.
    body: If salvation depended on keeping the Law, the promise would be void — but God gave the inheritance to Abraham as a gift, which is why it requires faith, not works.

keyIllustration: "A Canadian railway company needed land owned by an Indigenous chief named Crowfoot. He treasured the promise. But he never once redeemed it. We do the same with God's promises — we carry them, admire them, but never cash them in."

  - Pray based on what God has already promised — prayer is taking God at His word and asking Him to do what He said.
