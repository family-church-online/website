# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "God's promises stand no matter how impossible the circumstances look — He protected the line of the Messiah through exile, assassination, and chaos, and He will keep every promise He has made to you."
keyScriptureRef: "Ezekiel 34:23-24 ESV"
keyScriptureText: "I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David, and he shall feed them: he shall feed them and be their shepherd. And I, the Lord, will be their God, and my servant David shall be prince among them. I am the Lord; I have spoken."

mainPoints:
  - title: "God promised David an eternal dynasty through Nathan's prophecy."
    body: Six hundred years before Christ, Nathan told David his throne would never end — a promise that looked impossible when Babylon destroyed Jerusalem and scattered the people into exile.
  - title: Ezekiel reaffirmed the promise during the darkest moment.
    body: "Writing from Babylonian captivity, Ezekiel declared God would raise up a Shepherd-King from David's line — using the language of shepherd to emphasize He would serve, not exploit, His people."
  - title: Two assassination campaigns nearly wiped out the line of Christ.
    body: "Athaliah murdered all royal heirs except one infant who was hidden; Jehoiakim was cursed and removed after tearing up God's word — yet God preserved the genealogy unbroken all the way to Jesus."
  - title: Jesus fulfilled the prophecy as the Good Shepherd who lays down His life.
    body: He did not come as a conquering king demanding service, but as the Shepherd who knows His sheep, calls them by name, and willingly dies to protect them from wolves.

keyIllustration: "A diver separated from the group at night discovered he had run out of air. His survival instinct was to panic and thrash toward the surface — which would have killed him from decompression. Instead, he had been trained to exhale slowly, trust his equipment, and ascend gradually. When God's promises seem impossible and every instinct screams to panic, Scripture trains us to trust what He has spoken and let Him bring us through safely."

application:
  - "When your circumstances contradict God's promises, remember: He protected the Messiah's line through exile, assassination, and the fall of Jerusalem. Nothing you face can derail what He has spoken over you."
  - If you are carrying guilt and wondering whether you can be forgiven, bring it to the Good Shepherd now. He laid down His life specifically so you could be freed from what you have done.
  - Jesus is returning as both Shepherd and Judge. Those who trust His word are His sheep — and nothing will ever harm them again.
