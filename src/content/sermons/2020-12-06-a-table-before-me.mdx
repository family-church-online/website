# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: The Great Shepherd of heaven left His throne to become the Lamb of God so that we — helpless sheep — could sit at His table, fed and protected in the presence of our enemies.
keyScriptureRef: "Psalm 23:1-5 ESV"
keyScriptureText: "The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want. He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters. He restores my soul. He leads me in paths of righteousness for His name's sake. Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for You are with me; Your rod and Your staff, they comfort me. You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies; You anoint my head with oil; my cup overflows."

mainPoints:
  - title: The Shepherd became the Lamb.
    body: Jesus, who commands millions of angels in glory, laid down His majesty to be born helpless in flesh — not because He was weak, but because He chose to save us.
  - title: The Shepherd feeds and defends us.
    body: "In Psalm 23, God provides green pastures, still waters, restoration, and protection — all the gospel summarised in four lines: He feeds, cleanses, restores, and leads us in righteousness."
  - title: We feast while our enemies watch.
    body: "At the Lord's table, sin, death, and Satan can only stand by as the Shepherd invites us to eat with Him — vulnerable, yet utterly safe under His rod and staff."
  - title: We respond with humility.
    body: Christ emptied Himself to become a servant; we are called to do the same, knowing that goodness and mercy pursue us — not because of our strength, but because of His.

keyIllustration: "A shepherd on a remote farm bottle-feeds orphaned lambs, each with their own personality — some brave, some timid, some bullies. These fragile creatures depend entirely on the shepherd for food, water, and protection from lions and bears. The shepherd defends them with nothing but a rod and staff, risking his own life. This is the image David uses: the Lord is not a passive caretaker, but a fierce protector who fights for His sheep."

application:
  - "When you come to the Lord's table, remember Him — not just what He did, but who He is: the Shepherd-King who left glory to serve you."
  - When life feels dangerous, remember His rod and staff. Your enemies cannot reach you at His table.
  - "Respond to His humility with your own: serve one another as lambs, knowing the Great Shepherd defends you."

toRemember: The Lamb who was slain is the Shepherd who rules with a rod of iron — and He invites you to sit at His table, safe in His presence, as your cup overflows.
