# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "In a world of artificial intelligence, shifting cultural norms, and media agendas, Scripture alone is God's living, unchangeable authority — and we must saturate ourselves in it daily to discern truth from myth."
keyScriptureRef: "John 17:14-17 ESV"
keyScriptureText: I have given them your word, and the world has hated them because they are not of the world, just as I am not of the world. I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one. They are not of the world just as I am not of the world. Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.

mainPoints:
  - title: We live in an age of exploding information but vanishing objective truth.
    body: "Artificial intelligence can now write essays, diagnose diseases, and construct persuasive arguments from millions of data points — but it cannot tell you what is morally right, and its \"authority\" will be shaped by whoever programs it."
  - title: The world actively reverses good and evil.
    body: Isaiah warned that people would call evil good and good evil. Today, institutions label the nuclear family dangerous, silence prayer in public, and penalise biblical conviction — proving we cannot trust cultural consensus as our moral compass.
  - title: We are not of this world, and Scripture sanctifies us in that truth.
    body: "Jesus does not pray for us to be taken out of the world but to be kept from the evil one and set apart by God's Word. Accepting that we do not belong here frees us to reject pressure and stand on Scripture without fear of rejection."
  - title: Scripture is not human opinion — it is God-breathed and carried along by the Holy Spirit.
    body: "Peter testifies that he was an eyewitness, not a mythmaker. Paul declares all Scripture profitable for teaching and equipping. Jesus Himself prioritised the fulfilment of Scripture over His own life, saying in the garden, \"How then would the scriptures be fulfilled?\""

keyIllustration: A student can now ask ChatGPT to write an entire essay, consulting specific sources and mimicking a particular style — and teachers cannot tell the difference. Soon, essays will be obsolete. Legal research, medical diagnosis, and expert opinion will all be automated. But when those systems become mandatory and shape every decision you make, who controls the morality they enforce? The Tower of Babel attempted to reach heaven without God; artificial intelligence risks the same — building an all-knowing authority that replaces submission to the Creator.

application:
  - "Gather your family around Scripture daily — your children need to learn what is right and wrong from God's Word before the world rewrites it for them."
  - Ask God to give you hunger for His Word and to remove any barrier keeping you from engaging it fully and joyfully.
  - Recognise that standing on biblical truth will cost you socially — and embrace that you do not belong here, so rejection is expected and bearable.

toRemember: "A church that knows God's Word and applies it is a powerful, living church — and in a world drowning in information, Scripture is the only ground that will never shift beneath your feet."
