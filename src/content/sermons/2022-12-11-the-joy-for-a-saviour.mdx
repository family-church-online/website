# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "Jesus came to save His people from sin, death, and Satan—but His work isn't finished. When He returns, He will crush evil, establish justice, and reign over a world finally at peace. Christmas is both celebration and anticipation."
keyScriptureRef: "Psalm 98:1-3 ESV"
keyScriptureText: "Oh sing to the Lord a new song, for he has done marvelous things! His right hand and his holy arm have worked salvation for him. The Lord has made known his salvation; he has revealed his righteousness in the sight of the nations. He has remembered his steadfast love and faithfulness to the house of Israel. All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God."

mainPoints:
  - title: "Jesus' name reveals who He is."
    body: "When the angel told Joseph to name the baby Jesus (\"God saves\"), it was because Jesus Himself would save His people from their sins. The prophecy calling Him \"Immanuel\" (God with us) confirms it: Jesus is God in the flesh."
  - title: Salvation is both spiritual and physical.
    body: "Jesus has already saved you from sin, death, and Satan's accusations. But God also promised to establish peace on earth—to destroy evil governments, end war, and rule from Jerusalem. That promise is still coming."
  - title: God keeps His promises to Israel—and we benefit.
    body: "Salvation came through God's faithfulness to Israel, but the blessing extends to all nations. Jesus fulfills every Old Testament prophecy about the Messiah, both at His first coming and at His return."
  - title: "Satan's defeat is certain."
    body: "From Genesis 3 onward, God promised that the serpent's head would be crushed. Revelation 20 describes that final judgment in one short paragraph—it will happen in an instant, and Satan's destruction is eternal."

keyIllustration: "A species of wasp lays its eggs inside a fig tree. The female wasp enters a tiny tunnel in the fig, loses her wings in the process, pollinates the flowers inside, lays her eggs, and dies. The fig dissolves her body. When the male wasps hatch, they fertilize the females and then chew tunnels to help them escape—but the males never leave. The females collect pollen and fly to a new fig to start the cycle again. The illustration reminds us that the gospel—like pollen—was never meant to stay with us. It's meant to be carried and spread."

application:
  - "Don't just celebrate what Jesus has done—look forward to what He will do. His return will bring justice, peace, and the end of corruption and evil."
  - "Salvation is a free gift received by faith. You don't earn it, but gratitude should compel you to share it with others."
  - "Don't be the five percent who take the gospel and go their own way. Be part of the ninety-five percent who carry the good news to a world that desperately needs it."

toRemember: "Christmas isn't just about looking back at Jesus' birth—it's about looking forward to His return, when He will crush evil and reign forever. Let that hope shape how you live and share the gospel today."
