### The Emphasis on Motive

[00:21] In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus addresses significant spiritual practices including giving, praying and fasting. In each of these practices, he places his main emphasis upon motive. When we observe these spiritual practices, it shouldn't be to impress people nor are they a measure of our spiritual maturity. Practices such as giving, praying and fasting are important because God can use them to lead us in life and he will bless us accordingly.

### Prayer as Essential Practice

[02:08] In Matthew chapter six verses five to eight, Jesus teaches us, when you pray, don't be like the hypocrites who love to pray publicly on street corners and in synagogues and where everyone can see them. [03:04] I tell you the truth, that is all the reward they will ever get. But when you pray, go away by yourself, shut the door behind you and pray to your father in private. Then your Father who sees everything will reward you. When you pray, don't babble on and on as some religions do. In other words, we don't pray as a mantra. We do not offer repetitive prayer. Don't be like them, for your father knows exactly what you need even before you ask it. So there is no need to habituate a perpetual repetitive prayer. It's important for us to pray on an ongoing basis but with meaning and with the intensity it deserves.

[03:30] Please note that the Lord states when you pray, not if, but when we pray. [04:06] It's not optional. It's a given that all of us should pray. We need to take the initiative, stir ourselves up to pray. In Acts sixteen, we find Paul as he went from city to city, strengthening churches in their faith and also spreading the gospel. The first place he sought was where prayer was already happening. [04:28] If we're aware of that, he actually went to the places where prayer was already taking place and seeds were being sown. [05:02] We too need to live in this attitude of prayer where we're easily moved to pray for different things we encounter each and every day.

### Understanding Fasting

[10:52] So then, what is fasting? Fasting is to voluntarily abstain from food or other legitimate enjoyments for a specific period and for spiritual purposes. We can fast from food, technology or even entertainment. Fasting is willing to set aside legitimate appetites of the body whether it's food, entertainment or technology for a specific period of time and concentrate on praying and seeking God. It not only lends a note of urgency and persistence to our prayers, but it is also a powerful personal discipline to train our will, to develop self control and tune our spiritual sensitivity towards God. We don't fast to try to manipulate or force God into action.

[11:43] God answers according to his will, his good and pleasing will alone. Fasting and praying aligns us to the purpose God has for us. Often we have what I refer to as the Ant's perspective. [12:26] He sees everything. He can see what is before us, after us, around us, what I often refer to as the bird's eye view. He sees everything. Like an eagle in the sky, God sees what is ahead us and moves us towards it. Though we may not fully understand the mystery of fasting and prayer, but when we willingly set aside appetites of the flesh to seek God, we are a, clearing space for him to come and speak with us and b, we're calling out to him to quench our thirsty hearts with his presence. [13:08] We're seeking to draw near to him.
