# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "When anger rises, stop and listen first—because your reaction reveals whether you trust yourself or God, and only God's way brings peace."
keyScriptureRef: "James 1:19-20 ESV"
keyScriptureText: "Know this, my beloved brothers: let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God."

mainPoints:
  - title: Recognize the signs of escalating anger.
    body: "Exaggeration, generalization, and assumption are red flags that anger is about to spiral. When you hear yourself saying \"always,\" \"never,\" or \"everyone,\" stop and ask if it's actually true."
  - title: Understand before you react.
    body: Most anger is rooted in fear, helplessness, or misunderstanding. If you listen first—really listen—you defuse half the problem before you open your mouth.
  - title: "Remember that your anger doesn't fix anything."
    body: "Venting makes it worse. Silence and prayer make space for clarity. The opposite of anxiety is not control—it's prayerful gratitude."
  - title: Stay focused on your purpose.
    body: "Paul was being beaten by a mob and still asked permission to preach. When you remember why you're there, distractions lose their power."

keyIllustration: "A parent on the sideline of a school hockey match grew frustrated with what seemed like wrong call after wrong call by the umpire. Heat rose with every whistle. Shouting followed. Later, a fellow parent—who happened to be from the opposing team—walked alongside. A student greeted the parent: \"Evening, Pastor Pete.\" The weight of that moment was instant—credibility lost, witness compromised, all because anger was vented instead of examined. The next week, a similar call was made. This time, after the match and after calming down, the parent approached the umpire and simply asked for an explanation. Turns out the rule had changed in 2023, and the call was correct. Listening first would have saved the anger entirely."

application:
  - "When you feel heat rising, shut up first. Repeat James 1:19-20 in your mind until the emotion settles."
  - Ask God for compassion for the person frustrating you—they may be harassed and helpless, just like you.
  - "Replace anxiety with prayer and thanksgiving. Name what you're grateful for before you name what you're angry about."

toRemember: Let your gentleness be evident to all—the Lord is near, and He will give you peace when you hand Him your anger.
