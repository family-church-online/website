# ── ABOUT LISTS ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
takeaways:
  - The human heart is deceitful and fickle—adoration turns to violence in an instant
  - Suffering refocuses us on eternal purpose and detaches us from temporary comfort
  - "We rise through the power of community, God's promises, and clear purpose"
  - People-pleasing will always fail; God-pleasing sustains through persecution
  - Church leaders exist to care for, correct, and encourage believers when trials come

audience:
  - "You've been betrayed by people you served or helped"
  - "You're staying down because getting back up feels pointless"
  - You base your identity on approval, likes, or public opinion
  - "You're isolating yourself instead of letting others help you stand"

# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: We rise from betrayal and persecution not by our own strength, but through the power of community, the promises of God, and the clarity of purpose He gives us.
keyScriptureRef: "Acts 14:19-20 ESV"
keyScriptureText: But Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, and having persuaded the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead. But when the disciples gathered about him, he rose up and entered the city.

mainPoints:
  - title: Human hearts are fickle and sinful.
    body: Popularity is fleeting; the same people who worship you will stone you when their loyalty shifts.
  - title: We need people around us when we fall.
    body: Paul only rose when the disciples surrounded him—isolation kills hope, community restores it.
  - title: "God's supernatural power enables perseverance."
    body: No one survives a stoning and walks back into the same city without divine strength upholding them.
  - title: "Purpose gets us up when emotion can't."
    body: "Paul didn't just survive—he revisited every hostile city to appoint elders and strengthen believers because his mission mattered more than his pain."

keyIllustration: A twelve-year-old once observed that people who have suffered deeply tend to be better people. Suffering strips away shallow comforts and reorients our hearts toward what truly matters—not what we accumulate in this life, but how we trust and serve Jesus through it.

application:
  - "Look for someone who can't get up this week and help them—through encouragement, presence, or pointing them back to Jesus."
  - "Stop seeking approval from people; their opinions change in an instant. Seek God's approval instead."
  - Pray for your church leaders and submit to their care—they exist to help you stand when trials come.

toRemember: "When you don't feel like getting up, remember: the power of people around you, the power of God's promises, and the power of purpose He has given you."
