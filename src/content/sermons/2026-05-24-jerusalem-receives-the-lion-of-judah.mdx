# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "Christ will return to Jerusalem as the Lion of Judah to judge the nations and establish His kingdom—but until that day, you're called to build something eternal with the time you have left."
keyScriptureRef: "Joel 2:28-31 ESV"
keyScriptureText: And it shall come to pass afterward that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh. Your sons and daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men shall see visions. Even on the male and female servants in those days I will pour out my Spirit. mainPoints:
  - title: "Pentecost fulfilled part of Joel's prophecy—but the Day of the Lord is still coming."
    body: "The Spirit was poured out. The \"wonders in the heavens\" and Christ's return are still ahead. We live on the green line in between."
  - title: "The \"time of the Gentiles\" is the long stretch we're in now."
    body: Jesus said Jerusalem would be trampled until the time of the Gentiles is fulfilled. That period began in 70 AD and continues today—a season of both suffering and opportunity.
  - title: Herod built palaces; martyrs in Caesarea built the church.
    body: "Kneeling in sand that hurt, surrounded by luxury that's now ruins, early Christians chose to build something that outlasted Rome. Their witness brought thousands to faith."
  - title: Jesus will return to the Mount of Olives when Israel repents.
    body: "Scripture is clear: Christ will not return until the Jewish nation mourns for the one they pierced and says, \"Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord.\" Zechariah 12, Zechariah 14, and Jesus' own words in Luke 21 all converge on this moment."

keyIllustration: "At Caesarea's hippodrome—where Jews and Christians were martyred for entertainment—kneeling in coarse sand that dug into the knees, the contrast became inescapable: Herod's palace is rubble; the church those martyrs built with their blood is still here. They had nothing but prayer. We're here because they built anyway."

application:
  - "Ask yourself honestly: what am I building? A comfortable life, or something with eternal value? Your green line—the time between now and your last breath—is shorter than you think."
  - "Get on your knees first, then get up and work. Prayer is where kingdom-building starts, but it doesn't end there. Disciple someone. Serve in the church. Use your gifts for God's glory, not your own comfort."
  - No one can stop what God is building through you. Herod tried. Rome tried. Islam has tried. The gates may be sealed, but the Lion is still returning. Keep building.

toRemember: "Two thousand years is a long time—but it's not eternity. Live, plan, and build for what lasts forever."
