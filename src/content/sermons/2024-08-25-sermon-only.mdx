# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "Jesus confronts us with three provocations: Are you seeking God's will or your own? Are you pursuing God's glory or your own? Can you give grace and receive it, or are you trapped in a self-serving religious system?"
keyScriptureRef: "Luke 14:11 ESV"
keyScriptureText: For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.

mainPoints:
  - title: "Seek God's Will, Not Your Own"
    body: "The Pharisees used a sick man as a trap, caring nothing for his suffering — only for their agenda. Jesus exposes their hypocrisy: they claim to defend the Sabbath but would rescue their own ox without hesitation. Religion becomes dangerous when it serves our comfort instead of God's compassion."
  - title: "Seek God's Glory, Not Your Own"
    body: "Guests at the meal scrambled for seats of honour, elbowing past each other to be seen. Jesus teaches that the humble heart doesn't need external validation because it knows its value comes from God. If you can't honour another person above yourself, you cannot truly glorify God."
  - title: Show Grace and Receive Grace
    body: "Jesus tells the host: stop only inviting people who can repay you. Invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind — those who can't give you social capital. Then He tells a parable: the wealthy, the comfortable, the respectable all decline the kingdom invitation. The feast is filled with outsiders. The question is sharp: Have you accepted the invitation, or are you too comfortable to come?"

keyIllustration: "A man with dropsy — a visibly serious medical condition — is present at the same dinner as Jesus. The Pharisees are watching Him carefully. It becomes clear: they invited this sick man not out of compassion, but as bait. They knew Jesus would heal him, and when He did, they could accuse Him of breaking the Sabbath. Jesus heals the man and immediately sends him away, then confronts His accusers: Who has done good here, and who has done evil?"

application:
  - "Ask yourself: When I quote Scripture or defend what's \"right,\" am I truly pursuing God's will, or am I just protecting my preferences and comfort?"
  - "Examine your social life and hospitality. Are you only comfortable around people who reflect well on you, or are you willing to extend grace to those who can't repay you?"
  - "Don't assume your religious background guarantees your place at the kingdom feast. Many are invited, but few come. Have you truly accepted the invitation?"

toRemember: "This is the God who knows He's being persecuted by evil people — and still goes to eat with them, giving them every opportunity to turn around, to leave their self-centred lives, and to live for Him."
