# When reputation hides reality

### Understanding the Letter to Sardis

Revelation and the seven letters to the seven churches. Today is going to be another one of those letters, probably a different way of approaching it as we look at this letter to a church in a city called Sardis. We first have to understand why this letter is written, who it's written to, and what it's addressing. We can see what it means to them, then take that message and apply it to us. It's not written to us, which is why it's sometimes difficult to understand what books like Revelation are trying to tell us. We don't first try to understand what the first people would have understood from it. Put it in its correct context, and then you can apply it once you know what it was trying to communicate to them.

### David and Ahithophel's Betrayal

It's one thing to understand the letter. It's another thing to be motivated to do something different. But if you don't actually do something different, you waste your time studying it, and you actually become a bit thick skinned to that change. It becomes harder and harder to change if you keep hearing a message to change, keep understanding it, but don't apply it.

Who's read about David and Bathsheba? God loved King David—the word says David was a man after God's own heart. But David did a terrible thing. He saw a woman who wasn't his wife, had a desire for her, got her pregnant, and wanted to hide the fact that it was him, so he had her husband murdered.

David made incredible mistakes that caused a lot of pain for himself and his family. His son Absalom caused him a huge amount of pain. David banished Absalom because of something he did. Absalom eventually came back, angry at his dad for the inconsistency in the way he was dealt with. He tried to take the kingdom away from David. He rebelled to such a point that David had to take all his family and run out of Jerusalem and hide, because Absalom was going to kill him and take his crown.

And it wasn't the only betrayal. David had a much older man, a very wise counselor called Ahithophel. Ahithophel was with him through thick and thin. When David needed advice about military problems or anything else, he could go to this man. He was a highly respected, very wise counselor and amazing to David. Then, when Absalom launched this rebellion against David, Ahithophel abandoned David. He stabbed him in the back and went over to Absalom and said, "I'm here to serve you. I'll serve you in destroying my king, who I've served all this time." Have you ever been in that situation where somebody you trusted, somebody you thought loved you, somebody you thought had your best interests at heart, and then you get betrayed like that? This is going to bring us into a very useful thing to learn this morning.

### Jesus Holds the Churches

Revelation three, one to six: "And to the angel of the church in Sardis write: The words of him who has the seven spirits of God and the seven stars. I know your works. You have the reputation of being alive, but you are dead. Wake up and strengthen what remains and is about to die, for I have not found your works complete in the sight of my God. Remember then what you received and heard. Keep it and repent. If you will not wake up, I will come like a thief and you will not know at what hour I will come against you. Yet you still have a few names in Sardis, people who have not soiled their garments, and they will walk with me in white for they are worthy. The one who conquers will be clothed thus in white garments, and I will never blot his name out of the book of life. I will confess his name before my Father and before his angels. He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches."

Whenever there's a greeting in the letter, it starts with a part of an image that John has already shared. There's a picture of Jesus that John sees when he's told to write these letters, and it's a powerful picture—a disturbing one, a frightening picture. It's not there to show you what Jesus looks like, but to show you who he is, what his heart is, what his mission is. He's got white hair, flaming eyes like fire, a sharp two-edged sword coming from his mouth. He's holding stars in one hand, walking between lampstands with feet like burning bronze. His voice sounds like thunder or even louder. John sees this and he's terrified. But each part of this vision is repeated to each church to teach them something about Jesus and something about their need and their problem at the time. Jesus is writing to these seven churches because they're doing something wrong. Something is going wrong, and Jesus loves us too much to leave us and let us carry on doing the wrong thing.

The image that this church is given is "the one who has the seven spirits of God and the seven stars." The seven stars are the leaders of the seven churches. The image is to remind you that Jesus holds in his hands the leaders of our churches. It's an incredibly comforting thing to know that Jesus holds me in his hand, that he holds the elders of this church in his hand. Without that, I'm wasting my time, messing this job up, and probably going to destroy myself and some other people with me. But by his grace, he's holding me, and that's amazing.

He has the seven spirits. It doesn't say he has the seven spirits in his hand, and this is an incredibly confusing way of communicating that Jesus sends the Holy Spirit of God into the church. Zechariah four, two to six: "'What do you see?' He said to me. I said, 'I see, and behold, a lampstand all of gold with a bowl on top of it and seven lamps on it, with seven lips on each of the lamps. And there are two olive trees by it, one on the right of the bowl and one on the left.' I said to the angel, 'What are these, my lord?' The angel answered, 'Do you not know what these are?' I said, 'No, my lord.' Then he said to me, 'This is the word of the Lord: Not by might, nor by power, but by my Holy Spirit, says the Lord of hosts.'"

A few verses later in verse ten he talks about the seven eyes of God. It doesn't mean God has seven eyes or that there are seven holy spirits. The number seven is an image of perfection. When Zechariah sees this image of all these sevens and says, "What is this?" the angel says, "Not by might nor by power, but by my Holy Spirit." The image of Jesus with the seven stars and having the seven spirits is intended to remind the church that the Holy Spirit is the power by which anything happens. He is a person just like Jesus. He is being explained with the number seven, but he is one person. God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit is one person, and he is the power. He is the agent of power through which God the Father works through Jesus. The message for the church is: I hold the church in my hand and I bring the Holy Spirit to the church.

The spirit of God is perfect. The spirit of God brings life. The spirit of God sees everything. And this oil from these olive trees that continually flows into these lamps and burns is an image of life being given by the Holy Spirit. Why would this church need to be reminded, "I hold your leaders in my hand and I bring life"? We're going to see that now.

### The Reputation of Being Alive But Dead

The next verse says: "I know your works. You have the reputation of being alive, but you are dead." This is strange because here's where the commendation, the praise, where you've done well would normally sit in the letter. But it's missing. Instead you hear: "I know your works. You have a reputation for being alive, but you are dead."

The tragedy is their commendation, their praise, their compliment, the things that are good about them are not being said by Jesus but by other people. They have a good reputation. Other people say good things about them. But what does that matter if it's not God saying good things about them? Here you see the whole collapse of this poor church. It's entirely possible to have a church that looks like it's thriving. People will say, "Go to this church. The preaching's amazing. Go to this church. The worship's incredible. Go to this church, the people are such amazing people. They make you feel so welcome. It's an incredible place." It's entirely possible to have all of that, and then the words that follow are devastating: "I know your works, you have a reputation for being alive, but you're dead."

Are you like me? Do you like knowing that people like you? Do you struggle when you find out that people do not like you? Are you moved perhaps too much by the opinion and acceptance of other human beings? If you are, this letter is speaking to you because it's speaking to me. Jesus says, "I don't care what other people think about you. I see. I know the seven eyes, the spirits of God, the Holy Spirit of God. He sees. He's with you. He knows. He's telling me who you really are, and you're not alive. You're not doing the stuff you're supposed to be doing." That's not a great thing to hear.

Wake up and strengthen what remains, for it's about to die. I get this as well. When people are saying good things about you all the time, it lulls you into a sense of security—very much like being asleep, because you're unaware of the problems. It's a nice, cozy place. If my alarm clock went off this morning, I did not want to get out of bed because I'm comfortable with the way things are. I hear noises, my mind is saying you've got to get up, you've got to do this, you've got to do that, and I'm ignoring them because I'm happy where I am. Having this good reputation can do exactly the same to us. Nice and warm, nice and comforting. I don't want to find out anything to the contrary. I don't want to get up out of this. I don't want to find out that maybe I'm not what God has called me to be, maybe I'm not what people think I am, and maybe I need to catch a wake up. Can you imagine the church? You got a letter from John. Fantastic. Read it. We all need to hear it. We're an amazing church. Everyone says how fantastic we are. Bang. That's what they get.

### Wake Up and Remember

Jesus says: "Remember then what you have received and heard. Keep it and repent. If you will not wake up, I will come like a thief and you will not know at what hour I will come against you." That's terrifying. It's saying wake up. Stop being lulled into this false sense of security by what people are saying. Instead, remember what you've received and heard and keep it and repent. It's here. It's in the Bible. It tells you what you're doing right. It tells you what you're doing wrong. Ask it. Pray over it. Come to the Lord and say, "Lord, if you need to shake me awake, please shake me awake now. Because I don't want to have what is dying die completely."

The more complacent we are, the more happy we are with the way things are, the more we take people's comfort and say, "Well that means I'm okay." Slowly but surely, something in us is dying. Our love for other people. Our desire to forgive people who've hurt us. Our desire to obey God. Our desire to spend time in prayer, to read his word, to share our faith with others, to encourage other people, to go to people we don't like and do wonderful things for them. To stand up for the truth. To stand up for people who are being hurt. The more this dull, sleepy state of a good reputation has its way, the more little things die in us.

The answer is to go to the scriptures and say, "Lord, show me who I am." David prays this. He says, "Lord, search me and know me. If there be anything wrong with me, show me. I don't want to lie here slowly but surely dying. Wake me up. Show me what I need to change. Show me what you need to change in me."

### White Garments—Few Remain Unsoiled

"Yet you have still a few names in Sardis, people who have not soiled their garments and they will walk with me in white for they are worthy." This comes from Zechariah. In Zechariah, we find out that there's a priest clothed in a white garment, but his white garment is dirty. He's standing before God in what is called a soiled garment, and Satan is standing there accusing this man before God, pointing out his faults, pointing out his failures. Would you rather have God point them out to you as you quietly sit before him in prayer and read his word, or would you like Satan to accuse you publicly and expose you?

As Satan is accusing Joshua the priest in the book of Zechariah, he is rebuked. He's told to shut up, and God clothes this priest in a clean white garment, takes away the stains, takes away the filth, and says, "The Lord rebuke you, Satan. Don't accuse somebody who God has made clean."

It's not a surprise that the same Zechariah where we get the image of the seven spirits also introduces the same image of this white garment. When we trust, when we believe in Jesus Christ, when we believe that he has died for us and he's taken our punishment, when we believe that it's by grace that we've been saved and by faith in Jesus only, we are given this beautiful white garment. It means our sins are forgotten. It means we are made right in the eyes of God. I get that and I'm pleased with that image, pleased to read it and relieved to read it, to be reminded that's who I am. I've been clothed in God's righteousness, not because I deserve it, but because he chose to do that for me.

But then I'm really challenged because it says you have a few still in Sardis who have not soiled their garments. That's a weird way of putting it. They are people in a church, they are Christians, we believe they've been saved, they've been given this white garment, but they've soiled it. They've messed it up. What does that mean? Because everyone in that church has done something wrong. Why have some of them not soiled their garments? The text gives us the clue. They've woken up. They woke up and they said, "Lord, please keep me from soiling my garments. Show me what I'm doing wrong. Help me repent. Help me to know what I'm supposed to do and to do it."

It's entirely possible for a church to be saved and not to do the things God is calling us to do, and even worse, to do the things he's told us not to do. It's all under the cover of people liking us and having a good reputation. It's all hidden. But he sees it, and he says, "If you can keep that garment clean, if you can stay awake, if you can keep awake, you will walk with me."

From Zechariah three, verse three: "Now Joshua was standing before the angel clothed in filthy garments and the angel said to those who were standing before him, 'Remove the filthy garments from him.' And he said to him, 'Behold, I have taken your iniquity away from you and I will clothe you with pure vestments.'" That was a priest of God and he needed cleaning. We get forgiven once, we get made whole once, we get reborn once, we get reconciled with God the moment we believe in Jesus. But that doesn't mean we can sit back and then make our clothes dirty for the rest of our lives. We're going to make them dirty, we're going to mess up. But if we can wake up every single day and say, "Lord, I know I do that. Please show me. Keep me awake. Keep me ready for you." Jesus says, "I'm coming at an hour you don't expect. He's coming back any day now. Will we have sorted that stuff out with him? Or will we be fast asleep, lulled into the sense of 'I'm okay because people think I'm cool'?"

### The Promise to Those Who Conquer

"The one who conquers will be clothed thus in white garments and I will never blot his name out of the book of life. I will confess his name before my Father and before his angels." Later on in Revelation seventeen, verse eight, we hear that those whose names have not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world will marvel at the beast. They will be drawn aside. They will be fooled into following Satan's plan. Having our name written in the book of life from before the foundation of the world means that Jesus knows he has determined exactly how he's going to save us. We are saved from absolutely everything if we hold on to our faith in him.

We can have debates about whether you can lose your salvation. We can have all sorts of debates about predestination and free will. But the bottom line is we are warned: don't fall asleep. You have this incredible security in the fact that Jesus chose you and saved you before the foundation of the world. Wonderful! Know that he has you, that you don't have to sit there at night afraid whether God has saved you or not.

But then come the warnings: don't fall asleep. Don't be complacent. You need to be cooperating with him all the time, confessing your sin to him all the time, trusting him to heal you and restore you all the time. If you are that way, if you are awake, if you do walk with him, if you do cooperate with his work in you, he will never blot your name out of the book of life.

Do you think it's a nice thing being loved by people? Having a good reputation? Being accepted? It's good. You enjoy that. I enjoy that. Nobody doesn't enjoy that. But what is it going to be like when Jesus puts you in front of God the Father and all the millions of angels and says, "This is my faithful servant. I love him. He trusted in me. Let me introduce you to all the angels. I love you and I'm not afraid to tell all the angels today, you're my faithful servant." Would you like that, or would you like the whole world thinking well of you? The scary thing is the very reasons that the world thinks well of us is often because we soil our garments the same way they soil theirs because we get involved in the same horrible stuff that we know we shouldn't with them. They welcome us. They're not threatened by us.

### Listen to What the Spirit Says

"He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches." This ends every single one of the letters. Today it's taken on a very new meaning for me. I don't see what it is that's soiling your garments. You don't see what it is that's soiling my garments. But I've been told the Lord sees, and I need to take the time and sit with him and say, "Tell me, Lord, so that I can deal with it with you. You can deal with it for me."

If you want to sit before the Lord today and say, "Lord, I don't want to be asleep under the false assurance of being popular or liked or respected or having a good reputation. I don't want to have that in the way. I want to know from you what needs work. If you're ready to do that today, then this is what it's saying. It's saying, if you have an ear, listen to what the Holy Spirit says to you today when you ask him that. When you take time with him today and say, "Lord, show me." Listen to what he says. It's a brave prayer. It's a scary prayer. It's the best thing you'll ever do. Much better having God show you what needs to change than Satan pointed out. Much more wonderful to be loved, to be accepted, to be valued, to be introduced to God and all of his angels than to any other audience.

Remember that we're asking the one who loves us to show us. We're asking the one who died for us to take away our sin, to show us what he needs to do in our hearts. Can you trust him? Can you trust he'll do that in love? Can you trust the process he'll take you through?

Ahithophel served David faithfully year after year after year. But the moment the opportunity came to betray him, he jumped at it. Absalom came to him and said, "What are we going to do? David has run away into the desert with his family. I want to seize power over this country. You were my father's advisor. You've come across to my side. Now give me your advice. What do we do?" Ahithophel said, "Take him now. Chase him now. Attack him now, because he's weak, emotionally down, hurting, running, vulnerable. If you don't attack him and kill him now, you won't." And on top of that, he said, "I want you to show the whole world that you have turned against him. I want you to show the whole world that you despise your father, that you're going to destroy him." He advised him to sleep with his father's woman on a rooftop for all Jerusalem to see. That was his advice. He took some of that advice, but God in his grace sent another adviser to persuade him not to attack David that night, but to wait to get a big army in place and then attack him. That's what saved David. But if he'd followed Ahithophel's advice, that could have been the end. But as it is, it just destroyed David's family. That was David's trusted advisor, somebody who had a great reputation, who nobody could see, who nobody knew what their real heart was. And you know why? He was Bathsheba's grandfather. He'd been waiting all that time to pay vengeance for what David had done.

I'd rather take that picture and say, imagine if he had said, "Lord, show me that hatred in my heart. Work it out for me." Imagine David had sat down and said, "Lord, show me the people that I need to sort out, that I need to fix up. Show me the people I need to go to and I need to fix this. Show me what's wrong. It looks like this guy is loyal to me, looks like he loves me." Imagine that they got to sit down together and talk, and David had been able to say to him, "Listen, I'm sure this must have devastated you. I'm so sorry I did that. We need to get this sorted. We need to get this right." All the pain that would have stopped. All the destruction. We can't trust reputation. We're very complicated creatures, and we need God to show us and help us and correct us, to wake up and let him clean us and keep us clean.

### Closing Prayer

Father, thank you for your word. I struggle so much with it, Lord. Just imagine all the things that I've done that need to be sorted out. And it's a difficult thing to say today: if we have an ear, let us hear what the Spirit says to us. But today we want to come to you and say, Lord, speak to us. Tell us what we need to hear, what we don't want to hear. Tell us what we need to hear because we don't want to be fast asleep when you come back. We don't want to be lulled into a sense of security where there isn't one.

At the same time, Lord, please give us an assurance of your love, of the fact that you have clothed us in white. You have saved us by faith in Jesus. Please don't let us take that for granted. Please don't let us get complacent in that. You have the most amazing plan for us. Please, Lord, let us not be a church that you have to say to us, "I've not found your works complete in the sight of my God."

Lord, thank you for what you've done for us, and thank you that we can look to this process, we can look to this joy of walking with you as you keep us in white garments every day. Every day we wake up. Every day you forgive us. Every day we review. Every day we trust that you are making us better and better. You're cleaning us. Oh, Jesus, you told us to pray, "Forgive us our trespasses." You told us to pray that every single day. Lord, keep us close to you. Keep us knowing your word, your law, your truth. Help us to be guided by that, by your Holy Spirit, and not by what the world thinks. Whether they think well of us or whether they despise us, what really matters is what you think, what you see, and how we respond to what you say.

And Lord, thank you for this promise: you will never, you will never blot our names out of the book of life, and we will walk with you in white. Not for a day, not for a month, not for a year, but for all eternity because you love us, because you gave your life for us. If you have an ear, hear what the Holy Spirit says to our church. In Jesus' name. Amen.
