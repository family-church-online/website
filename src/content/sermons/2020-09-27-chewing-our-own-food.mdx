mainPoints:
  - title: You can become dull to the Word.
    body: "Repeated exposure without personal application doesn't strengthen you—it numbs you. Drifting leads to doubting, doubting leads to dullness, and dullness leads to deliberate disobedience and eventual rejection."
  - title: Spiritual maturity requires chewing your own food.
    body: Just as a baby depends on milk processed by the mother, new believers depend on teachers. But maturity means learning to digest Scripture yourself—reading, interpreting, and applying it with the goal of being able to teach others.
  - title: Scripture reveals three things.
    body: It shows us who God is in His holiness and perfection, who we are in our sinfulness and need, and what Jesus has done to bridge the infinite gap between the two.
  - title: Growth happens through constant practice.
    body: Discernment—the ability to distinguish good from evil—comes not from intellectual knowledge alone but from repeatedly applying Scripture to real life until heart knowledge forms.

keyIllustration: A young rugby player named Jan Hendrik Wessels stands 1.95 meters tall, weighs 120 kilograms, and plays Super Rugby at nineteen years old. If he had stayed on milk as an infant, he would never have grown strong enough to compete. Spiritual growth works the same way—you cannot afford to stay a baby when bigger, stronger opponents are coming.

application:
  - "Ask yourself: Am I studying Scripture with the intent to teach it, or just to feel encouraged? The goal of maturity is being equipped to feed others."
  - "Use the three-question method: What does this passage say? What does it mean (about God, about us, about Jesus)? What do I do with it?"
  - "Join or start a Bible study group—growth accelerates when you study with others and practice explaining what you're learning."

toRemember: Scripture moves from head to heart when you experience what it promises—but you can only experience it if you study it, understand it, and obey it.
