# ── SERMON NOTES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
bigIdea: "Jesus opened a straight path back to God through His blood, but many of us still take the long way around—trying to earn what's already been paid for. Gratitude, not performance, is the Christian response to the New Covenant."
keyScriptureRef: "Luke 22:19-20 ESV"
keyScriptureText: "And he took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, \"This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.\" And likewise the cup after they had eaten, saying, \"This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.\""

mainPoints:
  - title: The New Covenant is a restored access way.
    body: "Sin created a barrier between us and God. The Old Covenant was the long way around—a system of sacrifices and laws. Jesus opened the direct path through His blood. Yet we often return to the old route, trying to earn God's favour through performance instead of walking through grace."
  - title: Gratitude in suffering is the ultimate Christian witness.
    body: Jesus gave thanks before breaking bread—knowing full well the torture, humiliation, and death awaiting Him. He models for us that gratitude is not contingent on circumstances but rooted in trust that God is working all things for His purpose.
  - title: Jesus earnestly desires your presence at His table.
    body: This is not a ritual to endure but an invitation to intimacy. He prepares a table for you in the presence of your enemies—your sin, your fear, your disappointment. He wants you there, and He paid the ultimate price to make it possible.

keyIllustration: A business owner shares how a client demanded repayment for work that was completed but fell slightly short of verbal estimates—not contractual obligations. Despite having legal grounds to fight, the financial cost of doing so was impossible to bear. Through prayer, bitterness gave way to gratitude. God was using the injustice to shape character, not to punish. The illustration shows how we pray for God to remove hardship when He may be using it to refine us—two prayers we think are different but may actually be the same.

application:
  - "Stop taking the long way around. If you're still trying to earn acceptance through rule-keeping or good behaviour, you're ignoring the open gate Jesus died to create. Walk through Him."
  - Live in gratitude regardless of circumstances. When life is unfair—and it will be—ask God not to remove the difficulty but to equip you through it and change you in it.
  - Come to the table with a clean heart. If you harbour bitterness, resentment, or unconfessed sin, deal with it before taking communion. The table is holy, and participation is a declaration of faith and submission.

toRemember: "Jesus gave thanks before He suffered because He knew the Father's purpose was unfolding—and He invites you to the table not because you're worthy, but because He paid the price to make you welcome."
