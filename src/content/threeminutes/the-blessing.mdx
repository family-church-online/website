Even that beautiful sensation of Lego bricks lodging themselves in the soft instep of your tired  feet as you negotiate your way towards their little beds is worth it. Bedtime is a fantastic opportunity for parent child bonding, and surprisingly, even for their neurological development. A chat, Bible story, prayer and blessing can form part of a nightly routine that may put our children at a significant advantage.

This is confirmed by Dr G. Reid Lyon from the National Institute of Child Health and Human Development. According to research, bedtime stories provide children with an array of benefits from improved logic and literacy skills all the way through to lowered stress levels. So clear are the advantages, that British philosopher Adam Swift thinks parents who read to their children at bedtime should think about how they are unfairly disadvantaging other people’s children! Yes you read that last sentence correctly. 

It’s not just an advantage to the children either. Some of the most precious and rewarding moments can come at the time when little minds are reviewing the events of the day and trying desperately to maximise time with us parents. Filling our own minds with Bible stories and the candid prayers of our kids is far more stress relieving than the day’s news headlines. The Bible tells us that faith comes from hearing the Word of God, and a stronger faith certainly leads towards a better night’s rest.

The cherry on the cake of the bedtime routine in our home is the blessing. I haven’t found any scientific study to cite in it’s favour, but God encourages us to bless one another and that’s enough. My wife and I can read or tell stories, pray for our children and hear their prayers, but we had better not leave the room without having blessed them! There’s a blessing included on this page, why not try it for a few nights with your children?
