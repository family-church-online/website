  text: "And they said to the mountains and the rocks, 'Fall on us and hide us from the presence of Him who sits on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb."
reflection: |
  They are clawing their way into caves, these people, begging the rocks to crush them — anything to be hidden from a face. You might expect the face they are fleeing to belong to something monstrous. A roaring lion, teeth bared. A towering figure of terror. But that is not what Scripture says they are running from. They are running from the wrath of the Lamb.
  
  Sit with how strange that is. A lamb is the gentlest thing in the field — small, mild, the animal that cannot defend itself, the one led quietly wherever it is taken. If you had spent your whole life mocking the faith, and someone warned you to beware the anger of a lamb, you might almost laugh. What could a lamb possibly do?
  
  And then the Lamb opens His mouth, and the kings of the earth hide among the rocks.
  
  This is the same Lamb who once was led to the slaughter and did not open His mouth — silent before His accusers, silent in the hour of His own unjust judgment, letting Himself be killed. The wounds are still on Him. He is not fearsome because He is cruel; His is the gentlest heart that ever beat. But gentleness is not weakness, and the love that bled in silence is the very love that will one day set every wrong to right. Here is the most astonishing thing of all: the Lamb whose wrath they flee is the same Lamb who bled for them — for the very ones now hiding in the rocks. There was mercy for them in those wounds, had they only turned and taken it.

lifeApplication: |
  It is easy to mistake gentleness for indifference — to assume that because God is patient, He is unconcerned, and that there will always be more time. The people in the rocks learned otherwise too late.
  
  But the same truth that sobers us is the one that saves us: the wounds of the Lamb still speak mercy today. Whatever you have been slow to bring to Him, whatever you have assumed can wait, bring it now, while His face is turned toward you in grace. And let His example reshape you — for the strongest love the world has ever known came wrapped in the gentleness of a Lamb. Wherever you hold power over others, hold it the way He does.
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, You are the Lamb who was led to the slaughter and did not open Your mouth.
  
  I stand amazed that the gentlest heart is also the strongest, and that You bled for me.
  
  Thank You that Your wounds still speak mercy, and that Your face is turned toward me in grace.
  
  Keep me from mistaking Your patience for permission to delay; let me come to You today.
  
  Teach me to carry gentleness and strength together, as You do.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
