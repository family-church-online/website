reflection: |
  Peter is so sure of himself. The room is warm with loyalty and he says the thing he truly means: "Lord, I will never leave You. Others may fall away, but not me — I would go to prison and to death for You." He has not yet met the part of himself that will crumble by a stranger's fire while a servant girl looks on. He does not know how weak he is. Not yet.

  And Jesus, who knows exactly how weak Peter is, does not argue with the boast or lecture him about pride. He says something far more tender and far more devastating: "Simon, Simon, Satan has asked to sift you like wheat. But I have prayed for you, that your faith may not fail. And when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers." Not if you turn again. When. The failure is foreseen, and beyond the failure a homecoming is already promised — and both of them rest on that one quiet line: I have prayed for you.

  That night the rooster crowed and Peter wept bitterly, exactly as he had been told he would. But his faith did not fail. It bent to breaking and did not break — not because Peter's grip on Jesus was strong, but because Jesus' grip on Peter never loosened. Here is the truth that steadies everything: left to ourselves, our faith would fail too. We are not stronger than Peter. But we were never left to ourselves. The One we are trying so hard to hold onto is, at this very moment, holding onto us and praying us home.

lifeApplication: |
  If your faith feels thin today — more flicker than flame, more doubt than certainty — hear this gently: you are not the one keeping it alive. The same Jesus who prayed for Peter is interceding for you right now, and your faith will not fail, because His prayers do not fail.

  You can stop measuring the strength of your own grip and rest in the strength of His. When you next catch yourself afraid that you are slipping, turn the fear into a small prayer: "Lord, hold onto me, because I cannot hold onto You." That prayer has already been answered before you finished asking it.
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, thank You that You are praying for me even now, exactly as You prayed for Peter.

  I confess how often I trust the strength of my own faith instead of the strength of Your grip.

  Thank You that my failures do not surprise You, and that beyond them You have already promised a homecoming.

  When my faith flickers and I am afraid it will go out, hold onto me, for I cannot hold onto You.

  Turn me back every time I wander, and use even my weakness to strengthen someone else.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
