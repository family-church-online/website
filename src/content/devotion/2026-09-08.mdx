reflection: |
  Twenty metres down, something remarkable happens to a diver who gets the balance right. At the surface you are always working — kicking to stay up, fighting the tug of the tank and the weight belt. But as you descend and adjust, there comes a moment when the pull downward and the lift upward settle into a perfect, silent equality. You stop sinking. You stop rising. You simply hang there in the blue, weightless, carried by the water itself. You are no longer spending yourself to stay in place. You breathe, and the reef opens beneath you, and you move through it with an ease that feels almost like being held by an unseen hand.

  Most of us live our faith the way a swimmer thrashes at the surface — certain that if we stop working for one moment we will sink. We labour to be good enough. We kick and strain to keep our heads above the water of our own guilt and our own expectations. And we are exhausted, because a life spent fighting to stay afloat has no strength left over for anything else.

  He does not offer to help us kick harder. He offers rest — the strange, buoyant rest of a soul that has stopped trying to save itself. It is only when we finally admit that we cannot do this in our own strength that we discover we were never meant to. Grace is the water that holds you the moment you stop fighting it. His yoke is easy not because there is nothing to carry, but because He is the One carrying it.

lifeApplication: |
  Where are you kicking hardest right now? Perhaps it is the effort to prove yourself — to God, to your family, to your own conscience. Perhaps it is the quiet dread that if you rest, everything will fall apart. Today, test the water. Take five unhurried minutes and stop working: no fixing, no proving, no striving. Simply breathe and say, "Lord, I cannot hold myself up, and I am going to let You hold me."

  Rest is not laziness; it is trust with its hands open. The diver who stops fighting does not drift away — he is finally free to move. Choose one burden you have been white-knuckling and, for today, let it rest in stronger hands than yours.
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, I am tired from trying to keep myself afloat.

  I confess how much of my faith has been kicking and straining, as though Your love depended on my effort.

  Thank You that You do not ask me to save myself, but invite me to rest.

  Teach me the stillness of trusting You — to hang weightless in Your grace and let You carry what I cannot.

  Be the water beneath me, holding me up when I finally stop fighting.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
