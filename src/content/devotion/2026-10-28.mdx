  ref: "Revelation 6:13"
  text: "And the stars of the sky fell to the earth, as a fig tree casts its unripe figs when shaken by a great wind."
reflection: |
  Anyone who has stood beneath a jacaranda in full bloom knows the strange spell of it. For a few short weeks the tree is impossibly purple, a cloud of blossom against the sky. And then the wind comes. The flowers let go all at once, drifting down in their thousands, until the pavement, the grass, even the roofs of parked cars are dusted the same soft violet — and for a moment, looking up through the falling petals, the whole sky seems to be coming down in colour.
  
  Scripture reaches for a picture like that to describe the falling of the stars — not a single shooting star we might wish upon, but the heavens letting go all at once, like a fig tree shaken in a gale, dropping its unripe fruit in a rush. Imagine standing under that: not petals but points of light, hundreds upon thousands of them, streaking and burning as they tumble toward the earth.
  
  The beauty of the jacaranda is bound up entirely in the fact that it does not last. That is what makes us stop and stare. And perhaps that is the quiet lesson folded into the falling stars as well — everything we have called permanent, everything fixed and dependable and overhead, is in the end only on loan. Even the lights we steer our lives by can be shaken loose.
  
  It is a sobering thought, but it is not a hopeless one. If even the stars are not fixed, then we were never meant to anchor our hearts to things that fall. The world and all its beauty is passing, like blossom on the wind. Only the One who made the stars, and who will outlast them, is steady enough to build a life upon.

lifeApplication: |
  Look honestly at where your sense of security is planted. So much of what we treasure is genuinely beautiful — relationships, achievements, the comforts we have worked hard for — and none of it is wrong to enjoy. But like blossom on a jacaranda, it is all, every bit of it, temporary.
  
  Hold these good things the way you would hold a falling petal — gratefully, gently, and with an open hand. Let their very impermanence turn your heart toward the One who does not fall. This week, invest something — your time, your attention, your love — into what will outlast the stars, and loosen your grip on what will not.
prayer: |
  Father, I so easily fix my heart on things that cannot last.
  
  Thank You for the fleeting beauty of this world, and for the way it points beyond itself to You.
  
  Teach me to hold every good gift with open hands.
  
  When the things I counted on fall away, let me find You still standing.
  
  Anchor my hope in the One who made the stars and will outlast them all.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
