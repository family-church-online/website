reflection: |
  Hang weightless above a coral reef and the whole world becomes colour and motion. There are cleaning stations where fish line up in an orderly queue, waiting their turn while tiny shrimp climb into open mouths to do their work, then change colour like a little traffic light to signal the job is done. There are sea slugs so vivid they look like ribbons of spilled paint draped across the coral. A great, curious fish drifts in close and nudges you, angling its head to be rubbed like a dog. It is all right there — teeming, extravagant, alive.

  And you could miss every bit of it. You could reach that perfect stillness and decide that stillness is enough — hang in one spot, safe and balanced, and never move. The reef would go on blazing with life just a few strokes away, and you would surface again having seen almost none of it, because you were content simply to float.

  Faith can settle for hovering too. We can reach a comfortable equilibrium — believing the right things, feeling safely held — and quietly refuse to move. But the invitation was never only to float; it was to go somewhere. Repentance is the first stroke that takes us off the spot. Obedience is not the toll we pay for a narrow, colourless life; it is the very movement that carries us into a fullness we would otherwise drift right past. The Spirit is given to those who choose to move.

lifeApplication: |
  What is the one stroke you have been refusing to take? You may know exactly what it is — a habit you keep meaning to lay down, an apology you have rehearsed but never made, an act of obedience you have admired in others but held at arm's length for yourself. Hovering feels safe. It is also how we miss the life that is right in front of us.

  Do not wait to feel strong enough; the strength comes in the moving, not before it. Choose one concrete act of obedience today and actually do it — not merely agree with it, not merely intend it, but take the stroke. You will find the water was ready to carry you all along.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You call me not to a small, safe, hovering life but to fullness in You.

  Forgive me for the times I have been content to float — believing rightly, yet refusing to move.

  Give me courage to take the one stroke of obedience I have been avoiding.

  Let repentance carry me off the spot and into the abundant life Jesus promised.

  And fill me with Your Spirit as I choose, today, to move toward You.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
