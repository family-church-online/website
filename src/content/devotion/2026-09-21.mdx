reflection: |
  The day has been merciless — the kind of heat that never quite loosens its grip, even long after the sun has gone down. You have been travelling for hours, and now you sit close to a low fire, because out here there is no other way to make a meal, and the wood smoke is already promising something good. One by one the frogs begin, then the insects, then the night birds, until the whole darkness around you is singing. Just as your shoulders finally start to drop and the tiredness turns sweet, something moves across the heavy air.
  
  It is a low sound, far off. The first time you hear it, you tell yourself you imagined it. The second time, you know. It is thunder, and it is rolling closer. There is a strange ache in that space between the first rumble and the storm you cannot yet see — you only hear it announcing itself, growing, coming nearer over the bush, until even the creatures around you lift their voices, because they know what is on its way.
  
  This is how heaven sounds when it is about to move. From the throne come flashes of lightning and rumblings and peals of thunder — not chaos, but announcement; something being made ready. And how easily we do with that holy rumble exactly what we do at the fireside. The stirring we felt was only a mood, we say. The sense that God was drawing near was only tiredness. The quiet certainty that He is coming, and soon, we tuck away as someone else's concern. But the rumble is real. He who was, and is, and is to come is not distant weather to be explained away — He is nearer now than He was a moment ago, and the sound on the heavy air is the sound of His approach.

lifeApplication: "There will be a moment today when you feel it — some small stir you would rather dismiss. A nudge to forgive before the day hardens around the offence. A longing you cannot quite name. An unease about something you have let quietly slide. Do not tell yourself you imagined it. That is the first rumble on the heavy air, and it is meant to turn your face toward the One who is coming. Live this ordinary day as someone who has heard the thunder and knows the storm is real — attentive, unhurried, ready."
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that You are not silent or far off, but that even now the sound of Your coming moves across my life.
  
  Forgive me for the many times I have heard Your voice and told myself I only imagined it.
  
  Open my ears to the quiet rumble of Your Spirit today, and give me courage not to explain it away.
  
  Keep me awake and ready, living as one who truly believes You were, and are, and are to come.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
