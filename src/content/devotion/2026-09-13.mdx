reflection: |
  Come to a fireplace the morning after. Last night it roared — bright flames leaping, crackling, throwing heat across the whole room. Now it looks utterly dead: a heap of grey ash, cold to the eye, the drama all gone. But rake gently through the ash and there, deep down, one coal is still pulsing a quiet orange. Lean close, breathe on it, and the glow answers you. The fire is not gone. It has only gone deep.

  Those bright, leaping flames are like happiness. They are wonderful while the fuel lasts — and they die the moment it runs out. Happiness leaves when the team loses, when the diagnosis comes, when the money runs short, when someone we love is no longer in the chair across the room. It was always tied to the wood on the fire, and the wood always, eventually, burns down.

  But joy is the ember beneath the ash. It is not the absence of hard things; it is the coal the cold cannot reach. When every visible flame of happiness has gone out and the room of your life feels grey and cold, joy is still there, glowing low and steady, waiting for a breath. And it endures because its heat does not come from your circumstances at all. It comes from something settled long before you were born: that you were chosen, that you were loved, that Christ laid down His life for you and will not let you go. That ember, the world cannot put out.

lifeApplication: |
  Take stock of what your joy has been resting on. If it rises and falls with the day's news — a good result, a kind word, a full calendar — you have been warming yourself at flames that will not last. There is nothing wrong with those flames; only do not build your soul on them.

  Learn instead to tend the ember. When happiness goes out — and one day it will — do not conclude the fire is dead. Kneel down, rake back the ash, and breathe on the coal: remember that you were chosen before you were born, forgiven at the cross, and held by a love no circumstance can cool. That remembering is the breath that brings the glow back up. The joy of the Lord — not the mood of the moment — is your strength.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You have given me a joy the world did not give and the world cannot take away.

  Forgive me for warming myself at the passing flames of happiness and forgetting the ember You placed deep within.

  When the visible fire goes out and my days turn cold and grey, remind me that the coal still glows.

  Let the knowledge that You chose me, loved me, and saved me be the breath that brings joy back to flame.

  Be my strength today — not because everything is bright, but because You never change.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
