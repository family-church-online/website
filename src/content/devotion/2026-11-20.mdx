reflection: |
  Fire has two faces. The same flame that warms a hearth on a winter evening, that cooks the meal and lights the room, can in a single careless moment leap the grate, catch the curtains, and bring a house down around you. Nothing about the fire itself has changed — only which way it is turned. What comforts and what consumes can be one and the same flame.
  
  Watch what happens to the censer. A moment ago it was a vessel of worship, cradling sweet incense, carrying the prayers of the saints upward in fragrant smoke. Now the same angel fills that same censer — not with incense, but with fire taken straight from the altar — and hurls it down to the earth. And the sky answers: thunder, rumblings, lightning, the ground itself heaving. The vessel that carried mercy upward now carries judgment down. For so long the altar had been a place of atonement and blessing; now it becomes the source of the fire that falls.
  
  There is a turning point in that reversed censer that we dare not miss. For a long age God holds out His patience, and the prayers rise, and the door of mercy stands open. But it does not stand open forever. A day comes when the time for those prayers has passed and the fire falls. This is not told to us to terrify us, but to wake us — because an open door is an invitation, and an invitation is meant to be answered now, while it is still an invitation and not yet a verdict.

lifeApplication: "We are good at assuming there will always be more time — time to deal with the part of our life we keep avoiding, time to come back to God once things are less busy, time to speak to the friend who does not yet know Him. God's patience is real and vast, but it was never meant to be mistaken for indifference, or for an open door that can never close. Today, do the thing you have been putting off: make right what is wrong between you and God, and do not let someone you love drift toward that falling fire without a word from you. The door is still open. Today is still \"now.\""
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You for the long patience that has held the door of mercy open for me.
  
  Forgive me for mistaking Your patience for permission to delay.
  
  Stir me to come to You today, while today is still mine to give.
  
  Lay on my heart the people I love who do not yet know You, and make me bold to speak.
  
  Let me live awake, not asleep, in the light of the day that is coming.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
