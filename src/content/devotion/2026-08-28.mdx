reflection: |
  Smell reaches memory faster than any other sense. One particular scent can drop you, without warning, back into a night you would give anything to forget. For Peter, that smell was charcoal smoke. He had stood over a charcoal fire in a courtyard, warming his hands in the cold, on the night he swore three times — to a servant girl, to strangers, to anyone who asked — that he had never known the Man on trial across the yard. The rooster crowed into that smell. He wept into it.

  Now it is a grey morning by the lake, and he has just thrown himself out of a boat and swum for shore, soaked and gasping. He climbs up onto the sand — and there it is again. Charcoal smoke. The very same scent, welded to the worst night of his life. And crouched over the coals, laying out fish and bread, unhurried, is the risen Jesus, making him breakfast.

  God did not restore Peter somewhere clean and far away from the scene of his failure. He lit the same kind of fire and stood on the other side of it with food in His hands. That is how grace works. It does not tiptoe around the place where we fell; it walks straight back into the smell of it and turns the fire of our denial into the fire where breakfast is served. The place of your deepest shame is exactly where He means to meet you — not with blame, but with breakfast.

lifeApplication: |
  There is probably a smell, a place, a date on the calendar that you flinch from — the memory of the time you failed most badly, denied what you believed, became someone you did not want to be. You may have quietly decided that particular corner of your life is off-limits, even to God.

  It is not. He is already crouched there, tending a fire, waiting to feed you. Bring Him the memory you avoid. Walk back up that beach in prayer. You will not find Him standing there with your failure in His hand; you will find Him with breakfast, and a fresh word: follow Me.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You do not meet me at a safe distance from my failure but right at the scene of it.

  I bring You the memory I flinch from, the night I would give anything to undo.

  Thank You that You are already there, tending the fire, holding out grace instead of blame.

  Turn the place of my shame into the place where You feed me and make me new.

  And when I have eaten, give me the courage to hear You say again: follow Me.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
