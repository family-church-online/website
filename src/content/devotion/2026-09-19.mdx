reflection: |
  You know the person. The one who poisons the office — who takes the credit that is not theirs, who cuts down anyone in the way — and who somehow keeps collecting the promotions. Meanwhile you come in early, do your work honestly, and are passed over once again. Or it is the student who never seems to open a book and sails through, while you carry a quiet dread about your marks. Or it is heavier and closer than any of that: you did everything right, and the bad news came anyway, and in the small hours you find yourself asking the oldest question there is — "Lord, why?"
  
  This ache is ancient. A man once wrote that his feet had almost slipped, because he envied the arrogant when he watched the wicked prosper. He could not make the sums come out. Good people struggled; cruel people thrived; and the harder he tried to reason it through, the more wearisome it became — until he walked into the sanctuary of God. There, in the presence of the throne, he finally saw how it all ends.
  
  Notice what he was given, and what he was not. He was not handed a tidy explanation. He was given God. The sight of the throne did not answer every question, but it put the questions in their place and made them bearable to carry. Some questions are not doors we are meant to force open in this life; for now, they belong to God. But we are offered something better than answers: the God who sits on the throne, making judgments that are righteous and fair and eternal. We do not always get the answer. We always get the Answerer — and the throne we cannot fully see is far steadier than the explanations we cannot get.

lifeApplication: "Name the \"why\" you keep carrying — the one that surfaces when the lights go out. Then, instead of trying one more time to solve it, do what that troubled man did: bring it into the presence of God and set it down. Trade, just for today, your demand for an answer for a long look at the One who reigns. You may find that the question loses none of its weight — but that Someone strong enough to carry it is now carrying it with you."
prayer: |
  Father, You know the questions I turn over when I cannot sleep, and the injustices that make no sense to me.
  
  Forgive me for demanding answers before I will trust You, as though I could run the world more justly than You do.
  
  Thank You that You are on the throne, making judgments that are righteous and true.
  
  Help me to lay my hardest questions at Your feet today and to rest in who You are, even when I do not understand.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
