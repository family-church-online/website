reflection: |
  He had been dead four days when the voice called him out. Picture the mouth of the tomb, the daylight too bright after the dark, and a figure appearing at the entrance — not gliding, but shuffling, because his feet are still bound together. His arms are wrapped tight against his sides. His face is hidden beneath a cloth. He is alive, impossibly, gloriously alive, and still dressed head to foot for the grave.

  Those linen strips were not only bindings; they were soaked in spices to mask the smell of death. And there he stands in the sun, breathing, his heart beating in his chest, wearing the clothes of a corpse. Someone has to step forward and begin the slow work of unwrapping him. Take off the grave clothes, the Lord says, and let him go.

  We tend to think the miracle is the coming out. But there is a second miracle in the unbinding — the patient peeling away of everything that belonged to the old, dead life, so the new life can actually move and walk and breathe free.

  Many of us have already come out of the tomb. We are alive in Christ, forgiven, raised with Him. And still we shuffle. We are wrapped in old habits and old angers, old ways of speaking we quietly told ourselves we could simply carry on with. We wonder why people step back from us even as we burn to share good news — never guessing that the smell of the grave still clings to clothes we have never let Him take off. He did not raise you so you could stand in the daylight still bound. The same voice that called you out is saying, gently, let Me remove these.

lifeApplication: |
  Name one grave cloth today — a single habit, a grudge, a way of speaking that no longer belongs to the person Christ has made you. You do not have to tear it off by sheer force of will. Bring it into the light and let Him begin to loosen it, one honest prayer at a time.

  And when His quiet conviction comes — maybe you shouldn't speak like that, maybe you could do this differently — receive it not as an accusation but as the kindness of hands unwrapping you, setting you free to move.
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, thank You that You called me out of death and into life.

  I confess the old grave clothes I have kept wrapped around me long after You raised me.

  Have Your way; loosen what still binds me, even the things I have been afraid to let go of.

  Thank You that Your conviction is never condemnation but the gentle work of setting me free.

  Let me walk out of this week freer than I walked into it.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
