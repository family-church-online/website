reflection: |
  There was a white shirt I loved and had quietly given up on. It carried a stain I had fought for a long time — every modern spray and powder, every trick I knew, and still the mark sat there, faint but stubborn, refusing to leave. I couldn't bring myself to throw it out. We keep the things we love, stain and all, telling ourselves we'll work it out one day. Then someone handed me a rough bar of old farmer's soap, the kind boiled down from animal fat and lye. This one was made from lamb's fat, and oddly it smelled less like soap than like a Sunday roast. I worked it into the fabric by hand, scrubbing, getting my fingers dirty in it — and the stain that nothing else could touch finally lifted, and the shirt came clean.
  
  There is a strange thing at the heart of that white-robed multitude. We are told they washed their robes and made them white — in blood. Blood. The very thing that stains more stubbornly than almost anything, and here it is named as the one thing that washes a robe spotless. It is a deliberate shock, meant to stop us short and make us look. And it will not let us keep our sin at a comfortable distance. It is easy to say, lightly, "I'm a sinner, I've sinned, I'm forgiven" — generic, tidy, hands clean. But that is not washing. That is not working with the stain.
  
  These people did the harder, dirtier thing. They pulled their sin apart and looked right into it — the words they had spoken, the lies they had told, the quiet injuries done to others — and they let themselves feel the full awfulness of it, and the hopelessness of ever scrubbing it out on their own. And only then, having stared at a mark they could not shift, did they come to the blood of the Lamb and find it clean. That is why their robes are so dazzling. The joy of being washed belongs to those who first knew they were dirty. Had I simply dropped that shirt in the machine and it come out clean, I'd have thought nothing of it. Because I had fought the stain for years, the clean cloth was a wonder. Let yourself see the real depth of your sin — not to despair, but so that His cleansing can become the wonder it truly is.

lifeApplication: "Sometime today, do the uncomfortable thing. Instead of a quick, general \"forgive me,\" pray: Lord, show me my stains. I don't want to look, but I need to. Name the specific thing — the actual word, the actual lie, the actual wound you caused — and let yourself feel its weight rather than waving it off. Then bring it, exactly as it is, to the blood of the Lamb. Getting your hands dirty with your own sin is not morbid; it is the road to the deepest joy there is — the joy of a stain you could never remove being lifted clean away."
prayer: |
  Lord, show me my stains — the ones I have learned to keep at a safe, generic distance.
  
  I don't like to look, and I don't want to, but I need to. Help me to face them honestly.
  
  Thank You that the blood of the Lamb washes clean what nothing else could ever touch.
  
  Wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.
  
  Give me the deep joy of knowing exactly what You have forgiven.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
