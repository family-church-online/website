reflection: |
  Watch a small child at play and you will see someone who believes, with his whole body, that he cannot be hurt. He leaps from the top step, tears down the hill, throws himself into the game as though the ground were soft and the world could not touch him. Watch him grow, and the fearlessness lingers a while into those teenage years on the sports field, where every young body seems certain it will last forever.

  Then life begins, quietly, to teach us otherwise. We learn how thin the thread really is — that a single mosquito can carry death, that one small bubble in a vein can stop a heart, that a foot slipping from a pedal at speed is all it takes. Most of us are handed, sooner or later, a gift we never wanted: the diagnosis on the page, the near-miss on the road, the moment we realise how quickly it could all have been over. We would give anything to hand it back. And yet it tells us something we badly need to hear.

  Job said it plainly: we dwell in houses of clay, crushed as easily as a moth between morning and evening. We spend so much of our strength insisting we are made of sterner stuff — head down, push through, one more wall. But that pretence is not courage; it is the very thing that keeps us running until the day we fall. The bravest and most freeing words we may ever say are also the hardest: I am not bulletproof. I cannot carry this. For only the one who admits he is made of clay will ever reach for the hands that can actually hold him.

lifeApplication: |
  Where are you still pretending to be made of iron? Perhaps you are carrying a diagnosis, a fear, or an exhaustion you will not name to anyone, least of all yourself, because naming it feels like defeat. But the jar of clay was never meant to hold itself together.

  Today, instead of running at one more wall, try setting your hand flat against your own chest and feeling the heartbeat you did not command and cannot guarantee. Let it remind you how held your whole life already is. To admit you are fragile is not to fall apart; it is to finally stop pretending you are the one keeping yourself alive.
prayer: |
  Lord, I have worn myself out pretending to be strong, and I am tired of running at walls.

  Thank You that You know I am dust, and that You love this house of clay anyway.

  Forgive me for the pride that calls pretending "strength" and refuses to admit my need.

  Teach me to number my days, to hold my life gently, and to treasure the breath You give.

  When I am afraid of how fragile I am, remind me Whose hands I am already resting in.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
