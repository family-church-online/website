reflection: |
  You are lying in a hospital bed, and the reports are spread out where you can see them — the scans, the numbers, the careful faces of the people reading them. Fear does its slow work in a room like that. You find yourself counting how bad it might be, how quickly everything could end, how little of it is in your hands. And then, somewhere beneath the fear, a quieter thing begins to rise. You are still breathing. Your heart, which you have never once told to beat, is beating still. And the thought arrives almost like a whisper: He has got me. He has been holding me all along.

  Scripture says of the Son that He "upholds the universe by the word of His power." He did not simply make the world and step back to watch it run. He did not save you and then leave you to keep yourself saved. This very moment, the reason your lungs fill and your heart keeps its rhythm is that He is speaking power into you, breath by breath, holding together what would otherwise fall to dust.

  We so easily imagine that staying alive is our own anxious achievement. But look back over your life — every danger you never saw, every night you were sure you could not survive, and somehow morning came. You are reading these words because Someone has been sustaining you the whole way, without pause, without tiring. And if He is the One keeping you alive, moment by moment, then perhaps you can loosen your white-knuckled grip on keeping yourself alive. The monitor's steady beat is not your accomplishment. It is His faithfulness, made audible.

lifeApplication: |
  You do not need a hospital bed to learn this. Pause for a moment, wherever you are, and simply notice your own breathing — the breath going in and out without your permission, the pulse you are not managing. Every one of them is being given to you, right now, by a God who has not looked away for an instant.

  Whatever weighs on you today, set it down for the length of three slow breaths and remember: the One holding the universe together is holding you too. You are not keeping yourself alive. You never were. And that is very good news.
prayer: |
  Father, I confess how tightly I grip my own life, as though its every beat depended on me.

  Thank You that You uphold all things by Your word, and that You are upholding me even now.

  Thank You for every breath I did not earn and every danger You carried me through unseen.

  When fear tells me it all rests on my shoulders, remind me Whose hands are really beneath me.

  Teach me to rest in the One who has never once stopped sustaining me.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
