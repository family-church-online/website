reflection: |
  In the middle of a merciless desert, where the rock throws the heat back at you, there is a place called Ein Gedi — a thread of spring water slipping out of the ground, and around it life clings on. Wild ibex come there for the water, sure-footed mountain goats that belong to the cliffs. There is a photograph taken at that spring of a single ibex standing on the narrow steel edge of a signpost — all four hooves gathered onto a strip of metal no wider than a hand, perfectly balanced, entirely at home in a place that looks impossible to stand at all.

  That is the picture the prophet leaves us with, at the end of everything failing. After the fig tree that will not blossom and the fields that yield no food, after naming every reason to lie down in the dust, Habakkuk says: "God, the Lord, is my strength; He makes my feet like the deer's; He makes me tread on my high places." Notice what he does not say. He does not say God drags me through the deepest valley on my belly and I barely crawl out alive. He says God gives me the feet of a deer to run on the heights. We were not made to grovel along the ground.

  A season of merely surviving can teach the soul to crawl, until we forget we were ever meant to do anything else. But the same God who holds us in our weakness does not intend to leave us curled up and small. He means to set us on the high places, poised and unafraid where once we could not imagine standing. It will not happen in a single leap. It begins with one step — one small, frightening step out of the shell, into something that scares you a little because it is life and not merely survival. Take it, and you will find the feet He promised were already beneath you.

lifeApplication: |
  What is the one small step out of the shell for you today? Not the whole mountain — just the first edge. A phone call to someone you noticed looked downcast. A door you have been afraid to open. A hope you have not let yourself hope. Do one small thing that stretches you toward life, and guard what you feed your mind while you do it — turn away from the endless stream of what is grim and frightening, and fill your thoughts instead with whatever is true and lovely and worthy of praise.

  Strength rarely arrives all at once; it comes as you step. God gives just enough to lift your head, then enough to stand, then enough to run. Put one hoof on the edge. He will hold you there.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You did not save me to leave me crawling through the dust.

  You are my strength; make my feet like the deer's and teach me to stand on the high places.

  Give me the courage today to take one small step out of the shell, toward life and not just survival.

  Guard my mind; help me to turn from what is dark and to dwell on what is true and good and lovely.

  Strengthen me step by step — enough to lift my head, enough to rise, enough to run — and hold me on the edge.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
