keyScripture:
  ref: "Revelation 8:10–11"
  text: "...The name of the star is Wormwood. A third of the waters became wormwood, and many people died from the water, because it had been made bitter."
reflection: |
  Wormwood is a real plant, and anyone who has tasted it does not forget it. It yields a substance so intensely bitter that it can poison; its very name has become another word for bitterness itself. Now imagine a world where a blazing star falls from the sky and touches the rivers and the springs — the clear, cold, life-giving water that people depend on — and turns it to wormwood. Picture kneeling at a stream you have drunk from all your life, cupping the water to your mouth, and recoiling: it is bitter, undrinkable, and the water that was meant to give life now carries death.
  
  It is a terrible picture, and it is no accident that bitterness is the thing the water becomes. Long before this, God had used that very word to describe a human heart turning away from Him — He warned His people to watch for "a root bearing poisonous and bitter fruit," meaning a heart that leaves Him to serve other gods. In His telling, idolatry and bitterness grow from the same root. When we abandon the fountain of living water and dig our own cracked wells instead, what we draw up turns bitter in us. The sweetness the world promised slowly sours, and we cannot understand why the things we chased have left such a bitter taste.
  
  And yet — this is the part we so easily miss — even a judgment as bitter as poisoned water is, underneath, an act of mercy. God lets the counterfeit wells taste as bitter as they truly are, because as long as they still taste sweet we will never leave them. The bitterness is not Him abandoning us; it is Him calling us home, making the false springs undrinkable so that we will finally turn and look for the only water that has ever satisfied. He would rather we taste this bitterness now and live than drink our sweet poison contentedly all the way into eternity. The bitter cup, strange as it sounds, is grace.

lifeApplication: |
  If something you once chased has turned bitter in your mouth — a pleasure that has stopped pleasing, a pursuit that has left you emptier than before — do not simply grit your teeth and go looking for a sweeter version of the same thing. Let the bitterness do its merciful work.
  
  Treat it as a signpost rather than a dead end, pointing you away from the broken well and back toward the living water. Come and drink from the only spring that has never once turned bitter: God Himself, offered to you in Jesus.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that even the bitter things in my life can be Your mercy, calling me home.
  
  Forgive me for the cracked wells I have dug for myself instead of coming to You.
  
  Where life has turned bitter, help me to see Your hand drawing me back, and not only my loss.
  
  Thank You that You are the fountain of living water that never runs dry and never turns bitter.
  
  Let me come and drink deeply of You today, and find You truly satisfying.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
