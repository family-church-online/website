reflection: |
  Walk through a school at the start of the day and you will see it: a child of about eight, quietly unhappy because the water flask in her bag is the wrong one. It works perfectly well. It holds water exactly as it should. But it does not have the right name printed on the side, and so, to her, it is not good enough. Somewhere she has already absorbed the message, deep down where she cannot even see it working: what I have is not enough — I want the one that she has. She is eight years old, and already it is in her.
  
  We smile at that, but we should be slower to, because the same ache runs straight through us and out the other side. We have built entire industries upon it. Advertising and marketing exist, at heart, to do one thing — to make us want what we do not have, to make the thing we already own feel a little shabby beside the thing we could own instead. A thousand images a day, each quietly whispering: not enough, not enough, you would be happier with more. And it works. It works on children with water flasks, and it works on us with cars and houses and holidays and the shape of someone else's life.
  
  The trouble is that covetousness never actually arrives anywhere. The wanting does not stop when we get the thing; it simply moves on to the next thing and begins again. You cannot buy your way to the far side of it. The only way out runs in a different direction entirely — in learning to turn from the endless comparing, to receive what is already in our hands as a gift, and to find, in God Himself, the "enough" that no purchase has ever been able to give. Contentment is not more. Contentment is Him.

lifeApplication: |
  This week, try to catch the whisper in the act. When an advertisement, a screen, or a neighbour's new thing stirs that familiar flicker of not enough, pause and name it for what it is. Then, deliberately, turn the thought around: instead of reaching for the thing you lack, give thanks for something already in your hands.
  
  Gratitude is the one thing covetousness cannot survive. The more honestly you count what God has already given, the quieter the whisper becomes.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You for the many good things You have already placed in my hands.
  
  Forgive me for the restless wanting that is never satisfied, however much I am given.
  
  Open my eyes to the ways I am being taught to covet, and help me to see through them.
  
  Teach me the secret of contentment — to find my "enough" in You, and not in more.
  
  Turn my comparing into thankfulness, and my wanting into worship.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
