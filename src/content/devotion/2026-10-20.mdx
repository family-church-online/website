reflection: |
  A young man is on his knees in the dust while the stones come down. They are thrown by people who were his neighbours, his fellow worshippers, and at the edge of the crowd a younger man named Saul holds the coats of those doing the throwing. You would expect such a man, with his last strength, to curse them. Instead he lifts his voice and prays, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them." And then he is gone.
  
  Hold that picture beside the cry of the souls under the altar: how long before You will judge and avenge our blood? At first the two seem to clash. One prays for mercy on his killers; the others seem to ask for their punishment. But listen more closely. These are not people consumed by the desire to settle their own scores. They are people who, like the man among the stones, have already forgiven — and who now leave the great weight of justice in the only hands clean enough to carry it. "Vengeance is Mine, I will repay, says the Lord."
  
  We feel the ache they feel. We watch the powerful grow fat while they lie to the poor and bleed them dry. We watch the plainly guilty walk free. Something in us rises up and says, where is justice, and when is it coming? That ache is not sinful; it is the echo of a holy longing God Himself planted in us.
  
  The difference is what we do with the weight. We can clutch it and let it curdle into bitterness, or we can hand it over. A heart can long with all its might for God's justice and still refuse all personal vengeance — the man among the stones proved it is possible. He could pray for mercy precisely because he had entrusted the judging to God.

lifeApplication: |
  Few of us must decide whether to avenge the death of someone we love. But most of us are carrying something smaller and no less real — the harsh words spoken over us, the exclusion from the group, the blame we did not earn. The temptation is to rehearse the comeback, to keep the wound warm so we never forget who did it.
  
  Try instead what the man among the stones did. Name the wrong honestly before God, lay it in His hands as something He has promised to settle perfectly, and then do the harder thing — ask Him to be merciful to the very person who hurt you. It is still hard to turn the other cheek. But when you remember you are not the judge, your hands are suddenly free.
prayer: |
  Sovereign Lord, holy and true, thank You that You see every injustice and that not one of them escapes You.
  
  I hand You the wrong that was done to me; it is too heavy for me to carry and too important for me to settle.
  
  Free my heart from bitterness and the appetite for revenge.
  
  Teach me to pray for mercy over those who have hurt me, as Your servants have always done.
  
  Hasten the day of Your perfect and final justice.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
