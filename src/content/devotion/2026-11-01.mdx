reflection: |
  Think of the first thing your hand reaches for in the morning. For many of us it is the phone, and within seconds the thumb is already moving — headline after headline, each one heavier than the last. War. Disaster. Cruelty dressed up as cleverness. Love, as the old warning put it, growing cold. You can feel something in your chest tighten before your feet have even touched the floor.
  
  It is possible to spend so many hours soaking in the world's fear that we have almost none left for the voice that casts fear out. The sobering truth is that things in the world are hard, and they may grow harder still. But the answer was never to look away and pretend. The answer is in where we choose to spend our minutes — more of them in the Word than in the news, more of them in prayer than in the endless scroll that leaves us anxious and empty.
  
  Because endurance is not the same thing as merely surviving. The promise is not made to the one who grits their teeth and hangs on, grey and joyless, until the end. It is made to the one who keeps living and keeps loving and keeps sowing. And some of that sowing is quieter than we think — a kind word, a small seed of truth dropped into someone's life almost in passing, that may lie dormant for years and then, in some dark and desperate season, finally take root and grow.
  
  None of us earned our place in this hope. Grace was a gift, undeserved, freely given — which means the person beside us, and the stranger who has never once heard it, has exactly the same claim upon it that we did: none, and all. So we endure not with clenched fists but with open hands, holding fast to a joy the headlines cannot touch, scattering seed as we go, longing to make heaven crowded. The night may be loud, but it is not the end of the story. The One who holds it all is still on the throne, and those who are His will be with Him.

lifeApplication: |
  Notice, this week, how you spend your first waking minutes and your last ones before sleep. If the scroll of bad news is quietly feeding an anxiety that prayer never gets the chance to answer, change the order. Give the Word the first word. Let it be the voice that frames your day.
  
  And then sow something. Speak the kind word. Drop the small seed of truth into a conversation without needing to see it grow. Pray, by name, for someone who is far from God. You may never watch the harvest — the seed you plant today could take root in a season you never see — but you are not called to produce the harvest, only to keep scattering, with open hands and an unhurried joy, right to the end.
prayer: |
  Father, forgive me for giving my fears more of my attention than I give to You.
  
  Help me to spend more of my minutes in Your Word than in the noise of the world.
  
  Make me faithful to sow seeds of truth and kindness, even when I never see them grow.
  
  I lift up those I love who do not yet know You — draw them to Yourself, Lord.
  
  Thank You that You are still on the throne, and that those who endure will be with You.
  
  Give me the boldness to keep living and loving and sharing, right to the very end.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
