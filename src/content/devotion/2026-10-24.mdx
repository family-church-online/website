reflection: |
  The wedding hall is full — light and music, long tables, laughter rising to the rafters. The host moves among his guests, and then he stops at one man. "Friend, how did you get in here without wedding clothes?" The man has nothing to say. He had slipped in just as he was, in whatever he happened to be wearing, and now, exposed in the middle of all that brightness, he is put out into the dark.
  
  It is an uncomfortable scene, and it is meant to be, because it asks a question we would rather avoid: by what right do any of us expect to belong at that feast? What would we be wearing?
  
  Now set that man beside the souls under the altar — the ones who had given everything, down to their very lives. You might expect them, of all people, to have earned their place, to arrive with a garment stitched by their own faithfulness. But that is not what happens. They are each given a white robe. Given. Not earned, not sewn in a panic at the last moment, not scraped together out of their own goodness — simply handed to them, clean and pure, washed white by Another.
  
  And then comes a word that sounds almost strange after such suffering: rest a little longer. Here is the quiet heart of it. You cannot dress yourself for this feast. The robe is a gift, and because it is a gift and not an achievement, you can finally stop striving. You do not have to prove you are worthy to be in the room. You are made righteous. The garment is already on your shoulders. So be still, and rest.

lifeApplication: |
  Somewhere in you, perhaps, there is a needle still moving — the quiet, exhausting project of sewing your own garment. Being good enough. Doing enough. Earning the right to be at peace with God by the sheer effort of your performance. It never quite finishes, and it never quite covers you.
  
  Lay down the needle. The robe you have been trying so hard to make has already been made for you, washed clean at a cost you did not pay. And if this week has been one long striving — proving, achieving, holding yourself together — hear the word spoken over those who had given everything: rest a little longer. You are already clothed. You are already welcome at the feast.
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You for the white robe I could never have made for myself.
  
  Forgive me for all the ways I try to earn my place at Your table.
  
  Wash me clean, and cover me in the righteousness that is Yours and not my own.
  
  Teach me to stop striving and to rest in what You have already given.
  
  Thank You that the wedding feast is coming, and that You have prepared a place for me there.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
