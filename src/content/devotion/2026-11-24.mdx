reflection: |
  For most of the year, a sport you enjoy barely crosses your mind. The season is over, the field is quiet, and you can go weeks without a single thought about it. Then the matches begin again. The games appear on the television, the scores come up in conversation, and before long it has quietly moved to the front of your mind — you find yourself thinking about it, talking about it, planning your Saturday around it. Nothing about the game itself changed. What changed is that it came into view.
  
  Our attention works like this with almost everything. When the whole family is well, illness is the furthest thing from our thoughts; the moment one person falls sick, it is suddenly all we can think about. When the money is covered and life is cruising along, we hardly consider it; let one unexpected bill arrive and it fills the whole horizon. We are wired to notice what presses against us now and to forget what is not urgent. It is a kind of survival instinct, and in its place it is useful.
  
  But that same instinct carries a cost. Because we attend only to what is in front of us, the things that matter most — the quietest, least urgent things on an ordinary day — can go unconsidered for weeks. We can live a long time without once lifting our eyes to God, to eternity, to the state of our own soul, simply because nothing in the day forced the subject. And here is what is so easy to miss: the most important realities are usually the ones that make the least noise. They will not force themselves into view. We have to choose, deliberately, to set our minds there — to turn our gaze upward before the day's loud and urgent things turn it away.

lifeApplication: |
  You already know how to bring something into view, because you do it without effort for the things you love. Do it on purpose for the things that matter most. Build one quiet moment into your day that the urgent things cannot crowd out — the first minutes of the morning, a pause before a meal, the last thought before sleep — and turn your mind, deliberately, to God.
  
  Not because a crisis forced you to, but precisely because none has. The soul that is tended in the calm is the soul that stands steady when the storm finally comes.
prayer: |
  Father, forgive me for how easily the loud and urgent things of my day push You out of view.
  
  Thank You that You are near even on the days I forget to look for You.
  
  Teach me to set my mind on the things that are above, and not only on what is in front of me.
  
  Help me to build quiet moments with You that nothing can crowd out.
  
  Keep my eyes lifted to what is eternal, so that I am never caught unready.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
