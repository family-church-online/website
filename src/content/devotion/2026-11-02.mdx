reflection: |
  It is the middle of the night in a garden full of olive trees, and a crowd is coming through the dark with torches and lanterns and weapons. A detachment of soldiers, temple officers, Judas walking at the front. They have come expecting to corner a frightened Galilean teacher and drag Him away before the city wakes.
  
  Jesus does not hide. He steps toward them and asks whom they are looking for. "Jesus of Nazareth," they say. And He answers with three quiet words: "I am He." No sword is drawn. No blow is struck. And yet these trained, armed men stumble backward and fall to the ground — flattened by nothing more than the voice of the One they came to arrest.
  
  We grow so comfortable with the gentle Jesus — the shepherd who gathers lambs, the friend who eats with sinners, the One patient with our slowness and tender toward our wounds. And He is all of that; we are not wrong to love Him for it. But the same lips that would soon say "Father, forgive them" are the lips that laid grown soldiers in the dirt without lifting a hand. The Lamb and the Lion are not two Jesuses. They are one.
  
  Here is what the garden teaches us: the tenderness of Jesus is not weakness, and His gentleness with us is a choice, not a limit. The One who stoops to carry you is the same One before whom armies cannot keep their feet. And when we see Him whole — merciful and mighty, Lamb and Lion — our fear of everything else begins to shrink. We need never be afraid of the One who loves us. We need only stand in awe of Him.

lifeApplication: |
  When you pray today, notice which Jesus you are picturing. Many of us have quietly shrunk Him down to only the parts that soothe us, and in doing so we have made Him smaller than He is — and ourselves more fearful than we need to be. The Jesus who is gentle enough to carry your worst day is strong enough that no illness, no enemy, no circumstance can overrule Him.
  
  Let the scene in the garden change how you walk into this week. The soldiers fell because they met the living God and did not know Him. You get to meet the same Jesus and call Him Lord. Bring Him your fears, but bring them to the whole Christ — the Lamb who was slain for you and the Lion whom nothing can stand against.
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, thank You that the voice that laid soldiers in the dust is the same voice that gently calls me by name.
  
  Forgive me for the times I have made You small, keeping only the parts of You that comfort me and forgetting Your holiness and power.
  
  Help me to hold the whole picture of You — Lamb and Lion, merciful and mighty.
  
  Let my awe of You grow until it crowds out my fear of everything else.
  
  I thank You that the One who loves me is the One no power can overrule.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
