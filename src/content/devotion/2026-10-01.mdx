reflection: |
  It is a startling thing to weep in a room full of glory. John is standing in the throne room of heaven — light like diamond and ruby and emerald, angels calling holy, holy, holy, elders falling on their faces — and he breaks down. Not a quiet sniffle, not a passing sadness. He weeps loudly, the kind of grief that bends a body double. Because the search has been made and no one is worthy, and the weight of that crashes down on him.
  
  His tears are not really his alone. Think of how many thousands of years people have wept exactly like that — at gravesides, staring at a place where a loved one used to be; beside a hospital bed, knowing what is coming; over a shattered marriage, a child who has walked away, a betrayal from the very person held closest. The loudest weeping always seems to come from the hands of the ones we love most. No one is spared it.
  
  And here is the thing worth sitting with: John's grief is the grief of all creation gathered into one man's cry, and it is being heard in the one place where it can actually be answered. The tears are not the end of the scene. They are the sound of longing for Someone worthy to step in — and that longing is about to be answered with a day when every tear is wiped away and death is no more.

lifeApplication: "If you are carrying grief today, you do not need to dress it up or shrink it to fit a room full of light. Bring the loud version — the real weight of it — to God, who does not flinch at it. And let your tears become a longing rather than only a wound: a homesick ache for the day He has promised, when His own hand will wipe your face dry. Name that hope out loud today, even through the tears."
prayer: |
  Father, You know the weeping I have done and the weeping I still carry.
  
  Thank You that You do not turn from my loud grief but gather it up.
  
  Turn my tears into longing for the day You have promised.
  
  I hold on to Your word that You will wipe every tear from my eyes.
  
  Come quickly, Lord Jesus. In Your name, Amen.
