reflection: |
  It is the darkest night Egypt has ever known, and in one small house a father is doing something strange. He has taken a bunch of hyssop, dipped it into a basin filled with the blood of a lamb, and he is painting it across the top of the doorframe and down both sides. Inside, the family waits. They do not go out. They have been told to stay behind that marked door through the night while something terrible moves across the land. And as death passes from house to house, it comes to this doorway, sees the blood, and passes over. Inside, they are spared — not because they were braver or better than their neighbours, but because they were covered.
  
  Centuries later, the same pattern appears again. Before a season of great judgment is permitted to fall, God gives a command: let no harm begin until His servants have been sealed, marked on their foreheads, set apart and protected. First the people are marked; then, and only then, is the storm allowed to break. It is the same picture drawn twice — a God who, before anything else, makes certain that His own are covered and kept.
  
  It would be easy to read all of this as distant history and miss that we are standing inside the very same story. For the mark that saves has never been our goodness, our effort, or our record. It has always been blood — now the blood of the Lamb of God, poured out for us. If you belong to Him, you are not a person anxiously hoping to be good enough to survive the night. You are a person already covered, already sealed, already passed over. The door of your life bears a mark you did not paint yourself, and because of it, you are safe.

lifeApplication: |
  When fear comes — fear of judgment, fear of death, fear that you are somehow not safe with God — do not answer it by hurrying to prove yourself. Answer it the way that family behind the marked door did: by trusting the blood. You cannot paint the mark yourself, and you do not need to; Christ has already done it.
  
  Rest there. Let the quiet security of being covered change how you move through an anxious day. A sealed and sheltered people can afford to be calm, and gentle, and unafraid.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You did not leave me to survive the night by my own strength.
  
  Thank You for the blood of Jesus, the Lamb, that covers the door of my life.
  
  When I am afraid that I am not safe with You, remind me that I am already marked, already sealed.
  
  Forgive me for the times I try to earn a protection You have already given me freely.
  
  Let me walk through this day in the calm of one who is sheltered and kept.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
