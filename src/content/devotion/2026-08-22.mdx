reflection: |
  Sometimes, hovering just above a reef, a diver feels the whole ocean begin to breathe. A swell rolls in and pushes him forward over the coral; then it turns and drags him back the way he came. Forward and back, forward and back, like a child's swing laid flat on the water. Fight it — kick against the pull, strain toward where you want to be while the sea hauls you the other way — and you will wear yourself out and go nowhere. But there is another way. You wait. When the surge carries you forward, you kick with it; when it pulls you back, you simply let it, resting until the next swell comes. And so, almost without effort, you cross the reef.

  So much of our worry is that exhausting fight against the pull. The circumstance drags us backward and we thrash toward the shore we long for, straining and striving and sleeping badly, certain that if we stop pushing for even a moment all will be lost. Paul knew that fight, and he points to a different way through: "Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God." Not by clenching harder. By handing the swell over — and by giving thanks in the middle of it.

  Thanksgiving is what loosens our grip. The more we name the good that is still here — that we can see, that we are loved, that the Son of God gave His life for us — the less the backward pull can frighten us. And then comes the promise: a peace that surpasses all understanding, standing guard over our hearts and minds like a sentry at the door. Joy was never the absence of the waves. It is the quiet art of stopping the fight — letting God carry you forward when He moves, and resting when He does not, until you find you have crossed water you could never have fought your way through.

lifeApplication: |
  Is there a swell dragging you backward right now — a worry you have been kicking against so long your soul is exhausted? Today, try ceasing the fight. Take the very thing you keep straining over, name it honestly to God, and then, before you ask for anything, begin to give thanks. Thank Him for one small mercy, then another, then another, until the list outgrows the worry.

  You are not pretending the wave isn't there; you are letting yourself be carried instead of torn apart by it. Rest when the surge pulls back. Move when it carries you forward. And let His peace stand guard over the parts of you that have not slept in weeks.
prayer: |
  Father, I am worn out from fighting the pull of things I cannot change.

  Teach me to stop thrashing, to hand the swell over to You, and to let You carry me.

  Thank You that I can see, that I am loved, that Your Son gave His life for me — there is always something to bless You for.

  Where anxiety has robbed my sleep and my joy, let Your peace stand guard over my heart and mind.

  Help me to move when You move me and to rest when You do not, trusting You with the whole crossing.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
