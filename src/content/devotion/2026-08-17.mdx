reflection: |
  A snail sets out across a wide road, and halfway over, the ground begins to tremble. A car roars past, its wheel missing the small shell by inches. And the snail does the only wise thing it knows: it draws itself in and waits, tucked away in the dark, listening for the danger to pass. Another car comes. Better to stay in. And another. Safer not to move at all.

  There is nothing foolish about that shell. It was made for exactly this — for the moment the wheel comes close. When life turns relentless and every day asks more than we have, we learn to do the same. We pull in. We stop planning anything new, stop hoping for anything bright, and narrow our whole world down to the single task of getting through today. It is a mercy to be able to do that. Sometimes it is the only way through a hard season.

  But a shell was only ever meant for the passing car. The snail that never comes out again does not stay safe; it slowly starves in the very place that once protected it. And this is the quiet danger for us too — that the coping which carried us through the storm becomes the small dark room we forget how to leave. We can grow so practised at merely surviving that we no longer notice we have stopped living. The first mercy God gives is not usually the way out. It is simply the light to see where we are: still curled inside, long after the road went quiet.

lifeApplication: |
  Somewhere today, notice the shell you have been living in. Perhaps it is the invitation you keep declining, the friend you no longer call, the plan you will not let yourself make because hoping feels too costly. You do not have to fling yourself out into the road all at once. For now it is enough to lift your head into the light and admit, gently and without shame, "I have been surviving, and I would like to live again."

  His mercies are new this morning — not next year, not when the season finally turns, but this morning. There is fresh grace waiting at the very edge of your shell.
prayer: |
  Father, I have been hiding longer than I knew, and I am only now lifting my head to the light.

  Thank You that there is no shame in the shell — that You understand how hard the road has been.

  Thank You that Your mercies are new every morning, waiting for me even here.

  Give me the courage to notice one small thing I have stopped doing, and the grace to begin it again.

  Draw me out gently, at Your pace, until I am living and not only surviving.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
