reflection: |
  It is the last night in Egypt, and in a small house a family is doing something strange. A lamb has been killed, and now, with a little bunch of hyssop, they brush its blood across the top and down the sides of their doorframe. Then they go inside, sandals on their feet, staffs in their hands, and eat quickly in the lamplight. Outside, in the darkness over the whole land, death is moving from house to house.
  
  Everything hangs on that smear of blood on the wood. Not the thickness of their walls, not the goodness of the family inside, not anything they have achieved — only a mark on the door that says this house belongs to God, and the destroyer passes them by.
  
  Centuries later, the same pattern appears again. Before the winds of judgment are loosed on the earth, angels are held back with a single instruction: wait until the servants of God have been sealed on their foreheads. First the mark, then the storm. God always marks His own before He lets the night fall.
  
  And here is the part that should bring tears to our eyes: the marked are never the deserving. The families in Egypt were no holier than their neighbors. The ones sealed in Revelation have lied and failed and fallen like the rest of us. The mark is not a prize for being good enough to survive — it is a gift that says, "You are Mine, and I will bring you through." Our safety was never in ourselves. It was always in the blood, and in belonging to Him.

lifeApplication: |
  You may be very aware today of the ways you fall short — the failures you would never say out loud, the quiet sense that if anyone really knew you, you would not make the cut. Hear this gently: the question was never whether you had earned your place. The people behind the blood-marked doors were spared because they belonged, not because they were worthy.
  
  So stop trying to earn what has already been given. If you belong to Jesus, His mark is on you — not a mark you made, but one He placed. Let that settle something in you today. You do not have to live anxiously trying to secure your own safety. You can live from the security He has already given, resting under a mark you did not have to deserve.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You mark Your own and carry them through the night.
  
  Thank You that my safety has never depended on my being good enough, but on the blood of the Lamb and on belonging to You.
  
  Forgive me for the times I have tried to earn what You freely give.
  
  When I am overwhelmed by my own failures, remind me that the mark on me is Yours, not mine.
  
  Help me to rest today in the quiet confidence of belonging to You.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
