  text: "And as they continued to ask him, he stood up and said to them, 'Let him who is without sin among you be the first to throw a stone at her."
reflection: |
  A woman is dragged into the open, caught in the act, and a ring of men has already gathered stones in their fists. The law is on their side; the verdict feels settled. And then one sentence changes the weight of every hand in that circle: let the one without sin throw first. One by one the stones drop to the dust. Not because the woman was innocent, but because not one accuser could claim to be clean enough to be her judge.
  
  There is a rule hidden in that moment, and it reaches far beyond that courtyard. You are only fit to pass judgement if you yourself are without sin. Every hand that reached for a stone had to open and let it fall, because every hand belonged to a sinner. And that leaves a searching question hanging in the air of heaven: if judging rightly requires perfect purity, then who is left? Who could ever open a scroll of judgement without their own guilt disqualifying them first?
  
  Only one hand in all of history never gathered a stone it did not have the right to throw. Only One is holy, innocent, unstained — and so only One can hold the verdict of the world without it condemning Him too. The wonder is not merely that He is qualified to judge. It is that the only Person with every right to throw the stone is the very One who chose instead to take our place.

lifeApplication: "Watch your own hands today for the stones you are quietly holding — the judgement of a family member, a colleague, a stranger who wronged you. Before you throw, remember the circle that emptied out one dropped stone at a time. And then remember the astonishing mercy shown to you by the only One who had the right to condemn and chose not to. Let a stone fall from your grip today."
prayer: |
  Lord, I so easily gather stones to throw at others while forgetting my own guilt.
  
  Thank You that You alone are holy and innocent, the only One fit to judge.
  
  And thank You that having every right to condemn me, You took my place instead.
  
  Loosen my grip on the judgements I am holding against others today.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
