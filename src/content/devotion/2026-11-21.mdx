reflection: |
  Switch on a bright light in a room that has been dark for hours and the first thing you do is flinch. Your hand goes up, your eyes screw shut, and everything in you wants the dimness back just for a moment longer. It is worse still if there are things in that room you would rather not see — the clutter you had stopped noticing, the mess in the corner, the dust on everything. The light has done nothing wrong. It has only shown you what was there all along. But in that first moment the light feels less like a kindness and more like an accusation.
  
  When John explains why the world turned away from Jesus, he does not say that people could not see the light. He says they would not. The light came — not an idea but a Person, full of grace and truth — and the heartbreaking thing is that people loved the darkness more. Not because the dark was better, but because the dark hid what they did not want exposed. They loved their sin before they ever gave Jesus a thought, and so when the light arrived, they shut their eyes to it.
  
  Here is what is easy to miss in our own flinching: the light that exposes is the very same light that heals. We hide our worst corners from God as though His seeing them were the danger — but He already sees, and He comes not to shame us but to clean the room. To step into the light is not to be condemned; it is to be rescued. The only real tragedy is to love the dark so much that we keep our eyes shut to the One who came to save us.

lifeApplication: "Most of us keep a corner or two in shadow — a habit we justify, a resentment we feed, something we would hate to have brought into the open. We imagine that keeping it dark keeps it safe. But the thing we hide in the dark only grows there. Today, name honestly before God the corner you have been keeping unlit, and let Him in. Do not flinch away. He is not standing over you with an accusation; He is standing there with a light, because He means to make the whole room clean. And pray, too, for someone you know who is still choosing the dark — that the light would become, for them, a mercy rather than a threat."
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, You are the light that came into the world, and I confess I have sometimes loved my shadows.
  
  Shine into the corners of my life I have kept unlit, and do not let me flinch away.
  
  Thank You that You expose my sin not to shame me but to heal me.
  
  Give me courage to live in the open before You, with nothing hidden.
  
  Have mercy on those I love who are still choosing the dark, and draw them into Your light.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
