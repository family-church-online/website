reflection: |
  Two small children stand waiting in a supermarket while their parents shop. One of them kicks the other — maybe by accident, maybe not. The kicked one, naturally, kicks back. So the first kicks again, a little harder this time. You kicked me, so I'll kick you.
  
  Back and forth it goes, each blow a shade fiercer than the last, neither willing to let the other have the final word, until all at once there is a tangle of arms and legs against the fridges, something crashes to the floor in the dairy aisle, and two red-faced parents come rushing to pull them apart. We smile at the scene — and then, if we are honest, we recognise ourselves in it.
  
  We tell ourselves our version is more dignified than a child's kick in a shop, but underneath it is the same arithmetic: they hurt me, so I will find a way to hurt them back. Even a grudge quietly nursed is really a plan for someone's harm, a refusal to let the last blow be theirs. And this is the mercy hidden in God saying vengeance belongs to Him. I could kick that shin for the rest of my life and never come close to the justice He is able to bring. Why spend myself settling a score I can never finish, when He has said the account is His to keep?

lifeApplication: "There is probably a name that came to mind as you read — someone you are still, in small and subtle ways, kicking back at. A cool reply. A story you tell about them. A door you keep closed. Today, refuse the next return blow. Do not fire back the clever remark. Do not replay the offence one more time. Hand the whole account to God, who keeps far better records than you do and settles them far more justly, and feel how much lighter your hands are with nothing left to swing."
prayer: |
  Lord, You have seen every wound I carry and every score I am tempted to settle myself.
  
  Forgive me for the grudges I have nursed and the small revenges I have called by gentler names.
  
  Vengeance is Yours, not mine — help me truly to hand the account into Your hands and leave it there.
  
  Free me from the endless trading of blows, and let my hands be open instead of clenched.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
