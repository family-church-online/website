keyScripture:
  ref: "Isaiah 53:7"
  text: "He was oppressed, and He was afflicted, yet He opened not His mouth; like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent, so He opened not His mouth."
reflection: |
  Picture a lamb the way a child's storybook draws one: fleece white as snow, small and timid and calm, trotting along without a sound. Nothing in it resists. When it is taken, it is simply taken. This is the lamb Isaiah saw from centuries away — oppressed, afflicted, led to the slaughter, and silent the whole way. And this is exactly how Jesus went: hauled before Herod, before Pilate, before the high priest, accused and mocked, and He opened not His mouth. No lashing out, no defence, no unmaking of His accusers, though He could have unmade the world. He went quietly, because He knew this was the cup given to Him.
  
  Now hold a second picture beside the first. The same lamb, but no longer white and untouched — standing instead in the throne room of heaven, bearing the marks of one who has been slain. Blood and scars. Not hidden, not healed smooth as though the wound had never happened, but kept. This lamb does not trot along timidly; it stands firm at the centre of everything, and its scars are not shame. They are the evidence. They are the proof held up to everyone who ever doubted the thing could be finished: it is finished. He has conquered.
  
  Between those two pictures lies the whole of the gospel. The silent lamb had to go first — willing, unresisting, saying nothing in His own defence — before the standing lamb could wear those scars like a banner. The victory did not arrive instead of the wounds. It came through them. The marks He could have erased, He chose to keep, because they are the memory of what His love cost and the guarantee that the cost was paid in full.
  
  We tend to want our own scars erased — the failures smoothed over, the painful seasons tidied out of the story as if they never happened. But look at how the risen Lamb carries His. He does not pretend the wound away; He stands in the open with it, and it has become the very thing that gives others hope. What was done to Him in silence, He now holds up as proof. And the marks of our own deepest suffering, surrendered to Him, may yet become the clearest evidence we can offer that He is able to bring anyone all the way through.

lifeApplication: "Is there a scar in your story that you keep carefully covered — a failure, a grief, a season you would rather pretend had never happened? We spend a great deal of energy keeping such things smooth and out of sight. But consider that the risen Christ did not hide His wounds; He stood in the open and let them speak. The hardest chapter of your life, surrendered to Him, is often the very place another struggling person finds hope — not a polished account of a life that never broke, but the honest evidence of One who carried you through the breaking. You do not have to hide what He has healed. Held up to Him, even the scars can preach."
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that You went silently to the slaughter, opening not Your mouth, because You knew it was the cup the Father had given You.
  
  Thank You that You kept Your scars — that You stand in glory still bearing the proof of what Your love cost.
  
  Teach me to trust that You bring victory through wounds and not only instead of them.
  
  Take the scars in my own story that I keep hidden, and make them, in Your hands, a place where others find hope.
  
  Help me to entrust myself to You, as You entrusted Yourself to the Father who judges justly.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
