reflection: |
  A fire is burning in a courtyard, and a man is warming his hands at it. Only hours earlier he had sworn he would die before he would ever leave his friend. Now a servant girl looks hard at him, and then another, and a third time the question comes, and three times he says it: I do not know the man. Not far off, the friend he is denying stands abandoned — the rest of His companions already scattered into the dark, fled the moment the soldiers closed in. The most important night of His life, and every single person who loved Him has left.
  
  We forget how much of that He absorbed. He was run out of His own hometown. He watched His Father's house turned into a marketplace and felt the anger rise. He was betrayed with a kiss, deserted by His friends, denied by the boldest of them, and left utterly alone. Rejection, grief, exhaustion, physical agony, the particular ache of being let down by the people closest to you — there is no room in the whole house of human suffering that He did not enter.
  
  And yet, when we are hurting, we so often imagine Him as a figure on a high and distant pedestal — holy, certainly, but far off, presiding rather than understanding. We carry our pain with the quiet certainty that no one, least of all a perfect God, could really know what it feels like from the inside. So we pray upward, toward Someone we half suspect is too far above it all to feel it with us.
  
  But the God we are praying to is the One who was left alone in the courtyard. When we come to Him and say please help, He does not glance down and shrug, that's a you problem — the distant gods of other stories do that. This One leans in and says, I know. I have been there. He was tempted in every way we are, and never once from the safety of a comfortable distance. The remoteness we feel is our own invention; the truth is that there is no one nearer to our pain than the Man who was acquainted with grief. Whatever you are certain no one understands — He does. Not from a report. From memory.

lifeApplication: "Notice where you turn first when something goes wrong. For most of us the honest order runs: worry about it, try to fix it ourselves, search it online, ask a forum of strangers, run it past everyone we know — and then, somewhere near the bottom of the list, maybe I should pray about it. We save for last the One who knows us best. This week, try reversing the order. Before the search bar, before the group chat, before the five attempts in your own strength, bring it to the One who has been there. He understands your situation more intimately than the wisest advice you could find, because He has felt it from the inside."
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that You are not a distant God on a far-off pedestal, but One who stepped into our own skin and knows our pain from the inside.
  
  Thank You that when I come to You, You never say "that is a you problem" — You say, "I know; I have been there."
  
  Forgive me for the times I have tried everything else first and come to You only when I had run out of options.
  
  Teach me to turn to You first, before the worry and the searching and my own best efforts.
  
  When I am most certain that no one understands, remind me that You do — not from a distance, but from memory.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
