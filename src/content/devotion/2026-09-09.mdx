reflection: |
  Picture the scene. A crowd of respectable, religious city people — educated, careful, sure of themselves — and standing in front of them is a fisherman. His hands are rough. His accent is the broad, unmistakable drawl of the countryside, the kind that makes sophisticated people assume the speaker is simple. And this unpolished man looks them in the eye and says the most confronting thing imaginable: the One you condemned, God has made both Lord and Christ. You did this.

  There are only two ways to receive words like that. One is to bristle — How dare you. Who are you to judge me? The other is what happened that day: they were cut to the heart. Not merely embarrassed, not merely persuaded, but pierced — wounded in the deep place where we keep our self-defence. And out of that wound came the most hopeful question in all of Scripture: "Brothers, what shall we do?"

  We tend to run from that piercing feeling. We treat conviction as though it were condemnation, something to argue away or numb. But the cut is not God rejecting us; it is God reaching us. No one feels that wound unless He is already drawing them near. The sharp ache of knowing the truth about yourself is not the door slamming shut — it is the door being opened. A heart that can still be cut is a heart He has not given up on.

lifeApplication: |
  The next time you feel that inner sting — reading a verse that lands too close, hearing a truth you would rather dodge, catching yourself in something you have been excusing — pay attention to your first reflex. Is it to defend yourself, to explain, to change the subject in your own mind? That instinct to bristle is the one that keeps us stuck.

  Try the other response today. When something cuts you, do not reach for armour. Reach instead for the question those first believers asked: "Lord, what shall I do?" Let the wound do its healing work. A cut that leads you to turn around is a mercy, not an attack.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You love me enough to wound my pride rather than leave me comfortable in my sin.

  Forgive me for the times I have bristled and defended myself when Your word was trying to reach me.

  Give me a heart that can still be cut — soft, honest, unguarded before You.

  And when the cut comes, let it lead me not to shame but to the hopeful question: "Lord, what shall I do?"

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
