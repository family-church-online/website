reflection: |
  Two men are led out of a courtroom after a public flogging. Their backs are raw, their dignity has been stripped in front of a watching crowd, and they have been warned, not for the first time, to be silent. By every ordinary measure they should walk out crushed. Instead there is something almost unbelievable on their faces as they go. They are glad. They are rejoicing — not that the pain was pleasant, but that they had been counted worthy to suffer this dishonour for the name they love.
  
  That is a strange gladness, and it is worth being honest about what it is and is not. It is not pleasure. It is not pretending the lashes did not land, or that humiliation does not sting. It is something deeper and quieter than that — a warmth that rises up from underneath the suffering, carries you, steadies you, and somehow makes it possible to take one more day of the very thing that should have broken you.
  
  Perhaps you have felt it in your own hard seasons: you were hurting, and yet you were being carried, reminded in the middle of it how loved you are and how God will not waste a single tear. That is not a trick of the mind. It is a gift, and a supernatural one.
  
  Here is what makes it so precious. The world can reach your body, your reputation, your comfort — it reached the backs of those two men. But it cannot reach this joy. Joy like that is not the absence of suffering; it is the presence of God down inside the suffering, and no council, no crowd, no enemy has the power to take it from you.

lifeApplication: |
  When something costs you this week because you belong to Jesus — a snub, a closed door, a loss you would not have suffered otherwise — watch your own heart carefully. The instinct is to brace for bitterness or sink into self-pity. But there is another possibility, and it is the one those two men chose on their way out of the courtroom.
  
  Instead of only asking God to remove the hard thing, ask Him for the deeper gift — that quiet, carrying gladness that no one can confiscate. It rarely arrives as a burst of happiness. It comes as a warmth that lets you stand up and face one more day, and it is proof that you are not suffering alone.
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You for a joy that does not depend on my circumstances being easy.
  
  When I suffer for Your name, let that quiet warmth rise up in me and carry me.
  
  Guard me from bitterness and from sinking into self-pity.
  
  Thank You that no one has the power to take this joy away from me.
  
  Carry me through just one more day, and let that be enough.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
