reflection: |
  A sound begins. At first it is far off, and then it is everywhere — like the roar of many waters, like great peals of thunder rolling across the sky. It is the sound of a crowd no one could ever number, and they are all crying out one word together: Hallelujah. The long wait is over. The marriage of the Lamb has come, and the Bride has made herself ready.
  
  Stand in that sound for a moment and look at the faces in the crowd. Who is there? Some of them gave their lives for Him — they died under stones, in prisons, on scaffolds, for refusing to deny His name. And some of them lived long, quiet, unremarkable lives for Him, faithful through decades that no history book will ever record. And here is the wonder: in that roaring multitude there is no distinction between them. The martyr and the quiet servant stand side by side, dressed in the same white, singing the same word.
  
  There is no distinction because the thing that made them His was never how they died or how long they lived. Both belonged to Him, and both always will. "Whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's."
  
  That is where the fear in all of us can finally come to rest. Our security was never in whether we live or die, whether we succeed or suffer, whether our days are many or few. It rests entirely on whose we are. And the proof that He can keep us through anything is this: He Himself died and rose again, so that He might be Lord of both the dead and the living. There is no corner of our existence — this side of the grave or the far side of it — where He is not already Lord, and where we are not already His.

lifeApplication: |
  The week ahead almost certainly holds something you are quietly afraid of — a diagnosis, a conversation, a loss, a cost you are not sure you can bear. Underneath every one of those fears lies a single settled fact that nothing can shake: you belong to Him. Living or dying, you are the Lord's, and that was true before you woke this morning and will be true long after the last of your fears has passed.
  
  Let that free you today, not to huddle in self-protection but to turn outward. Somewhere near you is a person who is afraid, who needs to hear that they are held and that they belong. Carry the joy out the door with you and go and find them. Someone needs the very encouragement you have been given.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that I belong to You, and that nothing in life or in death can change whose I am.
  
  Whether I live or whether I die, I am Yours — quiet my fear of what lies ahead.
  
  Thank You that Jesus died and rose again so that He is Lord over both the dead and the living.
  
  Fill me with the joy of knowing I am kept, and send me out with it.
  
  Lead me to the person this week who needs to hear that they are held and that they belong.
  
  Hasten the day of the marriage supper of the Lamb.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
