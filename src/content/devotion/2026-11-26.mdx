reflection: |
  The people of Egypt worshipped the river. The Nile was not merely water to them; it was a god, the very source of their life — and one morning it ran red as blood, undrinkable, full of death. They revered frogs as sacred, and frogs came up and overran them until they were sick of the sight of them. They bowed to the sun as the greatest of their gods, and a darkness fell so thick that for three days no one could see his neighbour or rise from his place. One by one, the things they had trusted and adored were brought out into the open and shown to be exactly what they were: powerless. Each plague toppled a god.
  
  It would be comforting to read that as the superstition of a distant people, so unlike us. But we bow down too; we are only more polite about it. We hand our hearts to the approval of others, to the figure in our bank account, to our own cleverness, to the stars read in a horoscope, to voices we consult in the place of God. We may never call these things gods, yet we treat them as gods — we look to them for the security, the guidance, and the worth that belong to God alone. And like Egypt's river and sun, they cannot carry the weight we place on them.
  
  Here is the mercy hidden inside that uncomfortable truth. When God exposes an idol, He is not doing it to shame us or to strip us bare. He is doing it to set us free. He loves us too much to let us go on pouring out our lives before things that can never answer, never save, never love us back. He lets the false gods be seen for what they are, so that we will stop bowing to them — and turn, at last, to the One who truly holds our life in His hands.

lifeApplication: |
  It is worth asking, honestly and without flinching, what you actually turn to first. When you are anxious, where does your mind run for comfort before it runs to God? When you need direction, whose voice do you consult? The answer will quietly reveal where your trust is really placed.
  
  You do not have to be afraid of that discovery. Name the thing, hold it up in the light, and let God show you how small it is beside Him — and then hand the weight back to the only One strong enough to hold it.
prayer: |
  Father, You alone are God, and there is no other.
  
  Show me, gently and honestly, the things I have been trusting in Your place.
  
  Forgive me for looking to things that cannot save me for the security only You can give.
  
  Thank You that when You expose my idols, You do it to set me free and not to shame me.
  
  Turn my heart back to You, the living and true God, and let me serve You alone.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
