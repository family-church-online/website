reflection: |
  A door does not usually open on its own. Someone turns a handle, leans a shoulder, lifts a latch. So there is something arresting about a door simply found standing open — no hand upon it, no one stepping back from it, only an opening where a moment before there had been a wall. And beyond it, a voice as clear and bright as a trumpet, saying without hurry, "Come up here."
  
  We spend so much of our lives pushing against doors. The door of a decision that will not come clear. The door of a relationship that stays shut. The door of a question we cannot pry open no matter how hard we lean — why this, why now, why me. We wear ourselves out against handles that will not turn.
  
  But this door was opened by no hand of ours. It was opened from the other side, by the One who holds the key of David, who opens what no one can shut. And notice what the voice does not say. It does not say, "Work it out." It does not say, "Solve the mystery first, and then you may look." It says simply, "Come up here, and I will show you." The invitation is not to understand everything. It is to come and see. Sometimes the most faithful thing we can do is stop straining against the doors we cannot open, and lift our eyes to the One who has already opened the only one that finally matters.

lifeApplication: "Think of the door you have been pushing against this week — the closed situation, the unanswered prayer, the problem you keep turning over in the dark. Just for today, stop pushing. Lift your eyes instead to the door He has opened, the one no one can shut, and accept the simple invitation: come and see. You do not have to understand the whole room before you step toward the light. Let one closed door in your life become, today, a reason to look up rather than to strain."
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You open doors no one can shut, and that the way into Your presence stands open because of Jesus.
  
  Forgive me for wearing myself out against doors You have not opened, and for demanding answers before I will trust You.
  
  Teach me to hear Your gentle "Come up here" above the noise of my worrying.
  
  Lift my eyes today from what I cannot open to the One who has already opened the way.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
