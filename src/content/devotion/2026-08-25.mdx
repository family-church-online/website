  ref: "2 Peter 1:3"
  text: "His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness, through the knowledge of Him who called us to His own glory and excellence."
reflection: |
  There is a reflex almost all of us share. Someone places something good in our hands — and before we have even finished saying thank you, our other hand has already moved toward our pocket. What do I owe you? Let me pay you back. Let me at least cover something. We are so unpractised at receiving that a pure gift makes us uncomfortable; we would rather earn it than simply take it.

  Notice the word: granted. Everything we need for life and godliness has been granted — handed over, deeded to us, signed and settled. Not sold. Not lent. Not offered on terms. When someone grants you something, they do not follow it with a bill. Everything required to be forgiven, to be rescued from the corruption of this world, to be made new — all of it was placed in your hands at the cross, already paid for, in full.

  And still the hand creeps toward the pocket. We try to pay God back with our religious effort, our good behaviour, our anxious striving, as though grace were a loan we are quietly repaying. But the hardest, holiest thing a proud heart can do is to stop reaching for the wallet, open both hands, and receive. Everything has already been given. Our only task is to believe it deeply enough to say thank you.

lifeApplication: |
  Watch your hands today. Watch for the reflex to pay God back — the quiet feeling that you must earn your place, prove your worth, deserve the love you have already been given. Every time you catch it, stop.

  Open your hands instead. Say out loud, if you can: "It is already given. I receive it." Let today be shaped not by the debt you imagine you owe, but by the gift you have already been handed. Gratitude, not payment, is the right response to grace.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You did not sell me my salvation but granted it, freely and in full.

  Forgive me for the times I reach for my wallet, trying to pay back what You gave as a gift.

  Quiet the pride in me that would rather earn than simply receive.

  Teach me to hold out empty, open hands and take what You have already placed there.

  Let my whole life become one long thank You.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
