  text: And being in agony He prayed more earnestly; and His sweat became like great drops of blood falling down to the ground.
reflection: |
  In the garden, in the dark, a man kneels and prays until His body does something almost no one ever witnesses: His sweat turns to blood. It is not a figure of speech. There is a rare and real condition in which extreme anguish causes the tiny vessels around the sweat glands to rupture, so that blood seeps out with the sweat — and it comes only under the most crushing distress a human frame can bear. This is the state Jesus is in before a single soldier has laid a hand on Him. Before the whip, before the nails, before the cross — on His knees in the grass, He is already bleeding from the sheer weight of what is coming.
  
  And here is the thing that stops you. He asks whether, if there is any other way, this cup might pass from Him. We assume it is the torture He dreads — and who would not dread it? But listen to what He says His soul is doing: overwhelmed with sorrow, to the point of death. The agony crushing Him in the garden is not mainly the fear of pain. It is the fear of separation. He has been with the Father from before the beginning, never once apart, and now He is about to be forsaken — to take onto Himself the one thing that cuts a soul off from God. That is what makes Him sweat blood. Not the nails. The distance from His Father.
  
  We know separation only in small, aching pieces, and even those undo us. A mother goes back to work and spends the first morning weeping, unable to bear a few hours away from her child. We put a son or daughter on a plane to study on the far side of the world and feel something tear loose inside. We stand at a graveside, or watch someone we love move away, and discover a pain almost impossible to put into words. Now gather every separation you have ever felt into one, and multiply it past the edge of imagining — an eternity of unbroken nearness, about to be severed. That is the cup He was holding in the garden.
  
  And He would not set it down. Knowing exactly what it would cost — not only the pain He could see, but the far deeper wound of being cut off from His Father — He drank it anyway. He endured the separation so that we would never have to. Every tear of parting we have ever cried, He felt the infinite version of, and chose it, so that nothing could ever finally separate us from the love of God. The blood in the grass of Gethsemane was love doing its most costly arithmetic, and deciding that we were worth it.

lifeApplication: "If you have ever stood in an airport watching someone you love walk toward the gate, or sat in a suddenly too-quiet house after a child has gone, you know a little of what separation does to the human heart. Let that familiar ache become, today, a doorway into worship. The distance you have felt and dreaded is the very thing Jesus walked into on purpose, in its fullest and most terrible form, so that the final separation — being cut off from God forever — could never fall on you. When loneliness presses in, or the fear of being left behind, remember Gethsemane: He was forsaken so that you never will be. Nothing you face can separate you from the love He bled to secure."
prayer: |
  Lord, in the garden You sweat blood under the weight of what was coming, and still You did not turn away.
  
  Thank You that what You dreaded most was the separation from Your Father — and that You bore it so that I never have to.
  
  I can scarcely take in that my own loneliness and loss, in their smallest form, were felt by You in their fullest, and chosen for my sake.
  
  Forgive me for how lightly I have sometimes treated a sacrifice that cost You everything.
  
  When I am afraid of being left or alone, remind me that You were forsaken so that I never will be.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
