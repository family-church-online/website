reflection: |
  Picture a set of weighing scales held out in the open, the kind that measures food by the handful. And hear the price being called out: a full day's wages for a single quart of wheat — about as much as you could cup in two hands, and that still raw, still to be ground and baked before it feeds a single mouth. If you cannot afford the wheat, there is barley at three times the amount, because barley has always been the poor person's grain. And through it all comes a strange instruction: do not touch the oil and the wine. The luxuries of the comfortable are left untouched.
  
  It is a picture of a world where the poor scrape for a mouthful and the well-off barely feel it — and if we are honest, it does not sound as foreign as we would like.
  
  Scarcity has a way of exposing what our security was really resting on. When bread is cheap and the shelves are full, we can believe we are trusting God while we are quietly trusting our savings, our comfort, the unspoken assumption that tomorrow is taken care of. Take that away, set the scales out, and we discover how thin that security always was.
  
  But there is one treasure no scale can weigh out and no shortage can ration. Jesus called Himself the bread of life, and that bread does not run short in a famine. Everything else we lean on can be measured, priced, and taken. He cannot. The one whose life is built on Him still has everything that matters when the scales come out.

lifeApplication: |
  It is easy to say God is enough when the cupboards are full. The real test comes when the fields fail — and the time to prepare for that test is now, while things are still steady.
  
  Start by holding what you have a little more loosely. Give some of it away, deliberately, as a way of telling your own heart that these things are not your security. Practise gratitude for today's bread rather than anxiety about tomorrow's. And when the fear of scarcity rises — over money, over provision, over the future — answer it the way the prophet did, who looked at the empty fields and the barren vines and still said, "yet I will rejoice in the Lord." Not because the loss did not hurt, but because his joy was built on Someone the loss could never touch.
prayer: |
  Thank You that You are the bread of life, and that no famine can ration You.
  
  Forgive me for trusting quietly in my own provision while telling myself I was trusting You.
  
  Teach me to hold what I have loosely and to give freely.
  
  When the fear of scarcity rises in me, steady me with a joy that rests on You alone.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
