reflection: |
  You have to have been genuinely undone by heat to understand what the first rain does to a person. Days of it, nights of it, no cool corner anywhere in the house, sleep that never quite comes. And then, faint at first, a breeze lifts — that particular cool breath of air that arrives just ahead of the rain and promises, before a single drop has fallen, that relief is on its way.
  
  When the downpour finally breaks over you, you do not run from it. You stand out in it. You let it soak you through to the skin, laughing, cooled at last after the long ordeal, and afterward you sleep the deep, easy sleep of someone whose suffering is finally, wonderfully over. It is only rain — and yet it feels like being given your life back.
  
  That soaking relief is the smallest picture of what waits on the far side of everything hard. There is a day coming that Scripture calls the marriage supper of the Lamb — not merely relief, but celebration; a feast, a wedding, a welcome. Imagine the roar of a great stadium of voices singing as one, and then multiply it beyond counting: that is the sound of the redeemed gathered at the table. Tears wiped away, death undone, God Himself dwelling with His people at last. Blessed, we are told, are those who are invited to that supper — and lest we ever doubt it, the promise comes with its own seal: these are the true words of God.

lifeApplication: "If the heat of this life is pressing hard on you right now, hold on for the breeze. The storm of a broken world does end, and what follows is a table set with your name on the invitation. And in the meantime, ask Him only for today: one more day to love the person who is hard to love, one more day to do the good you keep failing to do, one more day lived for His glory with the strength He supplies. The rain is coming. The feast is ready. You are invited by name — so keep going."
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that the sorrow and heat of this world are not the end of the story.
  
  Thank You for the feast You have prepared, the welcome that waits, the tears You will one day wipe away.
  
  When life presses hard, lift my eyes to the relief and the reward that are surely coming.
  
  Give me strength for just today, that I might live this one more day for You and for Your glory.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
