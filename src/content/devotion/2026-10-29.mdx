  ref: "John 2:15–16"
  text: "And making a whip of cords, He drove them all out of the temple, with the sheep and oxen. And He poured out the coins of the money-changers and overturned their tables. And He told those who sold the pigeons, 'Take these things away; do not make My Father's house a house of trade."
reflection: |
  Picture Him crouched on the ground, saying nothing, working a length of cord between His fingers. Strand over strand, slowly, patiently, braiding a whip by hand. It is not quick work. It takes time — long minutes at the very least, time enough for anger to cool if anger were all it was. A few steps away people are still buying and selling. The coins are still clinking. And He simply keeps braiding.
  
  We tend to imagine this scene as a sudden storm — a figure sweeping in, overturning tables in a flash of fury. But look again at the small, telling detail. He saw what was wrong, and then He sat down and made the whip. He did not snatch up the nearest thing and lash out. He chose, deliberately, with His own hands and His own time, exactly what He was going to do.
  
  That is not the anger most of us know. Our anger is the blast of the car horn the instant someone cuts us off. It is the sharp word to a child before we have even finished hearing them out. It is reaction — fast, hot, and gone, leaving wreckage behind. People have even reached for this very scene to excuse exactly that kind of temper, as though the overturned tables gave us licence to lose ourselves.
  
  But His was a different thing altogether. Every strand of that whip was a decision. His anger had sat down, thought, and risen again as something clean — a settled, righteous resolve to put right what was wrong in His Father's house. And here is the comfort hidden inside it: the judgment of God is never a tantrum, never a sudden snap after one offence too many. It is patient. It is considered. It has sat with every single strand. We are never dealing with a God who flies off the handle, but with One who weighs, and waits, and acts in perfect righteousness.

lifeApplication: |
  Think of the last time your anger moved faster than your thought — the horn, the sharp reply, the message sent before you had cooled down. Most of the damage we do to the people we love is done in those unguarded seconds.
  
  There is a world of difference between the wrong being real and our reaction being right. This week, try putting even a small space between what provokes you and what you do about it — the length of a slow breath, a walk to the other room, the time it would take to braid a cord. In that space, ask what love actually requires. Righteous anger does exist, but it sits down first. It thinks. And then it acts to make things right, rather than simply to make itself felt.
prayer: |
  Lord, so much of my anger is reaction — fast, hot, and leaving wreckage behind.
  
  Teach me to be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger.
  
  Give me the grace to sit down before I act, and to weigh my words before I speak them.
  
  Where there is real wrong, let me respond the way You did — deliberately, and in love.
  
  Thank You that Your justice is never a loss of temper, but always perfectly righteous.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
