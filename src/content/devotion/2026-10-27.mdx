reflection: |
  Picture a cloth so coarse it scratches the skin raw — woven tight from the dark hair of goats, heavy and rough, the kind of thing no one would ever choose to wear unless something inside them had broken. For generations that was exactly what it meant. When grief came, or guilt, people pulled off their ordinary clothes and wrapped themselves in sackcloth. The roughness was the point. The discomfort said what words could not.
  
  So when Scripture reaches for an image to describe the dying of the sun, it does not simply say the sky went dark. It says the sun became black as sackcloth. The great light of the heavens pulled on the clothing of mourning. It is as though creation itself, seeing what was coming, dressed for grief.
  
  There is something almost tender in that picture, hidden inside something terrifying. The darkening sky is not cold machinery grinding to a halt. It carries sorrow. And sorrow, rightly felt, has always been the doorway home. The same voice that one day lets the sun put on mourning-cloth is the voice that has been pleading with us all along — not "rend your garments" but "rend your hearts." Return to Me.
  
  We tend to save our mourning for what we have lost. But there is a grief that leads somewhere good — a holy ache over the distance we have let grow between ourselves and God, a sorrow that does not sink us but turns us around. Before the sky ever dresses for mourning, we are invited to let our own hearts do it first, and to discover at the end of that honest grief not darkness but a Father who is gracious and slow to anger.

lifeApplication: |
  There is probably something you have been dressing over rather than grieving — a habit, a resentment, a slow drifting you have quietly justified. We are good at smoothing the surface, at keeping up appearances while the heart goes untended. But the darkened, mourning sky asks us to be honest instead.
  
  This week, let yourself feel the weight of it, just for a while. Not the crushing kind of guilt that drives you from God, but the godly sorrow that draws you back to Him. Name the thing. Bring it into the light. You will find at the end of that honest grief not a shut door but a Father who is gracious and merciful, waiting to receive you.
prayer: |
  Father, I have become skilled at covering over what I would rather not face.
  
  Give me the courage to rend my heart and not merely my garments.
  
  Let my sorrow over sin be the kind that turns me toward You, never away from You.
  
  Thank You that You are gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and rich in love.
  
  Receive me again, Lord, as I return to You with all my heart.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
