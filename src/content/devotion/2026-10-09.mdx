reflection: |
  Listen to the new song rising in the throne room, and notice how wide it reaches. By Your blood You ransomed people for God — and then the sentence opens out like a door onto the whole world: from every tribe, and every language, and every people, and every nation. No corner left out. Not for one people and not another, not for the respectable only and not the ruined — every tongue, every colour, every border crossed. It is the most generous line imaginable, a price paid broad enough to cover all.
  
  And yet there is a small word sitting quietly inside that generosity, easy to sing straight past. The song does not say You ransomed every tribe and nation. It says You ransomed people from every tribe and nation. From. Out of. A gathering drawn out of all the world — which means, gently but unmistakably, that not every last person is gathered in. The door is flung open to everyone; not everyone walks through it. The price has been paid for all; not all will receive it.
  
  That one word turns a comfortable wonder into something that ought to keep us up at night. If "from" is true, then around us right now are people the blood was poured out for who have never once said yes to it — ransomed, the price already paid, and still standing outside the door. People in the next office. The next car in the traffic. The next house along the road. The feast is bought and waiting; the only question still hanging over them is whether anyone will trouble to tell them they are invited.
  
  It is easy to share good news where it costs nothing — at home, among people who already believe it. The harder and truer test is whether we will carry it into the places where it is awkward: the workplace, the queue, the slow crawl of traffic, the conversation we would rather keep shallow. That small word "from" does not only describe who will be saved; it quietly asks what we are prepared to do about it. The price has already been paid for everyone we will meet today. Not everyone knows. And some of them are waiting for us to open our mouths.

lifeApplication: "Think honestly about where you actually share your faith — and where you do not. For most of us it is easy enough among family, at church, with the people who already agree. The costly ground is everywhere else: the colleague who irritates you, the stranger in the queue, the driver who cut in front of you, the neighbour you have waved at for years and never once spoken to about Christ. You do not need a sermon or a perfect script. You need only a willingness to let the good news out of the house. Pick one person this week who lives outside your comfortable circle, and let your words — or simply your kindness — tell them the price has already been paid, and the invitation is theirs."
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that by Your blood You ransomed people from every tribe and language and people and nation — that the price was wide enough to cover all.
  
  Break my heart over that small word "from," and over the people around me who are ransomed yet still standing outside the door.
  
  Forgive me for sharing Your good news only where it is easy and costs me nothing.
  
  Give me courage to carry the gospel into the hard places — the workplace, the traffic, the ordinary day.
  
  Bring one person to my mind today, and make me willing to tell them the invitation is theirs.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
