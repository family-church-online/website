reflection: |
  You have perhaps watched a doctor's face as he read the results, and felt your whole body brace for the worst. The pause before he speaks seems to last an age. And then the words come — it's clear, it's nothing, you are going to be fine — and something floods through you so strong it nearly takes your legs out from under you. Relief. Pure, weak-kneed, overwhelming relief. Anyone who has known a near-miss on the road, an injury that did not happen, a danger they walked out of untouched, knows the same feeling: the rush of joy when you were certain you were in terrible trouble and suddenly discover that you are not.
  
  There was a trouble far deeper than any diagnosis that we were genuinely in, and we were far less aware of it. And into that trouble, Jesus walked on purpose. They nailed Him to a cross, and the crowd at His feet jeered, "If You are the Son of God, come down from there." He could have. One word, and it would have been over. But He did not come down — because if He saved Himself, He could not save us. He stayed, and let human hands do their worst, and gave up His life, so that the wrath we had earned would never fall on us.
  
  This is why the Christian life, at its very centre, is not a life bent under the fear of judgment. It is the life of someone who was in the gravest danger imaginable and has been rescued — and who cannot stop feeling the relief of it. That is the joy underneath everything: not that we are better than anyone, but that we have been spared something we could never have survived. And once that relief has truly broken over you, it does something tender and outward-looking. You begin to notice the people around you who do not yet know they can be safe, and your heart quietly asks: do they know? Do they know what they can be rescued from?

lifeApplication: |
  Let yourself actually feel the relief this week — not guilt, not dread, but the clean joy of the rescued. When the old fear of not being good enough creeps back in, answer it with the truth that there is now no condemnation for those who belong to Christ.
  
  And then let the relief turn outward. Think of one person in your life who does not yet know this safety, and carry them in prayer, and be ready — gently, without pressure — to tell them the reason for the hope you carry, if the moment opens. You do not have to change anyone. You only have to let them know there is a way to be safe.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You — how could I ever thank You enough — that I have been saved from the wrath I deserved.
  
  Thank You that Jesus did not come down from the cross, but stayed, and gave His life for me.
  
  Let me live in the relief and joy of the rescued, and not under the fear of judgment.
  
  When the old condemnation whispers to me, remind me that in Christ there is none left.
  
  Put on my heart the people who do not yet know this safety, and make me ready to share it.
  
  May my gratitude drive me to worship You today, and every day, and for all eternity.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
