  text: "For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin."
reflection: |
  Think about a substitutes' bench near the end of a hard-fought match. There are men sitting there who could change everything — fresh legs, ferocious power, the kind of impact that turns a game. But there is one unbreakable rule about a substitute: he can only be brought on if he is already part of the team. You cannot reach into the crowd, grab the strongest-looking spectator, and send him into play. It does not matter how gifted he is. He was never one of the squad, so he cannot come in.
  
  That simple rule sits underneath one of the deepest things about our rescue. For Someone to stand in our place, He had to be one of us — genuinely part of the human team, not a powerful outsider looking on. And so the One who was worthy did not rescue us from a safe distance. He came onto the field, tested in every way we are tested, living a real human life with real hunger and real weariness and real temptation.
  
  Here is what that means when it finally lands: He does not sympathise with your struggles from the stands. He knows them from the inside, because He played the same game on the same field. Your substitute is not a stranger drafted in at the last minute. He is one of us — and being one of us is precisely what qualified Him to take your place.

lifeApplication: "When you feel most alone in a struggle — sure that no one understands the particular pressure you are under — remember that your Rescuer is not watching from outside the game. He has been tempted where you are tempted. Take the very thing you assume He could not possibly understand, and tell Him about it today as one who has been there. You are not explaining your life to a spectator."
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, thank You that You did not rescue me from a safe distance.
  
  You came onto the field, one of us, tested in every way yet without sin.
  
  When I feel that no one understands, remind me that You do — from the inside.
  
  Thank You that being one of us is what qualified You to stand in my place.
  
  In Your name, Amen.
