reflection: |
  It is one of the most painful households in all of Scripture. Two sisters married to the same man, locked in a bitter rivalry over who is loved and who can bear children. When one cannot have a child, she hands her servant girl to her husband as a kind of weapon. The other sister answers in kind. Children are born into a tug-of-war, named out of spite and longing and wounded pride, and two servant women are dragged into a fight that was never theirs.
  
  Anyone who has watched a family tear itself apart knows the ache of that house — the jealousy, the rejection, the slow poisoning of what should have been love. You would think a beginning that broken could only ever produce broken things.
  
  And the damage was real. It ran long — generations felt the fractures. Whole branches of that family later wandered off into idolatry, and one of them was quietly left off an honored list because of it. Sin's consequences reach further than we like to admit, often further than a single lifetime.
  
  But here is the wonder that should steal your breath: thousands of years later, God is still not wasting those children. The very sons of that broken, feuding house — the unwanted ones, the fought-over ones — He sends out as His messengers to carry hope to the world. And the branch that was left off the list is not erased forever; when the land is finally portioned out, that name is spoken first of all. Sin's reach is long. Grace's reach is longer. However far the damage has traveled in your family or your past, grace has already run on ahead of it.

lifeApplication: |
  Perhaps you carry something from a house like that — a family fracture you did not choose, a pattern that seems to repeat down the generations, a private failure you are sure has disqualified you for good. It is tempting to believe the damage has the final word, that some things have simply traveled too far to be redeemed.
  
  They haven't. The God who sent out the children of that feuding house, and who spoke the forgotten name first, is the same God who meets you now. Do not count yourself out. Do not count your family out. Bring the brokenness to Him honestly, and then watch for the ways grace has already run ahead of the damage — because it always has.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that Your grace reaches further than my sin, and further than the brokenness I inherited.
  
  Thank You that You waste nothing — not even the worst chapters of my family's story.
  
  Forgive me for believing that some damage is too far gone for You to redeem.
  
  Where I have counted myself out, remind me that You speak the forgotten name first.
  
  Help me to bring my brokenness to You honestly and trust that grace has run on ahead.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
