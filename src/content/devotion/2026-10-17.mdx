And they were given authority over a fourth of the earth, to kill with sword and with famine and with pestilence and by wild beasts of the earth."
reflection: |
  Of all four riders, this is the one no one wants to picture. A pale horse the colour of a corpse. Its rider carries a name instead of a weapon — his name is Death — and close behind him comes the grave itself, swallowing the fallen. A fourth of the earth. It is too large to feel; the mind flinches away from it.
  
  And yet notice something easy to miss in the horror. This rider does not come on his own authority. He comes only after a seal is broken by another hand. Even Death rides out on a leash. He goes exactly as far as he is permitted and not one step further.
  
  There is something stranger still. Out of this very darkness, Scripture tells us, come multitudes — more than anyone could number — who find God in the wreckage. It is like a body that has to get frighteningly worse before the healing can begin; what looks like pure destruction becomes, for countless people, the thing that finally turns them home. The moment everything they trusted is stripped away is the moment they stop trying to be their own god and say, at last, "I give up. I'm done. I need You."
  
  This is what our God is able to do. He can take even death — the thing we fear most, the thing that looks like the end of all mercy — and bend it toward mercy, so that the worst moment of a life becomes its last and best chance to come home. And He can do it because the One holding the seal is the One who already walked into death Himself and came out the other side, so that for those who are His, death is not the end of the story but the door home.

lifeApplication: |
  Maybe you are in some wreckage of your own right now — a loss, a failure, a diagnosis, a season where the things you leaned on have been stripped away. It is the last place you would think to look for mercy. But it may be exactly the doorway. Rock bottom has turned more hearts toward God than comfort ever has, because it is there, with nothing left to prove and nothing left to protect, that we finally stop performing and simply say, "Lord, I can't. I need You."
  
  And if it is the fear of death itself that haunts you — your own, or that of someone you love — hold on to this: for those who belong to Jesus, death comes on a leash, and on the other side of that door is home. Let that free you to live unafraid. And let it move you toward the people around you who are in their own wreckage — not with easy words, but with the quiet confidence of someone who knows that even there, mercy can be found.
prayer: |
  Thank You that even death comes only as far as You permit, and never one step further.
  
  Thank You that You can bend the darkest moment of a life toward mercy, and make it a doorway home.
  
  Where I am in my own wreckage, meet me there, and let it be the place I finally stop fighting and turn to You.
  
  Free me from the fear of death, and send me to those who are afraid.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
