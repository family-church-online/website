reflection: |
  Picture His face in the moment just before He rose. Thirty-odd years He had been away from home — the ache of it, the hunger, the sleeping under other people's roofs, the friends who ran, the soldiers, the nails. And now, standing on the hillside with His hands lifted over the people He loved, He begins to rise, and He knows. He is going home. Imagine the look on His face as He turns it upward and sees the glory that has been waiting for Him.
  
  We rush past this moment so quickly on our way to other things. We treasure the manger, we cling to the cross and the empty tomb, and then we hurry on. But stay here a moment. This is a homecoming. This is the Son, who gave up everything and endured everything, finally seeing the Father's face again.
  
  And here is what steadies the heart: He did not go home as one who barely survived. He went home having finished the work, having won. The hands lifted in blessing were scarred hands. The One who climbed back into glory is the very same One who first went all the way down.
  
  Which means the road home is open. He did not vanish into heaven and shut the door behind Him — He went ahead, the way someone goes ahead to prepare a place for the people coming after. His face turned homeward is the promise that one day ours will be too.

lifeApplication: |
  There are seasons when faith feels like a long way from home — when obedience costs more than you expected, when you are tired of holding on, when the glory feels far off and the suffering feels near. On those days, remember the face turned homeward. The One you follow walked the whole long road first, and He walked it all the way home.
  
  Let that change how you carry today. The weariness is real, but it is not the end of the story. Do the next small faithful thing — the kind word, the honest prayer, the quiet obedience no one sees — as someone who is on their way home, following the One who has already arrived and is holding the door open for you.
prayer: |
  Thank You that You endured the whole long road home, and that Your scarred hands are lifted in blessing over me.
  
  When I am weary and the way feels long, lift my eyes to the home You have gone ahead to prepare.
  
  Forgive me for rushing past the wonder of Your homecoming; teach me to treasure it.
  
  Help me to live today as one who is on the way to You.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
