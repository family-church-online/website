reflection: |
  There is a quiet satisfaction in finally handing someone over to God. You have been wronged, and you have carried it long enough, and at last you pray it: Lord, You deal with them. It feels like justice. And if you are honest, it feels a little like pleasure too — the private thought of that person one day standing before God, having to answer at last for what they did to you.
  
  And then, if the Spirit is gentle with you, another thought arrives on the heels of the first, and it is not a comfortable one. Somewhere out there, there may be a person praying that very prayer — about you. Someone you wounded and long ago forgot, quietly asking God to pour out justice on your name. The satisfaction drains away, and in its place comes something more honest: I need mercy every bit as much as they do.
  
  This is why we are told to love our enemies and to pray for the very people who wound us. Not to pray for their sentence, but for their mercy — because we are far more desperate for our own than we like to admit. And something quiet happens in us when we begin to ask God for a person's mercy instead of their downfall. The hardness we have carefully nursed starts, almost against our will, to thaw. It is very difficult to keep hating someone you are daily begging heaven to forgive. Praying for their mercy turns out to be the very thing that softens ours.

lifeApplication: "Bring to mind the person you would most like to see God deal with. Now, instead of asking for justice on them, ask for mercy — by name. It will feel wrong in your mouth the first few times; say it anyway. Pray it again tomorrow, and the day after. Watch what it does, not to them, but to you — how the grip of the offence loosens finger by finger, how it slowly becomes possible to think of them without the old heat rising. Mercy, it turns out, changes the one who prays it first."
prayer: |
  Lord, I confess how easily I long for justice on others while quietly depending on mercy for myself.
  
  Thank You that You are able to pour out justice on all that deserves it, yet choose again and again to show mercy.
  
  Help me to pray for the mercy of the very ones who have hurt me, even when the words come hard.
  
  Soften my heart as I pray, and change my attitude toward those I have found impossible to love.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
