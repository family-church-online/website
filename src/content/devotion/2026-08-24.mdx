reflection: |
  There is a moment a diver learns to find, somewhere beneath the surface, where the body stops arguing with the water. Weighted just right, lungs half full, you neither float up nor sink down. You simply hang there, suspended in the blue, held. The smallest breath lifts you a little; the smallest sigh lowers you again. You are doing almost nothing, and yet you have never been more free to move.

  Most of us live our days fighting the water. Some mornings we are sinking — dragged down by every failure, every sharp word, every thing we cannot fix — until we are sure we will drown in our own weakness. Other mornings we are thrashing the other way, kicking hard to stay on top of it all in our own strength, and by evening we are simply exhausted, arms burning, having stayed afloat by sheer effort and hated every minute of it.

  But there is a third place, and it is the only place that holds. It is the place where I tell the whole truth about myself — how weak I really am — and in the same breath rest my whole weight on how strong He really is. Not sinking into despair, not straining on my own power, but suspended between the two, held. It is only there, weightless in His grace, no longer fighting the water, that I finally have the freedom to move.

lifeApplication: |
  Somewhere today the water will start to pull at you — a demand you cannot meet, a fear you cannot shake, a person you cannot change. Notice which way you begin to move: down into despair, or up into frantic effort. Both will wear you out.

  Instead, stop kicking. Say it plainly: "I cannot carry this on my own." And in the same moment, let your full weight down onto Him. Fear how weak you are; trust how good He is. That is the weightless place, and from there — and only from there — you can face the day and even choose joy.
prayer: |
  Father, I confess the days I try to stay afloat by my own strength until I have nothing left.

  I confess the days I sink under my own failures as though You were not holding me at all.

  Teach me the weightless place — honest about my weakness, resting on Your power.

  Let Your grace be enough for me today, especially where I feel my weakness the most.

  Hold me there, and set me free to move.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
