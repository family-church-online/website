keyScripture:
  ref: "Revelation 5:6"
  text: "And between the throne and the four living creatures and among the elders I saw a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain, with seven horns and with seven eyes, which are the seven spirits of God sent out into all the earth."
reflection: |
  An elder has just said the word Lion. Lion of the tribe of Judah, Root of David, the One who has conquered — and every expectation lifts toward the shape of a king. You would turn, as John turned, to see the great beast stride into the throne room: mane and muscle, the conqueror arriving at last, the long wait finally over. And then you look. Standing there, between the throne and the elders, is a lamb. Not a lion. A lamb — and not even an unblemished one, but one bearing the marks of having been slain.
  
  It is the wrong animal. The word was Lion; the sight is Lamb. And the mistake is an old one. For centuries, careful readers had done the same arithmetic and reached the same wrong answer. The teachers of the law knew their Scriptures cold, and from them they had drawn a portrait of the Messiah: a conqueror on a warhorse, come to break the grip of Rome and restore the throne. So when He arrived as a carpenter, gentle, riding a borrowed donkey, they could not make Him fit. They had built a box, with real devotion, and the living God would not climb into it.
  
  We do the same more often than we would admit. We come to God with the shape of the answer already drawn — the problem solved this way, the healing on this timeline, the door opened here and not there. We ask Him to be the lion we pictured, striding in to fix what we have decided needs fixing, and we half-miss the Lamb standing right in front of us. Prayer is not left unanswered when the lion does not come; it is answered in the Lamb, in a way and a timing we did not draft.
  
  The danger was never that God is too small for us. It is that our picture of Him is too small for Him — a box built out of half-understood hopes. The Lamb in the throne room is the gentlest rebuke and the greatest relief at once: He will not be the king we invented, because He is so much more than the king we invented. And the scene leaves one quiet question hanging over us — are we seeking His will, or asking Him to bless our own?

lifeApplication: "Notice the shape of your own recent prayers. Is there a request you have been pressing on God with the answer already sketched in — the relationship restored by a certain date, the job that must be this job, the outcome that has to look like the one in your mind? Try holding the sketch loosely today. Bring Him the need, and then leave off dictating the rescue. Watch for the Lamb where you have been scanning the horizon for the lion — the quiet provision, the unexpected person, the slower healing that turns out to be the deeper one. He may not answer in the animal you were expecting. He will answer as the One who knows what the conquering truly requires."
prayer: |
  Lord, forgive me for the boxes I build for You out of my own hopes and half-understandings.
  
  I confess the prayers where I have asked You to bless my plan rather than seeking Your will.
  
  Open my eyes to see the Lamb standing where I keep looking for the lion.
  
  Teach me to trust Your ways when they run higher than mine, and Your timing when it is not my own.
  
  Make me willing to receive the answer You give rather than the one I drafted.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
