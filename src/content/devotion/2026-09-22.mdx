reflection: |
  On the high ground the storms do not arrive politely. Anyone who has lived where the open veld meets that wide, bruised sky knows the particular terror of it — the whole horizon lit white, and the crack arriving with almost no gap between the flash and the sound, because the strike is that close. Walk out the next morning and you can find where the lightning came down.
  
  A tree that had stood for decades, split open and blackened, all but obliterated in a single instant. And scattered near it, bricks that were nowhere near the tree, smashed and flung aside by the sheer force that tore through the ground. You stand over the wreckage of it and something in you goes quiet. You are not so much afraid as undone — made suddenly small, aware that you understand almost nothing of the power that passed through here in the dark.
  
  Scripture says this shattered aftermath is not even the fullness of God's power — only its faint outskirts, the small whisper we manage to catch of Him. We spend so much of our lives quietly cutting God down to a size we can carry in one hand: a helpful idea, a comfort for hard days, a name to reach for in emergencies. Then the sky splits open, and the ruined tree tells the truth. What we have seen of Him is only the edge of the edge — and the thunder of His full power, no one can understand.

lifeApplication: "It is worth letting awe back in — not the manufactured kind, but the honest smallness you feel standing under a sky far bigger than you. When you next feel dwarfed by something that will not bend to your control — the night sky, the ocean, a circumstance too large to manage — do not rush past the feeling. Let it remind you that the God you pray to is not tame, not manageable, not yours to shrink. And take heart: this immense God, whose power splits trees and whom no mind can fully hold, is the very One who knows your name and bends near to listen."
prayer: |
  Lord, You are greater than anything I can picture, and I have too often made You small.
  
  Forgive me for treating You as a comfortable idea rather than the God whose power I cannot begin to grasp.
  
  Restore to me a right and holy awe, that I might worship You as You truly are.
  
  Thank You that this same immeasurable power is gentle enough to hold my small life with care.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
