reflection: |
  Every wedding has a moment when the whole room turns. The talking dies away, someone catches the first sight of the bride, and a kind of gladness rises — an involuntary sound of joy, people getting to their feet, faces beginning to shine. All the preparing, all the waiting, all the long months of engagement narrow to this single moment: the feast has come, and the bride is ready.
  
  Now hear where the whole long, hard story of the world is finally heading. For those who belong to Jesus, it does not end in a courtroom. It ends at a wedding. "The marriage of the Lamb has come, and His Bride has made herself ready." And gathered there is a great multitude in white robes — people who came through the very worst the world could do to them, who washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.
  
  But how can the ending be a feast and not a sentence? Because the sentence has already been carried out. The judgment our sin deserved did not simply vanish; it fell — on Jesus, on His shoulders, at the cross. He drank that cup to the very bottom so that ours could be filled instead with joy. This is why those who are His need never dread the wrath of God: not because judgment is unreal, but because for us it is already finished. What is left for the people of Jesus is not the courtroom. It is the wedding hall, the lights, the music, and the shout of gladness when the bride comes in.

lifeApplication: "Whatever fear you carry into this new week — about your health, your future, the things you cannot control — let this settle it. The final word spoken over your life is not wrath but welcome. You are not being dragged toward a trial; you are being drawn toward a feast. Live today as one who has already been invited to the wedding, and let the certainty of that celebration lift the weight of everything still unresolved."
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that the story ends not in a courtroom but at the wedding feast of Your Son.
  
  Thank You that Jesus drank the cup of wrath I deserved, so that my cup could be filled with joy.
  
  Forgive me for living under a dread from which the cross has already set me free.
  
  Fill me this week with the gladness of one who is invited, kept, and welcomed home.
  
  Thank You, Lord, that You are on the throne, and there is no one like You.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
