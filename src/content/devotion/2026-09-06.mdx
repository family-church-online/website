reflection: |
  There are mornings when the first thing that rises in you is not praise but a plea to be let off. You open your eyes to the same tired world — the headlines, the heaviness, the ache of it all — and something in you whispers, Lord, I am done; take me home; if You are coming, come now. Many faithful people have prayed exactly that, quietly, into the ceiling, far more often than they would ever admit aloud.

  But imagine waking with the opposite prayer on your lips. Not take me now, but please, one more day. Not because the world has suddenly grown easier overnight, but because of a single thought strong enough to turn you right around: there are people I have not told yet. There is someone who may never hear it if I am gone tomorrow. So — one more day.

  That small turning, from wanting out to asking for one more day, sits very near the heart of what it means to choose joy. Joy is not the absence of the heaviness. It is a mind so fixed on Christ that even in a frightening world it can still rejoice, because it knows how the story ends and Who is holding it.

  And here is the freedom folded inside it: you do not have to have all the answers first. You will never be finished, never flawless — and that is not the bad news, it is nearly the whole point. He means to use the weak, unpolished, still-being-unwrapped you, because the treasure was always meant to shine through cracked and ordinary jars, so everyone can see the power belongs to Him and not to us. So ask for one more day. There is real joy to be had in it, and there are people still waiting to hear.

lifeApplication: |
  Ask God, this morning, for one more day — and then actually spend it as though you meant the prayer. Somewhere in the ordinary hours ahead, tell one person, in your own imperfect words, something of what God has done for you. You do not need the perfect explanation or every answer ready; you only need to be willing to be the cracked jar the light gets through.

  Choosing joy is often as small, and as world-changing, as this: waking up, asking for the day, and giving away what you have already been given.
prayer: |
  Father, on the mornings I want to give up, give me the prayer for one more day.

  Thank You that Your power is made perfect in my weakness, and that You are not waiting for me to be perfect first.

  Use even the cracked and unfinished parts of me to let Your light reach someone else.

  Fill me with a joy that does not depend on how easy the day is, and lead me to the person still waiting to hear.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
