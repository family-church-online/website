reflection: |
  You turn the packet over in your hand before you buy it — sugar, salt, the long list of things you cannot pronounce — because you have decided, this year, to be careful about what goes into your body. There is a row of supplements by the kettle. You count your steps. You have opinions about which oils are good for you and which are not.

  And then, somewhere between the kettle and the couch, you pick up your phone and pour in whatever happens to be there. The outrage of strangers. Three hundred comments beneath a headline, each one angrier than the last. The bright highlight reels of people you will never meet, quietly teaching you that your own ordinary life is not enough.

  We guard the door to the body so carefully and leave the door to the mind swinging open on its hinges. Yet the mind is where everything begins — every fear we rehearse until we believe it, every resentment we feed until it grows fat, every lie about ourselves that started as a single thought we agreed to keep thinking.

  He does not ask you to empty your mind. He asks you to fill it, on purpose, with a particular kind of food: whatever is true, whatever is lovely, whatever is worthy of His praise. Your mind will feast on something today — it always does. The only question left to you is what you will set before it.

lifeApplication: |
  Choose one input today and change it. Perhaps it is the first thing your thumb reaches for before your feet are even out of bed, or the scroll through bad news you let be the last thing you see at night. Trade it, just once, for something true and lovely — a single psalm read slowly, a verse carried in your pocket through the day, a few quiet minutes naming what is good before the world tells you what is wrong.

  You would not knowingly eat what is spoiled. Give your mind the same care you have been giving your body, and watch how differently the whole day comes to rest on you.
prayer: |
  Father, You know how easily my mind drifts to the very things that steal my peace.

  Forgive me for guarding so many lesser doors while leaving my thoughts wide open.

  Teach me to feed on what is true and lovely and worthy of Your praise, until my thinking begins to look like Yours.

  When the noise of the world presses in, draw my attention gently back to You.

  In Jesus' name, Amen.
