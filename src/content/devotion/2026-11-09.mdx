reflection: |
  Think of the largest crowd you have ever stood inside. A stadium filled to its last row, a city square packed shoulder to shoulder as the year turns, a station platform at the height of the morning rush. However enormous it felt in the moment, someone could have counted it. Given enough patience, every head could be numbered, every seat tallied, every ticket added up at the end of the day. There is a quiet comfort in a number. It draws a line around a thing and tells us its size.
  
  But here is a gathering that defeats the counting altogether. A multitude so vast that heaven itself does not pause to tally it — no one could number them. And they are not one kind of people, neatly drawn from a single place. They have come from every nation, every tribe, every people, every language on the earth. Faces that never met in life and tongues that never shared a word are pressed together before one throne, and every one of them is dressed in white, and every one of them is singing the same song.
  
  We have a quiet habit of shrinking God's family down to a size we can hold — the people who look like us, pray like us, come from where we come from. We picture heaven as a slightly larger version of our own small circle. But this crowd will not fit inside our imagination, and that is exactly the point. The reach of His grace runs further than our arithmetic can follow. And here is the wonder folded inside it: in a crowd that no one can number, you are not lost. You are known, you are named, you are clothed in white — one face held safe and visible inside a multitude beyond counting.

lifeApplication: "The next time your picture of God's people quietly shrinks to the size of your own kind, let this crowd stretch it back out. Somewhere today a believer is waking in a language you will never learn, in a country you will never visit, singing to the same Lamb. Let that widen your prayers beyond your own street and the handful of faces you already know. And when you feel small and overlooked, remember that being one among a countless many does not make you anonymous to Him. The God who can hold an uncountable multitude has no trouble holding you by name."
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that Your family is larger and more beautiful than anything I can picture or count.
  
  Forgive me for shrinking Your grace down to the size of people like me.
  
  Thank You that in a crowd beyond numbering, You still know my name and have clothed me in white.
  
  Teach me to pray for brothers and sisters in every nation, tribe, and tongue.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
