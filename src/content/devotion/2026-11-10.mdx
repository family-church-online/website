reflection: |
  In some houses, when a bar of chocolate has to be shared, the kitchen scale comes out. The bar is cut down the middle, one half is set on the scale, then the other, and the reading had better match to the gram, or there will be trouble. Children will count out jelly beans one by one to be certain no one received a single extra. Even the pets are at it — two cats fed side by side, and the moment one wanders off for a rest, the other is over at its bowl, just to check the food is really the same. The fear of missing out, of a brother or sister getting something we did not, runs so deep it shows up before we can even name it.
  
  We carry that same scale into our faith without realising it. We glance sideways at another believer's story — the dramatic rescue, the gift we don't have, the door that opened for them and not for us — and something in us reaches for the scale to check we have not been short-changed. Peter did it on a beach with the risen Jesus. He looked at his friend John and asked, in effect, "Lord, what about him? What does he get?" And Jesus, gently, would not even let the question stand: what is that to you? You follow Me.
  
  No two of us are called in the same way. One meets God in a blaze like a burning bush; another in a quiet, almost unnoticed rescue years in the making. One is handed a task that puts them at the front; another serves entirely unseen. These are not different measures of His love, weighed out to see who got more. They are different assignments from a Father who loves each child completely. The question that matters in the end is not where will I be, or did I get the same as my brother — it is whose am I, and who will I be with? Settle that, and the scale can finally be put away.

lifeApplication: "Notice this week the moment the inner scale comes out — the small sting when someone else is blessed, gifted, or noticed in a way you are not. It is such an old reflex that we hardly feel ourselves reaching for it. When you catch it, let the Lord's words to Peter answer it: what is that to you? You follow Me. Then try something harder than measuring: thank God out loud for the very thing the other person received. The portion on someone else's plate was never taken from yours. Your Father has not run out of love, and He has not forgotten your name."
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that You love me completely, not in measured halves weighed against anyone else.
  
  Forgive me for the times I have reached for the scale, counting what others received and resenting it.
  
  Help me to celebrate the gifts and the stories of those around me instead of comparing my own.
  
  When I ask "what about them?", remind me gently to simply follow You.
  
  Settle my heart on whose I am and who I will be with.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
