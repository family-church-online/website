reflection: |
  The whole throne room goes down. The elders fall on their faces, harps in hand and golden bowls of incense lifted — and the song swells until it is no longer only them but ten thousand times ten thousand angels, and then every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea, all of it at once, one great roar: Worthy is the Lamb. It is the most total act of worship imaginable, every living thing bent in the same direction at the same moment, and at the centre of it the One who was slain, receiving at last what He is owed.
  
  It would be easy to decide that this is what worship is — the overwhelming moment, the music swelling, the room full and the voices lifted high. And it is that. But notice: the elders do not only sing; they fall down. In that room, worship is posture before it is ever melody. It is the whole self going low before the One who is worthy. Which means worship was never meant to stay inside the song. It was always meant to follow us out of the room.
  
  So it turns out you do not need a band to worship. You need only to notice. A sunrise bleeding colour over the hills. The particular face of your own child, or the deepening lines on the face of an ageing parent. The animal, the sea, the plain astonishing fact that you woke this morning breathing at all — every bit of it came from the hand of the One on the throne, and every bit of it is an invitation to stop and give Him the glory. And worship is not only what we notice; it is also how we treat the people around us, because we are — whether we like it or not — the clearest picture of God that some of them will ever see.
  
  The elders show us the full shape of it: they fall down, and then, through the rest of the story, they rise and live. Real worship is not an hour on a Sunday with the music turned up; it is a whole life bent in one direction — noticing the Maker in a sunrise and honouring Him in how we speak to a stranger. The Lamb is worthy of far more than a song. He is worthy of the way we drive, the way we work, the way we treat the person in front of us. "Worthy" isthe word; the only question is whether the rest of our week agrees with our Sunday.

lifeApplication: "Try, this week, to let worship escape the Sunday service. Tomorrow morning, before the day swallows you whole, stop at the window for ten seconds when the light comes up, and simply say thank You — that is worship. Look at the face of someone you love as though you were seeing the Maker's handiwork, because you are. And then watch how you treat the people who can do nothing for you in return: the slow cashier, the difficult colleague, the stranger in traffic. You are the clearest glimpse of God that some of them will get. Worship is not only what you sing when the music is good; it is what you do when no one at all is leading you in a song."
prayer: |
  Worthy — worthy are You, Lord, the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wisdom and honour and glory and blessing.
  
  Thank You that I need no stage and no song to worship You — only eyes open enough to see Your hand in all You have made.
  
  Forgive me for the worship that stays inside one hour on a Sunday and never follows me into my week.
  
  Teach me to notice You in the sunrise, in the faces around me, in the very fact that I am alive and breathing.
  
  Make my whole life — the way I work and drive and speak to strangers — into worship that is worthy of You.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
