reflection: |
  You can feel it before you understand it — the low rattle of cups in the cupboard, a picture frame tapping against the wall, the floor shivering beneath your feet for a second or two. Even the smallest tremor does something strange to us. It reaches past our thoughts and touches that deep, childlike place that always assumed the ground would stay exactly where it was put.
  
  Now stretch that trembling out beyond anything we have words for. Not cups rattling, but buildings folding in on themselves. Roads splitting open. The pipes that carry our water and the lines that carry our light snapping in a single moment. Everything we lean on without ever thinking about it, gone in the space of a breath.
  
  We build our lives on things we quietly expect to stay still — a wage that arrives, a roof that holds, a body that works today the way it worked yesterday, a world that behaves tomorrow the way it behaved this morning. We hardly notice how much trust we have handed to the ground beneath us until the ground decides to move. And Scripture is honest with us: one day all of it moves. Every mountain, every island, nothing left where we last saw it.
  
  But there is one detail we dare not miss. The earthquake does not break loose on its own. It is released. Behind all the shaking is a hand that opened the seal, a throne that never so much as trembles, a King who is not caught off guard by a single second of it. Perhaps the ground has to give way beneath us precisely so that we will finally lift our eyes and find the one thing that never does.

lifeApplication: |
  Somewhere in your life there is a floor you are standing on without thinking — a plan, a person, an income, a quiet certainty that things will simply carry on. None of these are wrong to have. But it is worth asking, gently and honestly, what would be left standing if that floor moved tomorrow.
  
  Today, try naming the thing you are most afraid to lose, and then deliberately place it into the hands of the One who holds the earth itself. Loosen your grip on what can be shaken, and tighten it on what cannot. When the news rattles you and the world feels unsteady, let the trembling become a kind of summons — a reminder to look up at the throne that does not move.
prayer: |
  Father, so much of what I lean on can be shaken, and You know how tightly I hold it.
  
  Teach me to feel every tremor as an invitation to look up to You.
  
  Loosen my grip on the things that cannot last, and anchor me in the kingdom that cannot be moved.
  
  When the ground gives way, let me not be ruled by fear, for You are my refuge and my strength.
  
  Thank You that You are never caught off guard, and that nothing shakes the throne where You sit.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
