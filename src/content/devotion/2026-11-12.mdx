reflection: |
  There is a story from a revival in Wales where the local police, of all people, took up brass instruments and formed bands. Not because crime had been cleverly solved, but because it had simply dried up. The men who once kept them busy with drunkenness and brawling and petty wickedness had been swept up, in their hundreds, into a flood of the Spirit, and they were filling the churches instead of the cells. The officers found themselves with so little to do that they went looking for something to fill the hours — and so they played.
  
  That is how we love to picture revival: warm, joyful, a whole town made glad almost overnight, everyone drawn together and singing. And sometimes it comes just like that. But the single greatest ingathering ever described does not happen in a pleasant valley on an ordinary afternoon. It happens in the worst conditions the world has ever known. The vast multitude in white are named for us plainly — these are the ones coming out of the great tribulation. Their faith was not grown in comfort. It was forged in the fire, under the heaviest suffering imaginable, and still they came, in numbers no one can count.
  
  We tend to assume that God's best work needs good soil and fair weather — that the harvest comes only when life is kind. But the hardest ground in history grows the largest harvest of all. This is worth holding onto when your own circumstances are bleak and you cannot see how anything good could possibly grow there. The place where you feel most besieged may be the very field in which God intends to do His deepest work. He is not waiting for the conditions to improve before He moves. He moves in the tribulation, and brings a countless crowd out the other side, dressed in white.

lifeApplication: "Is there a corner of your life right now that feels like barren, trampled ground — a place of loss, or strain, or long waiting, where you have quietly decided nothing good can grow? Do not write it off. The crowd in white came out of the worst, not the best. Ask God not to snatch you out of the hard field too quickly, but to do in it the work only He can do. And watch for the brass bands — the unlikely, almost comic signs that He has been at work in the very place you least expected Him."
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that You are not waiting for easy days to do Your greatest work.
  
  Thank You for the countless multitude whose faith was forged in the fire and came through in white.
  
  Meet me in the hard ground of my own life, the place where I had given up hope of growth.
  
  Give me steadfastness in trial and eyes to see where You are already moving.
  
  Help me to count it joy, trusting that the glory to come outweighs the suffering of now.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
