reflection: |
  There is a particular kind of dread that belongs to an examination hall. You sit down, turn the paper over, and your eyes move down a page where nothing is familiar. Not one question. You turn to the next page, and the next, and the panic rises in your chest like water. Then, somewhere past the halfway mark, a few questions appear that you can actually answer, and you hold onto them the way a drowning person holds onto anything that floats — and somehow, barely, by grace more than by merit, you come through.
  
  We know that feeling far outside any classroom. It is the shape of crying out. It arrives when someone we love is in hospital, when a person we expected home has not arrived and the phone simply rings and rings, when we lie in the dark turning over a problem we cannot fix. From somewhere beneath our words something breaks loose: Lord, I need You. Please. And if we are honest, it very often feels as though that cry rises no higher than the ceiling and settles back down on us, unanswered, unheard.
  
  But there is a curtain in Scripture that gets pulled back, and behind it we are shown what is really happening to those desperate prayers. They are not disappearing into an empty sky. They are being gathered up, mingled with incense, and carried before the throne of God like a fragrance He loves. Every whispered please, every tear-soaked plea you were certain had gone nowhere — He has received it, treasured it, and He knows. Your cry has a destination, and it is not the ceiling. It is the heart of God.

lifeApplication: |
  The next time you pray and feel nothing — no warmth, no answer, no sense that anyone is listening — try to remember the incense. Picture your words not falling back on you but rising, received, cherished. You might even name your cry out loud, plainly, the way you would to a trusted friend: the person you are afraid for, the situation you cannot mend, the fear you have carried in silence.
  
  Keep crying out, the way you once clung to those few familiar questions on the page. The silence you feel is not the measure of whether God hears; His Word is. And His Word says your prayer rises before Him, and that He knows.
prayer: |
  Father, thank You that not one of my prayers has ever been lost to You.
  
  When my words feel weak and my faith feels thin, remind me that You gather them like incense and treasure them.
  
  I bring You now the cry I have been carrying, and I trust it into Your hands.
  
  Teach me to keep calling on You even when I feel nothing, believing that You hear.
  
  Thank You that You not only hear my cry, but You know me, and You love me.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
