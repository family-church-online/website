keyScripture:
  ref: "Revelation 6:2"
  text: "And I looked, and behold, a white horse! And its rider had a bow, and a crown was given to him, and he came out conquering, and to conquer."
reflection: |
  At first glance, the rider looks like the King come home. A white horse. A crown on his head. A figure riding out in triumph to conquer. If you were not looking carefully, you would cheer.
  
  But look again. The bow in his hand has no arrow. The crown was not won — it was handed to him by someone else. And he has no name. This is no king at all; it is a figure built to be mistaken for the King, and that is its whole purpose. It conquers not by force but by resemblance, winning hearts simply by looking almost right.
  
  This is the oldest and most effective of all deceptions. The lies that lead people furthest from God are rarely the obvious ones, the ones that announce themselves as evil. They are the ones that wear the right clothes and speak the right words and ride in on a white horse. "Many will come in My name," Jesus warned — not in some other name, but in His. There is a version of faith that keeps all the vocabulary and quietly loses the Lord.
  
  So how do you keep from being fooled by what looks almost right? Not mainly by studying the counterfeits — there are endless varieties, and you will never catalogue them all. You keep from being fooled by knowing the real One so well, so personally, that the imitation simply cannot hold your gaze. The one who truly knows Jesus feels the wrongness of the fake long before they can explain it.

lifeApplication: |
  Those trained to spot counterfeit money are not shown every possible fake. There are too many, and new ones appear all the time. Instead they handle the genuine note again and again until they know its weight and feel so well that anything false announces itself the moment it touches their hands.
  
  Faith works the same way. You will not protect yourself from every clever distortion by chasing down each one. You protect yourself by handling the real thing — time in His word, honest prayer, worship that is about Him and not about the spectacle — until you know Jesus so well that what is false simply does not feel like Him. Do not be dazzled by what is impressive. Ask of everything that claims His name: does this look like the Jesus I actually know?
prayer: |
  Thank You that You are the true King, and that You have given me Your word so I can know You as You really are.
  
  Guard me from the lies that look almost right; give me the discernment that comes from knowing You.
  
  Forgive me for the times I was dazzled by what was impressive rather than anchored in what was true.
  
  Draw me so close to You that nothing false can hold my gaze.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
