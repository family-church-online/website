  text: "He had no form or majesty that we should look at him, and no beauty that we should desire him. He was despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief."
reflection: |
  There were two males guarding that pride. One was a huge, handsome specimen — the kind you would point to and say, there is the king. The other was smaller, scarred, disfigured, mean-looking, the runt with a torn face. And anyone who knows that country will tell you: it is not the big, beautiful one you worry about. It is the little scarred one. When the battle finally broke and the magnificent lion could not manage it, the camera panned to the edge of the clearing — and in came the mongrel. Scarred, angry, powerful. He saw what was happening and he charged straight into the fight: not on my watch.
  
  We keep expecting rescue to arrive looking impressive. We look for the beautiful one, the obvious champion. But the One heaven has been waiting for did not come with form or majesty that we should be drawn to Him on sight. He came marred, despised, a man of sorrows, carrying scars. To the watching world He looked like the least likely to win.
  
  And that is exactly the point. The wounds we would have overlooked are the very thing that qualifies Him. The scars are not a disqualification; they are His credentials. When everything strong and splendid has failed to hold the line, it is the wounded One who charges into the clearing and turns the whole battle.

lifeApplication: "Notice today where you have been overlooking help because it did not arrive in the shape you expected — the quiet answer, the humble person, the wounded path. And notice, too, that your own scars may not disqualify you the way you fear. The very places where you have been hurt and healed may be where you are most equipped to charge in for someone else. Let the marred Champion reframe how you read strength."
prayer: |
  Lord Jesus, I have spent my life looking for rescue that looks impressive.
  
  Thank You that You came scarred and despised, and that Your wounds are the reason You are worthy.
  
  Forgive me for despising the humble ways You often move.
  
  Take even my own scars and make them useful in Your hands for others.
  
  In Your name, Amen.
