reflection: |
  Picture yourself sitting beside someone who has run out of hope. Maybe it is a chair pulled up to a hospital bed, or a kitchen table late at night, a cup of tea going cold between you. Their eyes have gone flat and tired, the way eyes do when a person has stopped expecting anything good. You do not have advice that will fix it. All you really have is a name — Jesus — and the story of what He has done.
  
  So you speak. Haltingly, maybe. And as you talk about Him, something in the room begins to change. A little light comes back into their face. Hope, which had gone, quietly returns. And you realize you are watching something holy: a person crossing, right there in front of you, from death to life.
  
  We so easily miss that this is part of why we were saved. We treat rescue as the whole story — I'm safe now, that is settled, that is all I need to think about. But the word God keeps using for His people is "servants." And a servant is not someone who merely belongs to a household; a servant is someone who does something in it. We were saved from something, yes. We were also saved for something.
  
  The tragedy is how soon we stop. We take the rescue and never discover the deeper joy on the other side of it — the joy of being used, of sitting with the hopeless and watching life return to their eyes. To be saved and never to serve is to be handed the keys to a door and never once walk through it. We are His workmanship, and the workmanship was always meant to walk in the works He prepared.

lifeApplication: |
  Who is the person God has placed near you who has lost hope? Not a stranger on the other side of the world — someone close enough that you already know their tiredness: a neighbor, a coworker, a family member, a friend who has stopped expecting anything good.
  
  You do not need a title or special training to sit beside them and speak the name of Jesus. Being saved is not the finish line of your story; it is the beginning of your usefulness. This week, ask Him to show you one person, and then do the simplest thing — be present, and tell them what He has done. You may find the joy you have been missing was waiting on the other side of your obedience.
prayer: |
  Lord, thank You that You did not only rescue me — You made me Yours to use.
  
  Forgive me for the times I have been content merely to be saved, forgetting the joy of serving You.
  
  Open my eyes to the person near me who has lost hope.
  
  Give me the courage to speak Your name and the words to tell them what You have done.
  
  Let me know the joy of watching someone cross from death to life.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
