reflection: |
  Picture the mouth of a cave, and the crowd forcing its way in. A king is there, his crown useless now, shoved up against a slave he would never have looked at yesterday. A commander crouches beside a beggar. The richest man in the city claws at the same rock as the poorest. Every rank and title the world ever invented dissolves in the scramble for somewhere, anywhere, to hide. And they are begging the mountains — begging the rocks themselves — to fall on them and cover them over.
  
  Not long before this, some of these same people had felt perfectly secure. There had been a famine so sharp that a whole day's wage bought only a little grain, and yet the oil and the wine were left untouched — the comforts of the well-off still in place while the poor went hungry. It is easy, when your own cupboard is still full, to believe the trouble will never quite reach you. False security is a quiet thing. It lets you sleep.
  
  But here every cupboard is bare and every wall has fallen, and the one thing they all share is that there is nowhere left to run. Wealth cannot buy a way out. Power cannot command one. The highest and the lowest are buried together under the very same rocks.
  
  And here is the ache at the centre of it: the presence they are so desperate to hide from is the only presence that could ever have saved them. They are fleeing the one face that was never their enemy. There is a refuge in this scene — there always was — but it is not a cave. It is a Person. The very name they are running from is the strong tower they could have run into.

lifeApplication: |
  It is worth asking what you are quietly trusting to keep trouble at arm's length — the savings, the standing, the sense that people like you are somehow safe from what happens to others. These are the full cupboards that let us sleep while our hearts drift from the only true shelter.
  
  There is a refuge, and it is not a thing you can store up; it is a Person. This week, instead of running to the usual places when fear rises — the bank balance, the phone, the reassurance of your own plans — run to Him. Make His presence the first place you flee, not the last. The name so many spend their lives running from is the strong tower you were always meant to run into.
prayer: |
  Father, I confess how easily I trust in what I can store up rather than in You.
  
  Forgive me for the false security that lets me sleep while my heart drifts away.
  
  When fear rises, teach me to run to You first, and not to the things that cannot save.
  
  Thank You that Your name is a strong tower, and that the righteous who run into it are safe.
  
  Be my refuge, Lord, for there is no cave and no wealth that can shelter me but You.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
