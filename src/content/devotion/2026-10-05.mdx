reflection: |
  The invitation came in gold lettering on a field of black: a gala evening, elite and rare, the kind of night worth leaving work early for and worth the long crawl through traffic to reach. So the expectation sits high when the doors finally open — onto a bare room. Five tables, two of them dressed with cloths and three of them plain chipboard. A couple of Ziploc bags set out in the middle, labelled "snacks." A tray carried round with plastic cups of warm Coke, each one only half filled, a lucky one or two with ice. It is hot. It is sparse. It is nothing like the promise.
  
  You stand there doing the quiet arithmetic of disappointment — how early you could slip away, whether there is somewhere to eat on the road home. And then a man steps up, offers a few words of welcome, and adds almost offhandedly, "Right, enough messing around." A wall swings open. Behind it is the real room: gold on the table, glasses catching the light, balloons and candles and a feast laid out, the whole evening you had been promised after all. The letdown was never the whole story. It was only the part you could see from where you were standing.
  
  There is a man in Scripture who knew that exact descent and rescue, though everything about his was greater. John stands in the throne room of heaven and weeps — not polite tears but gut-deep grief — because a sealed scroll is held out and no one, not one soul in heaven or on earth, is found worthy to open it. He can see the ruin that unopened scroll means. He is standing in the bare room, and from where he stands there is nothing but loss. Then an elder touches him: weep no more. There is One who has conquered. A wall is about to open.
  
  So much of faith is lived in the bare room — the part of the story we can actually see, which so rarely matches the promise. The thing John is handed is not that his grief is foolish, but that it is not the final word. Behind the wall there is already a feast, and the One worthy to open every sealed thing has already won. We weep from where we stand. Heaven weeps no more, because Heaven can see the room we cannot.

lifeApplication: "Think of the waiting room you are sitting in right now — the result that has not come, the medical test still pending, the interview you walked out of unsure, the phone that has not rung. From where you sit, the room looks bare, and the quiet arithmetic of disappointment is already running. Weeping there is honest; God never shames the tears. But \"weep no more\" is His word over you today — not because the waiting is finished, but because the One who holds the sealed scroll of your life has already conquered. You are not waiting to find out whether there is a feast. You are only waiting for the wall to open."
prayer: |
  Lord, You see the bare room I am standing in, and You know the tears I have not spoken aloud.
  
  Thank You that my disappointment is never the whole story, because You hold what I cannot yet see.
  
  Forgive me for reading the ending by the little I can make out from here.
  
  Teach me to wait quietly for You, trusting that You have already conquered.
  
  Quiet my anxious arithmetic, and let me rest in the feast You have prepared behind the wall.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
