reflection: |
  Think of the places we go looking for peace. The glass poured at the end of a hard day. The prescription that takes the edge off. The achievement we were sure would finally let us rest. One more piece of someone's approval, one more sign that we measure up. Each one works for an evening, and then the restlessness seeps back in, and off we go looking again.
  
  Underneath all that chasing is a quieter, deeper hunger — the need to know that we are enough. Enough to be loved. Enough to be accepted. Enough, if we are truly honest, to one day stand before God and be let in. So much of our striving is really an attempt to prove ourselves worthy.
  
  There is an old picture of a throne room where a search goes out for someone worthy — worthy to set everything right, worthy to open the sealed scroll of history. And no one is found. Not one person in all of heaven and earth measures up. Until a Lamb steps forward, bearing the marks of having been slain, and all of heaven erupts in a single cry: "Worthy are You."
  
  There it is — the whole gospel pressed into a sentence. Not "worthy am I," but "worthy are You." Peace does not come on the day we finally become enough; that day never arrives, and the chase for it will exhaust us to the end. Peace comes the moment we stop, lay down the striving, and tell the truth: I am not worthy — but He is. If one day I am asked why I should be welcomed home, I will not reach for the list of reasons I am a good person. I will say only this: I am not worthy, but He is, and He is the One I am trusting. That is where peace actually lives — not in becoming enough, but in resting in the One who was enough for us.

lifeApplication: |
  Be honest with yourself about where you have been going for peace, and what you have been trying to prove. Name it — the achievement, the approval, the thing you reach for when the day is done. And then notice how long its peace actually lasts.
  
  Then try something different. Take the thing weighing on you — the worry, the guilt, the burden you cannot put down — and bring it to God in prayer, with thanksgiving, exactly as it is. You do not have to tidy yourself up first. You do not have to become worthy of being heard. When the old urge rises to justify yourself, to prove you are good enough, lay it down and say the truest words you know: "Lord, I am not worthy — but You are. I cannot carry this. Please carry it for me." Say it until your soul believes it. That is not the end of your striving for peace; it is the beginning of actually having it.
prayer: |
  Thank You that You are worthy — that when no one else could set things right or open the way home, You could, and You did.
  
  Forgive me for the exhausting chase to prove that I am enough; I lay it down before You.
  
  I confess the truest words I know: I am not worthy, but You are, and You are the One I trust.
  
  Take the burdens I cannot carry, and give me the peace that the world can neither give nor take away.
  
  In Jesus' name, Amen.
