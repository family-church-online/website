But I will gain glory for myself through Pharaoh and all his army, and the Egyptians will know that I am the Lord.” So the Israelites did this.


  **5** When the king of Egypt was told that the people had fled, Pharaoh and his officials changed their minds about them and said, “What have we done? We have let the Israelites go and have lost their services!” **6** So he had his chariot made ready and took his army with him. **7** He took six hundred of the best chariots, along with all the other chariots of Egypt, with officers over all of them. **8** The Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt, so that he pursued the Israelites, who were marching out boldly. **9** The Egyptians—all Pharaoh’s horses and chariots, horsemen[[a](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2014&version=NIV#fen-NIV-1899a \"See footnote a\")] and troops—pursued the Israelites and overtook them as they camped by the sea near Pi Hahiroth, opposite Baal Zephon.


  **10** As Pharaoh approached, the Israelites looked up, and there were the Egyptians, marching after them. They were terrified and cried out to the Lord. **11** They said to Moses, “Was it because there were no graves in Egypt that you brought us to the desert to die? What have you done to us by bringing us out of Egypt? **12** Didn’t we say to you in Egypt, ‘Leave us alone; let us serve the Egyptians’? It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the desert!”


  **13** Moses answered the people, “Do not be afraid. Stand firm and you will see the deliverance the Lord will bring you today. The Egyptians you see today you will never see again. **14** The Lord will fight for you; you need only to be still.”


  **15** Then the Lord said to Moses, “Why are you crying out to me? Tell the Israelites to move on. **16** Raise your staff and stretch out your hand over the sea to divide the water so that the Israelites can go through the sea on dry ground. **17** I will harden the hearts of the Egyptians so that they will go in after them. And I will gain glory through Pharaoh and all his army, through his chariots and his horsemen. **18** The Egyptians will know that I am the Lord when I gain glory through Pharaoh, his chariots and his horsemen.”


  **19** Then the angel of God, who had been traveling in front of Israel’s army, withdrew and went behind them. The pillar of cloud also moved from in front and stood behind them, **20** coming between the armies of Egypt and Israel. Throughout the night the cloud brought darkness to the one side and light to the other side; so neither went near the other all night long.


  **21** Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and all that night the Lord drove the sea back with a strong east wind and turned it into dry land. The waters were divided, **22** and the Israelites went through the sea on dry ground, with a wall of water on their right and on their left.


  **23** The Egyptians pursued them, and all Pharaoh’s horses and chariots and horsemen followed them into the sea. **24** During the last watch of the night the Lord looked down from the pillar of fire and cloud at the Egyptian army and threw it into confusion. **25** He jammed[[b](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2014&version=NIV#fen-NIV-1915b \"See footnote b\")] the wheels of their chariots so that they had difficulty driving. And the Egyptians said, “Let’s get away from the Israelites! The Lord is fighting for them against Egypt.”


  **26** Then the Lord said to Moses, “Stretch out your hand over the sea so that the waters may flow back over the Egyptians and their chariots and horsemen.” **27** Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and at daybreak the sea went back to its place. The Egyptians were fleeing toward[[c](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2014&version=NIV#fen-NIV-1917c \"See footnote c\")] it, and the Lord swept them into the sea. **28** The water flowed back and covered the chariots and horsemen—the entire army of Pharaoh that had followed the Israelites into the sea. Not one of them survived.


  **29** But the Israelites went through the sea on dry ground, with a wall of water on their right and on their left. **30** That day the Lord saved Israel from the hands of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians lying dead on the shore. **31** And when the Israelites saw the mighty hand of the Lord displayed against the Egyptians, the people feared the Lord and put their trust in him and in Moses his servant."

summary: "Israel had been brought out of slavery in Egypt, but their journey was far from over. As Pharaoh's army pursued them, the Israelites found themselves trapped between the Egyptian army and the Red Sea, and their fear quickly turned into panic. God had already promised to bring them out of Egypt, yet they struggled to trust Him when obedience led them into a situation they could not solve themselves. God commanded Moses to stretch out his hand over the sea, and He made a way for Israel to cross on dry ground. Exodus 14 shows that God's faithfulness does not depend on circumstances being easy, and that trusting Him sometimes means moving forward before we can see how everything will work out."
about: "Exodus 14 teaches us what faith looks like when the situation in front of us seems impossible. Israel had followed God's direction, but following God did not prevent them from facing fear, danger, and uncertainty. When they saw Pharaoh's army approaching, they focused on what they could see instead of remembering what God had already done. God did not simply tell them to sit down and wait, because after telling Moses to stretch out his hand over the sea, He commanded the Israelites to move forward through the path He provided. For teenagers, this chapter speaks directly to moments when the future feels uncertain, when everyone else seems ahead, or when doing the right thing does not immediately make life easier."

Your future does not have to be completely mapped out before you can take the next faithful step."

Comparison can make you interpret another person's progress as evidence that God is doing less in your life. Exodus 14 reminds us that God's people do not all travel the same route or arrive at the same places at the same time. Your responsibility is not to copy someone else's journey, but to follow God faithfully in your own."
  - "You are facing a situation you genuinely cannot control.There are circumstances in life that you cannot fix through planning, effort, or positive thinking. You may be waiting for an answer, dealing with family difficulties, facing a friendship problem, or carrying a fear about something you cannot change. Exodus 14 does not teach that Christians should pretend these things are not painful or frightening. Instead, it teaches us to bring our fear to God while continuing to trust and obey Him. You can acknowledge that something is difficult without concluding that God has stopped being faithful."
  - "You know what God says, but obedience is difficult.Sometimes the problem is not that we do not know what God wants, but that we do not like what obedience requires. You may know that you need to walk away from a harmful relationship, stop participating in something you know is wrong, apologise to someone, forgive someone, or change a pattern that has become normal. God's Word does not become less true because obedience is uncomfortable. Exodus 14 reminds us that God's people are called to respond to His direction even when the path ahead is unfamiliar. Following God requires more than agreeing with Him intellectually, because trust eventually has to become action."
keyScripture: "Exodus 14:14 “The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.”


  Moses spoke these words when the Israelites were terrified because Pharaoh's army was approaching. They were looking at the enemy behind them and the sea in front of them, but God was already preparing their deliverance. Moses' instruction was not an invitation to laziness, because Israel would still have to follow God's instructions and eventually walk through the sea. The point was that they did not have to respond to fear as though everything depended entirely on their own strength.


  There are moments when you genuinely cannot control the outcome of a situation. You cannot control what another person decides, how quickly an opportunity arrives, what someone thinks about you, or how another person behaves. What you can control is whether you respond with fear, wisdom, obedience, prayer, and trust in God.


  “Be still” does not always mean doing nothing. Sometimes it means becoming still enough internally to hear God's instruction instead of allowing panic to control your decisions."
bigIdea: "God can lead you through a situation you cannot solve yourself, so trust Him with the part of the journey you cannot yet see.


  Israel could not create a path through the Red Sea, and they could not defeat Pharaoh's army by their own strength. God did what they could not do, but He still expected them to respond when He gave them a way forward. This means trusting God is not the same as sitting back and expecting Him to do everything for us. We obey what He has revealed, depend on Him for what is beyond our control, and trust Him with what we cannot yet see. Faith is not knowing the entire route, but trusting the God who does."
mainPoints:
  - heading: "1. Fear can make you forget what God has already done"
    text: "The Israelites had just witnessed God's power in Egypt, yet they quickly became terrified when they saw Pharaoh's army. Their circumstances made them forget their previous experience of God's faithfulness. Fear often narrows our attention until the current problem becomes the only thing we can see. When that happens, remembering what God has already done can help us regain perspective.


      Ask yourself, “What has God already brought me through?”"
  - heading: "2. Following God does not mean everything will immediately become easy"
    text: "Israel was exactly where God had instructed them to go, yet they still ended up facing an enormous obstacle. This challenges the idea that every difficult circumstance means you are outside God's will. Sometimes obedience takes you directly into situations where your dependence on God becomes deeper. Difficulty should therefore not automatically be interpreted as evidence that God has abandoned you.


      Ask yourself, “Am I assuming that difficulty means I made the wrong decision?”"
  - heading: "3. God sees possibilities that we cannot see"
    text: "The Israelites saw water, but God saw a pathway. They saw an army, but God saw an opportunity to demonstrate His power. We often judge our future according to what is visible in the present, but God is not limited by the information we currently have. His perspective is greater than ours.


      Ask yourself, “Am I limiting God to the options I can currently see?”"
  - heading: "4. Faith still requires movement"
    text: "The Israelites did not simply stand beside the Red Sea forever. Once God opened the way, they had to walk through it. Trusting God does not mean refusing to act, because there are moments when faith requires obedience, courage, discipline, or a difficult decision.


      Ask yourself, “What is the next step God is asking me to take?”"
  - heading: "5. Your fear does not have to determine your future"
    text: "Fear is a real emotion, but it does not have to become your decision-maker. The Israelites were afraid, yet God still gave them a way forward. Courage is not the absence of fear, but choosing obedience and trust even when fear is present.


      Ask yourself, “If fear was not controlling me, what would obedience look like?”"
keyIllustration: "Imagine that you are walking through an unfamiliar building during a power outage. You cannot see very far ahead, and every few steps make you wonder whether you are going in the right direction. Then someone who knows the building tells you to follow their voice because they already know where the exit is.\ 


  You may still feel nervous because you cannot see the whole route, but you have a reason to keep moving because the person leading you can see what you cannot.\ 


  Exodus 14 gives us a similar picture of trusting God when our perspective is limited, because Israel could not see the path before God opened it, but God already knew exactly where He was taking them."
meansForUs:
  - "Remember specific ways God has been faithful. Do not rely only on whether life feels good right now, because circumstances and emotions change. Write down prayers God has answered, situations He has carried you through, lessons He has taught you, and moments where His provision became clear. When fear tells you that God has never helped you, those reminders can help you remember the truth. Remembering God's faithfulness does not guarantee an easy future, but it gives you a biblical reason to trust Him."
  - "Separate what God has clearly said from what you simply prefer. Sometimes we call something “God's will” when what we really mean is that it is the outcome we want. Scripture gives us clear commands about how Christians should live, while many personal decisions require wisdom, prayer, counsel, and patience. Do not use phrases like “God told me” to justify something that Scripture does not support or to avoid wise thinking. Trusting God begins with obeying what He has already revealed and allowing Him to guide you in what remains uncertain."
  - "Stop treating someone else's progress as evidence that you have been forgotten. Israel had a route that looked different from what they might have expected, and their journey involved difficulty before they reached what God had promised. Your friend may have opportunities you do not have, and someone your age may appear much further ahead in life. Their progress does not automatically mean that you are failing or that God is doing less in your life. Focus on faithful obedience rather than constantly measuring your life against someone else's timeline."
