  5 The deep waters have covered them;

  6 Your right hand, Lord,

  7 “In the greatness of your majesty

  you threw down those who opposed you.

  You unleashed your burning anger;

  it consumed them like stubble.

  8 By the blast of your nostrils

  the waters piled up.

  The surging waters stood up like a wall;

  the deep waters congealed in the heart of the sea.

  9 The enemy boasted,

  ‘I will pursue, I will overtake them.

  I will divide the spoils;

  I will gorge myself on them.

  in the mighty waters.

  11 Who among the gods

  is like you, Lord?

  Who is like you

  majestic in holiness,

  awesome in glory,

  working wonders?

  and the earth swallows your enemies.

  13 In your unfailing love you will lead

  the people you have redeemed.

  until the people you bought pass by.

  17 You will bring them in and plant them

  on the mountain of your inheritance

  the place, Lord, you made for your dwelling,

  the sanctuary, Lord, your hands established.


  18 “The Lord reigns

  for ever and ever.”

  “Sing to the Lord,

  for he is highly exalted.

  The Waters of Marah and Elim


  22 Then Moses led Israel from the Red Sea and they went into the Desert of Shur. For three days they traveled in the desert without finding water. 23 When they came to Marah, they could not drink its water because it was bitter. (That is why the place is called Marah) 24 So the people grumbled against Moses, saying, “What are we to drink?”


  25 Then Moses cried out to the Lord, and the Lord showed him a piece of wood. He threw it into the water, and the water became fit to drink.


  There the Lord issued a ruling and instruction for them and put them to the test. 26 He said, “If you listen carefully to the Lord your God and do what is right in his eyes, if you pay attention to his commands and keep all his decrees, I will not bring on you any of the diseases I brought on the Egyptians, for I am the Lord, who heals you.”


  27 Then they came to Elim, where there were twelve springs and seventy palm trees, and they camped there near the water."

summary: "Exodus 15 begins with Israel celebrating because God has delivered them from Pharaoh and brought them safely through the Red Sea. Moses leads the people in a song that gives God credit for their salvation, power, and victory rather than celebrating Israel's own strength. Miriam and the women also respond with worship, showing that the whole community is called to remember and declare what God has done.\ 


  But the chapter does not end with celebration, because only three days later the Israelites find themselves without water in the wilderness. Exodus 15 shows us that faith has to survive beyond the emotional high of a miracle, because the God we worship in the breakthrough is the same God we must trust when the next problem appears."
about: "Exodus 15 teaches us what should happen after God brings us through something difficult, and what happens when the excitement wears off. Israel had just witnessed one of the greatest acts of deliverance in the Old Testament, yet three days later they were complaining because they could not find drinkable water. Their response shows how quickly gratitude can disappear when a new need becomes uncomfortable. God provided for them by making the bitter water at Marah fit to drink, and He used the moment to teach them about trusting His instructions and listening to His voice.\ 


  For us, this chapter asks an important question: Do you only trust God when you can clearly see what He has done, or do you trust Him when the next problem has not been solved yet?"
takeaway:
  - "Worship should remind you of who God is, not just what He has given you.Israel's song in Exodus 15 focuses repeatedly on God Himself, including His strength, salvation, holiness, power, and faithfulness. It would have been easy for the Israelites to celebrate simply because they had escaped Pharaoh, but their worship recognised that God was the One who had delivered them. Worship is therefore more than singing because life is going well or because you received something you wanted. It is a response to God's character and His worth, even when your circumstances change. When your feelings change, remembering who God is gives your faith something more stable than your mood."
  - "A spiritual high does not mean there will be no difficult days afterwards.Israel went from singing beside the Red Sea to complaining in the wilderness within a very short period of time. That does not mean the Red Sea miracle was fake or that their worship was meaningless. It shows that experiencing God's power does not remove the need to keep trusting Him. Teenagers can experience something similar after a powerful church service, youth camp, answered prayer, or meaningful moment with God, only to feel discouraged again when normal life returns. The goal is not to live permanently on an emotional high, but to develop a faith that continues when the excitement is gone."
  - "God cares about what happens in the ordinary moments too.The problem at Marah was not dramatic like the Red Sea, but the people still needed water. God was not only interested in Israel when Pharaoh was chasing them, because He also cared about their needs in the wilderness. Sometimes we expect God to work through huge, obvious moments while overlooking the ordinary places where He is teaching us dependence and obedience. Your everyday choices, friendships, schoolwork, attitudes, habits, and private decisions are also part of your relationship with God. Following Him is not only about what happens at church, because discipleship continues when nobody is watching."
  - "Trust is tested when the next problem arrives.Israel had already seen God deliver them from Egypt, but their response at Marah showed that yesterday's miracle did not automatically produce today's trust. When the water was bitter, they immediately complained to Moses instead of remembering the God who had just opened the sea. This is one of the uncomfortable parts of the chapter, because it shows how easily we can praise God when things work out and question Him when they do not. Faith grows when we learn to bring our needs to God without allowing frustration to rewrite everything we know about Him. The next challenge does not erase the previous evidence of God's faithfulness."

keyScripture: "Exodus 15:26


  “I am the LORD who heals you.”


  God reveals Himself as YHWH-Rapha, the LORD who heals. This statement comes in the context of God teaching Israel to listen to His voice and walk in His ways. It shows that God is not only concerned with rescuing His people from danger, but also with forming them into a people who know and obey Him.\ 


  The verse points to God's character as the source of healing and wholeness, while the wider Bible shows that trusting God does not mean we will never experience sickness or suffering."
bigIdea: "The same God who deserves your praise in the breakthrough is still worthy of your trust in the wilderness.


  Israel's song came immediately after an extraordinary victory, but the wilderness exposed whether their trust could survive ordinary difficulty. God did not stop being faithful simply because the Israelites became thirsty. Exodus 15 teaches us to remember God's character beyond the emotional moments and to keep following Him when circumstances become frustrating again.\ 


  The goal of faith is not to stay permanently on a spiritual high, but to become people who know God well enough to trust Him in both celebration and difficulty. Your faith cannot depend entirely on how your life feels today."
mainPoints:
  - heading: "1. REMEMBER WHAT GOD HAS DONE"
    text: "Israel's song in Exodus 15 remembers God's deliverance at the Red Sea. They praise His power, faithfulness, and holiness. Remembering God's past faithfulness helps us trust Him when new problems arise. Today's difficulty does not erase what God has already done. A healthy faith remembers."
  - heading: "2. DO NOT BUILD YOUR FAITH ONLY ON EMOTIONAL HIGHS"
    text: "Israel celebrated God's victory, but three days later they were struggling with fear and frustration. This shows that experiencing God's power does not remove the need for daily faith. Our relationship with God cannot depend only on how we feel during worship, church, or youth events. Feelings change, but God's character does not. Faith is grounded in God, not in our emotions."
  - heading: "3. BRING YOUR NEEDS TO GOD"
    text: "The Israelites genuinely needed water, and God provided for them at Marah. Having a real problem is not wrong, but allowing frustration to control our response can lead us away from trust. We can bring our fears, disappointments, and needs honestly to God. We do not have to pretend everything is fine to have faith. Bring the problem to God without forgetting who He is."
  - heading: "4. GOD CAN USE DIFFICULT MOMENTS TO TEACH US"
    text: "The wilderness became a place where Israel had to learn dependence and obedience. God used their circumstances to teach them to listen to His voice and trust His direction. Difficulty does not automatically mean that God is punishing you or has abandoned you. Sometimes difficult seasons reveal areas where our faith needs to grow. God can use the wilderness to shape us."
  - heading: "5. GOD IS THE HEALER, BUT LIFE WILL STILL INVOLVE SUFFERING"
    text: "Exodus 15:26 reveals God as “the LORD who heals you.” This tells us something true about God's character, but it does not promise that Christians will never experience sickness or suffering. We can pray confidently for healing while trusting God with the outcome. God remains faithful whether healing comes immediately, through treatment, over time, or not in the way we expected. We trust the Healer, not a guaranteed outcome."

The previous result did not become untrue simply because the next test was difficult, and in the same way, a difficult season does not erase the ways God has already been faithful to you.\ 


  Exodus 15 challenges us to build a faith that remembers yesterday's faithfulness while facing today's uncertainty."
